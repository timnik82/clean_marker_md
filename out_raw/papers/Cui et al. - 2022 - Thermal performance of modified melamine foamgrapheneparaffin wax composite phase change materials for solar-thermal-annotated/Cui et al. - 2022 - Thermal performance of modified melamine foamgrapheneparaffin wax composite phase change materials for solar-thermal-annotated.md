## ABSTRACT

The development of phase change materials (PCMs) is hampered by issues like leakage, poor thermal conductivity, and poor light absorption. In this study, we innovatively combined modified melamine foam (MF) and graphene nanoparticle (GNP) to address these defects of PCMs in the solar-thermal energy system. The MF was modified to endow the solar-thermal energy conversion ability and superhydrophobic interface characteristics due to the polypyrrole (PPy) by the in-situ polymerization and the polymerized octadecylsiloxane (PODS) layer coating on the sponge skeleton. The composite PCMs consisting of the modified MF, paraffin wax (PW), and GNP were fabricated and exhibited good leakproof ability, high thermal conductivity, and excellent solar-thermal energy conversion capability. Results showed that due to the effects of the PPy and the PODS on the sponge skeleton structure, MF@PPy-PODS/PW composite PCMs showed a decrease in the mass loss ratio to less than 0.34% and an increase in the thermal conductivity of 0.33 W/m⋅K and the solar-thermal storage efficiency of 75.68%. Besides, adding GNP with higher concentrations impacted positively on the thermal conductivity and the solar-thermal energy conversion ability but negatively on the phase change enthalpies. MF@PPy-PODS/ GNP3/PW composite PCMs increased the thermal conductivity to 0.59 W/m⋅K and the solar-thermal storage efficiency to 79.36% while decreasing the phase change enthalpy to 130.61 J/g. Furthermore, a thermoelectric conversion system driven by solar energy was developed to show the potential of composite PCMs for cleaner energy production. The open-circuit voltages of the MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs achieved 0.78 and 0.91 V at 2 and 4 Sun, respectively. The open-circuit voltages remained for a period of time and slowly dropped without the simulated solar light irradiation. This study provided a potential strategy for the optimal performance of composite PCMs and their application in solar thermal systems.

## **1. Introduction**

As the issue of reducing carbon emissions has become increasingly prominent, it is urgent to develop and use clean and sustainable renewable energy to replace fossil fuels in the future (Chu & [Majumdar,](#page-10-0)  [2012\)](#page-10-0). As of now, solar energy is the most common and abundant source of renewable energy ([Mathioulakis et al., 2017](#page-10-0)). Among the several forms to use solar energy, the solar-thermal energy conversion and storage is a typical renewable energy utilization technique, which has been employed in a wide range of applications in green buildings ([Chaiyat, 2015\)](#page-10-0), waste heat recovery system [\(Sharif et al., 2014](#page-10-0)), etc.

However, the instability of solar energy affects its application due to its low radiation energy density and intermittent nature [\(Zhang et al.,](#page-10-0)  [2016\)](#page-10-0). Therefore, to obtain long-term solar energy utilization, designing a proper thermal energy storage system is essential for storing and supplying dispatchable thermal energy and improving the stability and conversion efficiency of the solar energy utilization system ([Alva et al.,](#page-10-0)  [2017\)](#page-10-0).

Latent heat thermal energy storage system depends on the melting and solidification process of phase change materials (PCMs) to store and release large thermal energy, allowing for the inter-regional and intertemporal use of thermal energy ([Kenisarin and Mahkamov, 2007](#page-10-0)). By

combining the latent heat thermal energy storage system with the solar-thermal energy conversion technique, it is possible to effectively address the issue of solar instability [\(Alva et al., 2017](#page-10-0)). However, factors such as leakage, low thermal conductivity, and poor light absorption are fatal drawbacks of PCMs to hamper their application in the solar-thermal energy conversion and storage system [\(Tao et al., 2020](#page-10-0)). Therefore, the advancement of composite PCMs with good leakproof ability, high heat transfer performance, and efficient solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capability has received increasing attention.

To solve the leakage problem of liquid PCMs, the traditional method is to use physical encapsulation by loading PCMs into a container, while considering increasing the heat transfer surface-area-to-volume ratio, this method leads to a complex system design ([Xu et al., 2022\)](#page-10-0). Thanks to the rapid advancement of micro/nano-technology, more emphasis has been paid to encapsulating PCMs in micro/nano scale spaces to prepare stable-shaped PCMs [\(Cheng et al., 2021\)](#page-10-0). The common encapsulation techniques mainly include two kinds: one is to form stable core-shell structures, such as micro/nano-encapsulation ([Su et al.,](#page-10-0)  [2015\)](#page-10-0), electrostatic spinning [\(Luo et al., 2013](#page-10-0)), etc.; the other one includes using the surface tension and capillary force of porous materials to physically attach PCMs, such as expanded graphite-based composite PCMs [\(Wang et al., 2020\)](#page-10-0). The former encapsulation technology has a complicated preparation process and high cost and remains challenging in practical large-scale applications ([Aftab et al., 2018](#page-10-0)). In contrast, the latter encapsulation technology has a simple preparation process and low cost and has broad application prospects in solving the leakage problem of liquid PCMs. However, the good compatibility of porous materials and PCMs should be guaranteed to ensure the leakproof capabilities of the encapsulation approach indicated above for physisorption of PCMs by porous materials [\(Mao et al., 2021](#page-10-0)). In particular, the wettability of the porous skeleton surface is the key to the penetration of PCMs into it. For example, the lipophilic reduced graphene oxide (rGO) is used to modify the skeleton of porous materials to reduce the leakage of the paraffin wax (PW) [\(Cheng et al., 2020\)](#page-10-0). Besides, the rich interconnected structure of porous materials provides heat transfer channels in PCMs and accelerates the phase change process. This enhancement of composite PCMs helps to improve the heat transfer performance ([Lin et al., 2020](#page-10-0)). Moreover, it is worth noting that the light absorption capacity of porous materials determines the solar-thermal energy conversion efficiency of the composite PCMs, due to the weak light absorption capacity of PCMs ([Shao et al., 2021\)](#page-10-0). Many researchers are devoted to improving the light absorption capacity of porous materials, and results are confirmed that coating various solar-thermal energy conversion enhancement fillers on the porous skeleton surface, including carbon nanotubes ([Du et al., 2021\)](#page-10-0), rGO ([Yang et al., 2019](#page-10-0)), polypyrrole (PPy) ([Zhou et al., 2013](#page-10-0)), etc., are beneficial to improve the solar-thermal energy conversion ability of PCMs. Therefore, excellent skeleton properties of porous materials facilitate meeting the requirements for good leakproof ability, high thermal conductivity, and high solar-thermal energy conversion efficiency of composite PCMs.

Aiming to remedy the lack of PCMs for solar-thermal storage utilization, adding nanoparticles is also an attractive strategy. It is demonstrated that the addition of nanoparticles to PCMs can increase the kinematic viscosity, thereby weakening the mobility of the liquid PCMs ([Nikkhah et al., 2015](#page-10-0)), and improving the effective thermal conductivity of PCMs [\(Sajid and Ali, 2018\)](#page-10-0). Furthermore, nanoparticles dispersed in PCMs exhibit significant light absorption enhancement performance ([Yang et al., 2021](#page-10-0)), and thus PCMs achieve efficient solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capability, which is derived from the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) effect of nanoparticles ([Dong et al., 2022](#page-10-0)). Common nanoparticles with the LSPR effect include Au [\(Chen et al., 2017\)](#page-10-0), Ag ([Zhu et al., 2019\)](#page-10-0), carbon nanotubes [\(Tang](#page-10-0)  [et al., 2014](#page-10-0)), BN [\(Yang et al., 2016\)](#page-10-0), TiN ([Ren and Yang, 2018\)](#page-10-0), ZrC ([Yang et al., 2019\)](#page-10-0), etc.

Comprehensively, it is a promising choice to composite with porous

materials to overcome the drawbacks of PCMs. Besides, after the optimization by the nanoparticles, the leakproof ability, heat transfer performance, and solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capability of composite PCMs also achieve the desired improvement [\(Yang et al.,](#page-10-0)  [2021\)](#page-10-0). However, previous work has focused more on the study of porous materials properties by the modification and optimization, and few studies improve the encapsulating and thermal properties of PCMs with porous materials and nanoparticles simultaneously for the application of the solar-thermal conversion and energy storage. Because the pore size of porous materials is in micro-nano scales, the addition of nanoparticles can supplement the thermal properties of PCMs in the micro-nano pores ([Lin et al., 2020](#page-10-0)). Relying on the enhancement of porous materials and nanoparticles, combining these two strategies has the potential for further improving the solar-thermal energy conversion ability of composite PCMs.

To this end, this study innovatively combined modified porous materials and nanoparticles to address the defects of PCMs for solarthermal energy conversion and storage. Firstly, melamine foam (MF) with the advantages of a three-dimensional continuous porous structure, high porosity, and good mechanical characteristics was selected as the porous material. The PPy by the in-situ polymerization and the polymerized octadecylsiloxane (PODS) layer coating on the sponge skeleton of MF were used to endow the MF with solar-thermal energy conversion capability and superhydrophobic interface characteristics ([Zhou et al.,](#page-10-0)  [2013\)](#page-10-0). Then, the PW with isothermal phase change property, high latent heat, good thermal stability, and low price was selected as PCMs ([Cui](#page-10-0)  [et al., 2022\)](#page-10-0). Besides, graphene nanoparticle (GNP) was selected to further improve the thermal performance of PCMs. Finally, composite PCMs consisting of the modified MF, PW, and GNP were prepared by the vacuum impregnation method ([Milian and Ushak, 2019\)](#page-10-0). The microstructures and thermophysical properties of the obtained composite PCMs were characterized. The addition of modified MF and GNP significantly improved the leakproof ability, thermal conductivity, and light absorption capability of composite PCMs. The solar-thermal storage efficiency of composite PCMs was investigated within a simulated light testing system. Based on the improved thermophysical properties and solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capability of composite PCMs, a thermoelectric conversion system with composite PCMs driven by solar energy was developed to achieve a solar to thermal to electrical energy utilization pathway for clean energy production. This study provided a novel strategy for improving the thermal performance of composite PCMs and their application in the solar-thermal conversion and storage system.

# *2.1. Experimental materials*

Commercial melamine foam (MF) was purchased from Beiyou New Material Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Pyrrole (Py) monomer, polyethylene glycol 600 (PEG 600), Octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS), and GNP were obtained from Aladdin Industrial Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). PW, anhydrous ethanol, ferric chloride hexahydrate (FeCl3⋅6H2O), and methylbenzene were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Regent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

#### *2.2. Modified process of MF*

The modified process of MF consists of two steps, as shown in [Fig. 1](#page-2-0). Firstly, MF modified by PPy was prepared based on the in-situ polymerization method [\(Sun et al., 2019](#page-10-0)). In detail, some cubic MF with the dimension of 20 × 20 × 20 mm3 were soaked in the anhydrous ethanol and deionized water for a complete ultrasonically cleaning. After that, the cleaned MF was immersed into the based solution consisting of PEG 600, Py monomer, and deionized water, and then FeCl3 aqueous solution was dropped slowly into the above mixture. The temperature for the

<span id="page-2-0"></span>**Fig. 1.** (a) Schematic illustration for the preparation process of modified MF and composite PCMs. (b) In-situ polymerization reaction of PPy. (c) Dipping-coating by PODS.

in-situ polymerization reaction was set at 5 ◦C. To ensure sufficient Py monomers were provided during the reaction, the molar ratio of FeCl3 aqueous solution and Py monomer was selected as 3:2, and the time of polymerization reaction was set as 5 h. In the chemical oxidation polymerization reaction, Fe3<sup>+</sup> and PEG 600 were used as the oxidant and stabilizer to polymerize PPy from the Py monomer in an aqueous phase ([Li, M. et al., 2014](#page-10-0); [Muller et al., 2013](#page-10-0)). As shown in Fig. 1(b), it was crucial in the polymerization reaction that the Py monomer was oxidized by Fe3<sup>+</sup> to create a Py radical cation. As two Py radical cations dimerized, a bond that linked the two Py rings was formed by releasing H+. Afterward, the two Py rings were oxidized again and joined with Py radical cation intermediates to create tripolymers. Long-chain PPy molecules were created similarly by a continuous polymerization process [\(Li, X. et al., 2014\)](#page-10-0). Subsequently, the obtained PPy was hydrogen-bonded to PEG, resulting in a PPy-PEG cluster with a complex cross-linked network structure caused by electrostatic interactions between the PPy and PEG molecules. Following that, plenty of cross-linking PPy-PEG clusters were firmly trapped on the sponge skeleton of MF through hydrogen bonding or electrostatic contact to change the MF from white to dark color. After the polymerization reaction, the samples were rinsed with anhydrous ethanol and deionized water to remove unreacted residues until the rinse solution turned colorless. Then the samples were dried in an oven at 100 ◦C again, and the prepared products were obtained, labeled as MF@PPy.

Secondly, the superhydrophobic modification of the MF@PPy was created by self-assembly of OTS to obtain the polymerized octadecylsiloxane (PODS) layer coating on the sponge skeleton ([Zhou et al., 2013](#page-10-0)). 1 mL of OTS was dripped into 50 mL of toluene, and after magnetic stirring the mixture for 0.5 h, an OTS-toluene solution was obtained. MF@PPy was put into the above mixture solution and kept at room temperature for 1 h. Fig. 1(c) displayed the process of hydrophobic coating on the MF@PPy was formed in two steps, hydrolysis and condensation [\(Wang and Lieberman, 2003](#page-10-0)). To begin, the hydroxyl groups of the OTS were generated in situ by hydrolysis of the precursor. Then the generated hydroxyl groups proceeded to a reaction with the hydroxyl groups of the PEG and linked with the sponge skeleton of MF. The OTS hydrolyzed molecules responded synchronously by self-assembly cross-linking via Si–O–Si condensation. Consequently, the hydrophobic long-chain coating of PODS was immobilized on the sponge skeleton of MF. Following the modification, the samples were washed and dried again to get dark superhydrophobic products, termed

as MF@PPy-PODS.

## *2.3. Preparation process of composite PCMs*

The preparation process of composite PCMs is shown in Fig. 1(a). In this study, PW was selected as the basic PCM. A certain amount of PW was put into a water bath at 80 ◦C and heated until fully melted. Different masses of GNP, as nanoparticle filler, were added to the molten PW for magnetic stirring and ultrasonic dispersion to prepare nanoparticle-phase change materials (nano-PCMs). Then the unmodified MF and modified MFs, including MF@PPy and MF@PPy-PODS, were dipped entirely into the above suspensions and impregnated for 1h at 80 ◦C in a vacuum environment to remove air bubbles from the sponge skeleton. Once the vacuum impregnation process was complete, samples were taken out of the suspensions and cooled to room temperature. After that, samples were put into the oven to remove the residual PW from the surface of the composites, and the fabricated composite PCMs were obtained, termed as MF/PW, MF@PPy/PW, MF@PPy-PODS/PW, and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP/PW.

In this study, the components of the composite PCMs mainly include nano-PCMs and the sponge skeleton of MF, and the GNP concentrations are set as 0, 0.01, 0.05, and 0.1 wt%. According to the mass difference of samples before and after the vacuum impregnation process, mass ratios of nano-PCMs (*W*nano-PCMs) and the sponge skeleton of MF (*W*MF) in the composite PCMs are calculated and listed in Table 1.

The absorption peak at 1017 cm<sup>−</sup> 1 corresponds to the C–H bond in the plane vibration of PPy. Comparing the spectra of MF and MF@PPy, it can be confirmed that PPy has been successfully prepared and attached to the sponge skeleton of MF. To further characterize the structure properties, Fig. 3(b) shows the XRD patterns of PW, MF/PW, MF@PPy/PW, MF@PPy-PODS/PW, and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs. It can be observed that two diffraction peaks appear at 21.5◦ and 23.8◦, which correspond to the (1 1 0) and (2 0 0) crystal planes of PW, respectively [\(Wu et al., 2020](#page-10-0)). Meanwhile, the composite PCMs show the diffraction peak positions consistent with PW, with no significant shift in peak position. The results still indicate that the crystal structure of PW and composite PCMs well intact without any chemical interaction during PW, GNP, and MF or modified MF. However, compared to PW, the peak values of the composite PCMs show a slight decrease due to the reduction of PW mass ratio in the composite PCMs caused by the addition of GNP and MF or modified MF [\(Shao et al., 2021\)](#page-10-0).

In contrast, the residual weight of PW in the MF@PPy-PODS/PW and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP/PW has hardly changed. Besides, [Fig. 5](#page-5-0)(c) shows the mass loss of the composite PCMs at 100 ◦C at the heating time of 20 min. The mass loss rates of PW in MF/PW, MF@PPy/PW, MF@PPy-PODS/PW and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP1, 2, 3/PW are 15.50%, 8.60%, 0.34%, 0.25%, 0.10%, and 0.08%, respectively. The explanation for this phenomenon is concluded to the following three reasons: the first is that the PPy attached to the MF reduces the interfacial energy between PW and the sponge skeleton and enhances the capillary effect of the sponge skeleton ([Khosravi and Azizian, 2015](#page-10-0)); the second is that the PODS layer coating on the sponge skeleton is hydrophobic and facilitates improved compatibility between PW and modified MF, allowing PW to be more easily impregnated into the sponge skeleton ([Zhang et al., 2017](#page-10-0)); and the third is that the addition of GNP weakens the fluidity of melted PW to a certain extent ([Singh et al., 2020](#page-10-0)).

## *3.3. Thermophysical properties of composite PCMs*

Phase change temperature and latent heat are among the most vital parameters for determining the thermal energy storage properties of PCMs, which can be analyzed by DSC measurement. Fig. 6 displays the phase change behaviors of the PW, MF/PW, MF@PPy/PW, MF@PPy-PODS/PW, and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP1, 2, 3/PW composite PCMs. As shown in Fig. 6(a) and (b), the upward and downward curves represent the endotherm and the exotherm effects, respectively. It can be noticed that the DSC curve of PW shows two phase change peaks, where the first peak represents the solid-solid phase change process with a temperature of approximately 33 ◦C; the second peak represents the solid-liquid phase change process with a temperature of approximately 53 ◦C. Furthermore, the DSC curves of composite PCMs are pretty similar to those of PW, especially phase change temperature. As illustrated in

Fig. 6(c), the addition of GNP and MF or modified MF shows a slight impact on the phase change temperature of composite PCMs, while it has a negative effect on the phase change enthalpies of composite PCMs. Taking the melting enthalpy as an example, compared with PW (152.47 J/g), the melting enthalpies of MF/PW (141.89 J/g), MF@PPy/PW (139.56 J/g), MF@PPy-PODS/PW (138.82 J/g) are relatively lower. It is apparent that the above three composite PCMs exhibit a favorable latent heat storage density due to the significant effect of PW. The reduction of melting enthalpy is attributed to the sponge skeleton occupying part of the space for PCMs. Besides, the melting enthalpies of MF@PPy-PODS/ GNP1, 2, 3/PW composite PCMs (134.6, 132.47, and 130.61 J/g) further drop. It can be explained that the addition of GNP in the composite PCMs results in a decrease in the mass fraction of PW. Therefore, adding GNP with the appropriate mass fraction is essential to the residual latent heat of composite PCMs. Notably, similar phenomena of phase change temperatures and enthalpies are also found in the solidification process.

The cyclic thermal stability of composite PCMs guarantees their longterm service. Fig. 6(d) displays the DSC curves for MF@PPy-PODS/ GNP3/PW before and after 100 heating/cooling cycles. It is found from the DSC curves that phase change temperatures and enthalpy of the MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW remain almost unchanged after 100 cycles. The prepared composite PCMs show satisfied cyclic thermal stability, as evidenced by these reasonable and acceptable changes in temperature and latent heat in the long-term use process.

Thermal conductivity is an essential parameter in the thermal performance of composite PCMs. As shown in [Fig. 7](#page-7-0)(a), the thermal conductivity of PW is undesirable at 0.28 W/m⋅K, which is comparable to Yang's study [\(Yang et al., 2019](#page-10-0)). However, as the PW is impregnated into the sponge skeleton of MF, the thermal conductivity value of MF/PW slightly decreases to 0.25 W/m⋅K. Two reasons can explain the decrease in thermal conductivity: the one is that the unmodified sponge skeleton of MF has a lower thermal conductivity, and the other is that the unmodified sponge skeleton of MF has the hydrophilic interface property with a more considerable interface thermal resistance. Conversely, [Fig. 7](#page-7-0)(a) shows a rewarding improvement of the thermal conductivities of MF@PPy/PW and MF@PPy-PODS/PW to 0.31 and 0.33 W/m⋅K, which is attributed to the modification of PPy and PODS on

## *3.4. Solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capability of composite PCMs*

Solar-thermal energy conversion and storage is a meaningful way to utilize solar energy. Benefiting from the phase change process of PCMs to storing the thermal energy, the light absorption capacity of composite PCMs determines the solar-thermal storage efficiency. [Fig. 8](#page-8-0)(a) shows the UV–Vis–NIR absorption spectra of PW and composite PCMs with a broadband absorption from 200 to 800 nm. The light absorption capacities of PW and PW/MF composite PCMs are relatively weaker. In contrast, after modifying the sponge skeleton of MF, the spectral absorption of MF@PPy/PW and MF@PPy-PODS/PW composite PCMs show a significant improvement. Besides, it is attractive that MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs exhibit the strongest spectral absorption under the effect of GNP. This observation indicates that the modified MF is an excellent sponge skeleton for the solar-thermal energy conversion, imparting the composite PCMs with solar energy storage properties. Besides, GNP improves light absorption and solar-thermal energy conversion ability.

[Fig. 8\(](#page-8-0)b) shows the experimental device diagram, consisting of the simulant light source, thermal insulation material, and data acquisition unit. #### *3.5. Solar thermoelectric application*

As shown in [Fig. 9\(](#page-9-0)a), the thermoelectric conversion system with composite PCMs driven by solar energy is proposed in this study, which consists of four parts: simulant light source, MF/PW, and MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs, thermoelectric device, and water cooling system. The thermoelectric device is used to generate electricity directly from a heat source ([Borhani et al., 2021](#page-10-0)). To reduce the variation in power generation caused by temperature fluctuations on both sides of the thermoelectric device, the composite PCMs can be employed as the temperature stabilizer for the thermoelectric device to perform the solar-thermal conversion and storage process. The water cooling system maintains the cold side temperature of the thermoelectric device at 25 ◦C. Depending on this system, it is possible to convert solar energy into thermal energy and then into electric power.

As the lights turn on, the open-circuit voltage of the thermoelectric conversion system with MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW reaches 0.78 V at 2 Sun, which is 1.59 times that of the MF/PW, as shown in [Fig. 9\(](#page-9-0)b). Due to the modified MF and GNP, MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW has a better solar-thermal energy conversion and storage capacity than MF/PW. And the hot side of the thermoelectric device with the MF@PPy-PODS/ GNP3/PW provides a higher temperature than the MF/PW. Furthermore, [Fig. 9](#page-9-0)(c) displays that the open-circuit voltage of MF@PPy-PODS/ GNP3/PW is improved remarkably at the light intensity of 4 Sun, reaching 0.91 V, which is 1.48 times that of the MF/PW. This phenomenon is attributed to the higher heat source temperature provided by the increased light intensity in the solar-driven thermoelectric conversion system. Interestingly, as the lights turn off, the voltages of two composite PCMs remain for a while and slowly drop. Indeed, the MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW remains at a higher voltage than that of the MF/PW for the same time without the simulated solar light irradiation, indicating that the utilization of MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs increases the output power and efficiency of the thermoelectric device and improves the sustainability of the system. #### **4. Conclusions**

In this study, the superhydrophobic and flexible sponge skeleton of MF@PPy-PODS was facilely devised and fabricated via the in-situ polymerization method of PPy and dipping coating of PODS. Then, the novel high-performance MF@PPy-PODS/GNP/PW composite PCMs consisting of the modified MF, PW, and GNP were successfully prepared by the vacuum impregnation method, integrating good leakproof ability, high thermal conductivity, and desired solar-thermal conversion efficiency. The major conclusions could be drawn as follows:

- (1) Due to the PPy and the PODS layer coating on the sponge skeleton, the modified MF ensured to provide the leakproof ability and improve the thermal conductivity and solar-thermal energy conversion efficiency of MF@PPy-PODS/PW composite PCMs. The mass loss rate of composite PCMs during the phase change process could be reduced to less than 0.34%. The thermal conductivity of MF@PPy-PODS/PW improved to 0.33 W/m⋅K. The solar-thermal storage efficiency of MF@PPy-PODS/PW composite PCMs reached 75.68%.
- (2) Increasing GNP concentrations exhibited an improving trend in the thermal conductivity and the solar-thermal energy conversion ability while showing a decreasing trend in the phase change enthalpies. Compared to PW, MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW composite PCMs showed a 110.71% improvement in the thermal conductivity, increased to 0.59 W/m⋅K, and the highest solarthermal storage efficiency, increased to 79.36%, while exhibited the lowest phase change enthalpy, decreased to 130.61 J/g. Therefore, adding GNP with an appropriate mass fraction was essential to the residual latent heat of composite PCMs.
- (3) As the temperature stabilizer in the thermoelectric conversion system driven by solar energy, the open-circuit voltage of the MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW achieved 0.78 and 0.91 V at 2 Sun

and 4 Sun, which were 1.59 and 1.48 times that of the MF/PW. And the voltage of MF@PPy-PODS/GNP3/PW remained at a higher voltage than that of the MF/PW for the same time without the simulated solar light irradiation, owing to the effect of the modified sponge skeleton of MF and GNP on the output power and sustainability of the thermoelectric conversion system with composite PCMs driven by solar energy.

The prepared composite PCMs based on the modified MF and GNP showed good application prospects for solar-thermal energy storage. In addition, the thermoelectric conversion system with composite PCMs driven by solar energy had the advantages of direct power generation, high reliability, low maintenance requirements, and environmental protection. This study provided a potential strategy for developing and using clean energy.
