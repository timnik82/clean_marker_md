# Graphene-CoO/PEG composite phase change materials with enhanced solar-to-thermal energy conversion and storage capacity

#### ABSTRACT

Composite phase change materials (PCMs) with excellent light adsorption and high thermal conductivity have great potentials in the efficient solar-to-thermal energy utilization. In order to improve the light absorption and solar-to-thermal conversion efficiency, reduced graphene oxide nanosheets decorated with CoO nanoparticles (rGO@Co) were used as support for polyethylene glycol (PEG) PCMs. The hierarchical structure composed of rGO nanosheets provides a large space to impregnate a high amount of PEG with good shape stability during the solid-liquid phase transition. Meanwhile, CoO nanoparticles and rGO nanosheets with high light absorption can provide rGO@Co with enhanced light absorption capacity and solar-to-thermal conversion capacities. In addition, the well distributed CoO nanoparticles on rGO nanosheets could contact PEG chains better, act as heat transfer intermediate and reduce the interface thermal resistance for the thermal energy transfer between rGO and PEG during the thermal energy storage/release process. Therefore, the prepared rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs exhibit enhanced thermal conductivity with 51.79%~152.46% increasement as compared with pure PEG, high energy storage densities (131.94 J/g), good shape stability and high solar-to-thermal energy storage/release rates. Hence, the light-driven composite PCMs have great potentials for the efficient utilization of sustainable solar energy.

# **1. Introduction**

In the past decades, various kinds of energy storage and conversion technologies have been researched and developed to alleviate the energy crisis, such as using stainable solar energy (e.g., photocatalytic water splitting [\[1,2\]](#page-8-0), photocatalytic CO2 reduction [[3](#page-8-0)], solar cells [[4](#page-8-0)], solar-to-thermal conversion [[5,6\]](#page-8-0)), increasing the efficiency of fossil fuels [\[7\]](#page-8-0), or utilizing phase change materials (PCMs) to store/release the waste heat [[8](#page-8-0)]. Among these approaches, solar-to-thermal conversion has been attracting increasing attention and widely studied by researchers due to their environmental friendliness, cost effectiveness, and great potentials in practical applications [\[9,10](#page-8-0)]. In the solar-to-thermal conversion, the light-absorbing medium converted the energy from absorbed light into thermal energy. Therefore, the efficiency of solar-to-thermal conversion is strongly influenced by the light-absorbing medium, the solar-to-thermal conversion rate and the thermal energy storage materials.

Different from the traditional sensible heat storage materials, PCMs could realize high thermal storage and release capacity during the solidliquid phase change process, as well as small temperature variation and excellent stability. As a result, PCMs have been attracting increasing attention and applied in many fields, such as waste heat recovery, intelligent air-condition buildings, solar energy conversion, thermoregulated textiles and so on [[11\]](#page-8-0). To prevent the leakage during the solid-liquid phase change process, numerous solid supports (e.g., graphene oxide [[12\]](#page-8-0), graphene aerogel [\[13,14](#page-8-0)], carbon nanotube array [\[15](#page-8-0), [16\]](#page-8-0), mesoporous silica [\[17](#page-8-0)], carbon aerogel [[18\]](#page-8-0), metal organic frameworks [\[19](#page-8-0)], expanded graphite [\[20](#page-8-0),[21\]](#page-8-0)) have been investigated to encapsulate the PCMs in the shell layer or the porous channels as shape-stabilized composite PCMs [\[22](#page-8-0)–24]. However, for the solar-to-thermal conversion, carbon nanomaterials (e.g., expanded graphite, graphene nanosheets, carbon nanotubes, porous graphitized carbon) with high surface area and thermal conductivity, to encapsulate PCMs has been found to be one of the most effective strategies, which

Previous studies have demonstrated that reduced graphene oxide (rGO), carbon nanotube and carbon fiber as fillers or supports in PCMs composite can increase the photothermal conversion efficiency [\[25](#page-9-0),[26](#page-9-0)]. Nevertheless, the solar-to-thermal conversion efficiency of carbon-based PCMs is still not high enough due to the limitation of the thermal conductivity of the supports. As well known, one of the popular strategies to enhance the thermal conductivity of composite PCMs is to introduce highly thermally conductive metal particles into the PCMs system. Qian et al. prepared a series of highly thermally conductive composite PCMs by blending diatomite modified with Ag nanoparticles and PEG. The results showed that the phase change enthalpy of PEG/diatomite/Ag PCMs reached 111.3 J/g, and the thermal conductivity of PEG/diatomite PCMs containing 7.2 wt% Ag was 0.82 W m 1 K <sup>1</sup> , which was higher than that of PEG/diatomite composite [\[28](#page-9-0),[29\]](#page-9-0). In addition to precious metals, magnetic nanoparticles (e.g., Fe3O4) have also been used to prepare highly thermally conductive composite PCMs. Wang et al. prepared magnetocaloric Fe3O4-modified graphene nanosheets (Fe3O4-GNS) with improved light-collecting properties, which was then uniformly dispersed in PEG/SiO2 PCMs with efficient electromagnetic and solar energy conversion and storage [[30,31](#page-9-0)]. At present, the application of transition metal or oxide nanoparticles on the solar-to-thermal conversion of composite PCMs is still scare. Among the known transition metal oxides, diamond oxides (e.g., CoO) have been widely considered as having the best thermal transfer performance, but they have never been applied to the field of phase change.

In this work, rGO nanosheets decorated with well-distributed CoO nanoparticles were used as polyethylene glycol (PEG) support to improve the solar-to-thermal conversion for the first time, in which CoO nanoparticles act as heat transfer medium and reduce the interface thermal resistance between rGO and PEG chains. Besides, due to the high light absorption capacity and excellent thermal conductivity of rGO and CoO [[32,33\]](#page-9-0), rGO@Co improved the solar-to-thermal energy conversion efficiencies of the rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs. What's more, the prepared rGO/PEG and rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs also have improved thermal conductivities, increased solar-to-thermal conversion rates, excellent shape stabilities and high thermal energy storage efficiencies.

# **2. Experimental**

# *2.1. Raw materials*

Graphite flake (AR), KMnO4 (AR), 30 wt% H2O2 (AR), NaNO3 (AR), 36.5% HCl (AR) and concentrated H2SO4 (AR) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. NaOH (98%), Co(NO3)2⋅6H2O (98.0–102.0%) and polyethylene glycol 2000 (PEG2000) were purchased from Alfa Aesar Ltd.

# *2.2. Synthesis of rGO@Co composites*

Graphene oxide (GO) was prepared from graphite flakes by the modified Hummers' method [[34\]](#page-9-0). Typically, 1 g of graphite and 0.5 g of NaNO3 were dispersed into 40 ml of concentrated sulfuric acid by continuous stirring under ice water bath. Then, 3 g of KMnO4 was slowly added to the above solution and stirred in ice water bath for 2 h. After continuing stirring at room temperature for 12 h, 100 ml of boiling water was added into the above mixture and stirred for another 20 min 7 ml of H2O2 aqueous solution (30 wt%) was added under continuous stirring until no more bubbles were formed. The graphene oxide nanosheets were collected by centrifugation, washed several times with 5% HCl solution and deionized water until neutral, followed by freeze

drying.

The rGO@Co composites decorated with CoO nanoparticles were prepared via a thermal annealing method according to our previous results [[35\]](#page-9-0), as shown in [Scheme 1a](#page-2-0). Typically, 48 mg of GO was well dispersed in 24 ml of deionized water under sonication for 2 h. Then a certain amount (6 mg, 8 mg or 12 mg, respectively) of Co(NO3)2⋅6H2O was added into the GO solution and stirred at room temperature for 12 h. After adjusting the pH of the solution to ~11 with 1 M NaOH and stirring for 12 h, the resulting solid was collected, washed with water several times and freeze-dried for 24 h. The collected black powder was pyrolyzed directly at 500 �C for 2 h under flowing N2 atmosphere. According to the mass ratio of GO to Co(NO3)2⋅6H2O, the obtained products were named as rGO@Co-8, rGO@Co-6, and rGO@Co-4 in the order of increased CoO content in the composites. As a control, rGO was prepared using GO under the same pyrolysis conditions.

## *2.3. Preparation of rGO@Co/PEG composites*

rGO@Co/PEG composites with 80 wt% PEG2000 were prepared by a physical mixing and melt impregnation method [\(Scheme 1b](#page-2-0)). Prior to preparing the composite PCMs, the rGO and rGO@Co were vacuumed at 100 �C for 24 h to remove the possible gases/H2O. Then 0.4 g of rGO, rGO@Co-8, rGO@Co-6 and rGO@Co-4 were introduced separately into 20 ml of ethanol containing 1.6 g of PEG2000. The mixture was sonicated for 30 min, then vigorously stirred at 90 �C for 1 h, and then vacuum-dried at 80 �C for 24 h until the ethanol solution was completely evaporated. The prepared composites were named as rGO/PEG, rGO@Co-8/PEG, rGO@Co-6/PEG and rGO@Co-4/PEG, respectively.

## *2.4. Characterization*

The morphology of the samples was checked by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, ZEISS SUPRA55) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, TEI Tecnai F20). The phase structure of the samples was characterized by X-ray diffractometry (XRD, M21X) using Cu Ka ray (λ ¼ 1.541 Å). A Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, Nicolet 6700) was used to determine the chemical functional groups. The thermal stability was evaluated using a synchronous thermal analyzer (TG-DSC, Netzsch STA449F3) under flowing nitrogen atmosphere. Raman spectroscopy (Finder Vista) was performed to measure the graphitization of porous carbon. Differential scanning calorimeter (DSC, Q2000) measurement was used to characterize the thermal properties of composite PCMs. The thermal conductivity of composite PCMs was measured by a laser flash system (Laser Flash Apparatus NETZSCH MicroFlash LFA 457) at room temperature.

# *2.5. Solar-to-thermal conversion test*

The experimental device of the solar-to-thermal conversion system includes light capture, solar-to-thermal conversion, data collection and processing equipment. Solar-to-thermal conversion tests were performed on PCMs under simulated sunlight using a 300 W xenon lamp. First, 0.12 g of PCMs sample was ground and pressed into a tablet under a pressure of 8 MPa. Secondly, the pressed sample is placed in a position where the light intensity is about one solar light intensity for testing. The infrared temperature probe is used to monitor the temperature of the target sample at the same time. The data acquisition system with Temp Monitor S2 software was used to collect the data with the solar-tothermal conversion process.

As we all know, there are three main ways of thermal transfer: thermal conduction, thermal convection and thermal radiation [[46\]](#page-9-0). During the solar-to-thermal conversion process, the thermal transfer from the sun to the surface of the composite PCMs is thermal radiation, and the thermal transfer within the solid materials is thermal conduction. Therefore, thermal conductivity will be the main factor determining the solar-to-thermal conversion efficiency of solid composites. The above analysis shows that the solar-to-thermal conversion efficiencies of the rGO/PEG and rGO@Co/PEG composites are much higher than that of pure PEG, which can be due to the significantly superior thermal conductivity and light absorption ability of rGO and CoO than pure PEG. In addition, the rGO@Co/PEG composites with CoO nanoparticles exhibit higher light absorption ability than rGO/PEG and increases with the CoO amount [\[47](#page-9-0)], as determined by the UV–vis spectrum of rGO/PEG and rGO@Co/PEG composites (Fig. S8). Nevertheless, the solar-to-thermal conversion capacity of rGO@Co-6/PEG is slightly higher than that of rGO@Co-4/PEG, which may be due to the increased interfacial thermal resistance and hindered phonon transmission resulted from the uneven size distribution and aggregation of CoO nanoparticles in rGO@Co-4 since the filling amount, shape and size of thermally conductive fillers have an impact on the solar-thermal energy conversion and thermal energy transfer [[48\]](#page-9-0).

Combined with the characterization results and thermal properties of rGO@Co and rGO@Co/PEG PCMs, a possible mechanism for the enhanced solar-to-thermal conversion and storage has been proposed, as shown in [Scheme 2.](#page-8-0) The functional area of the solar-to-thermal conversion in PCMs is mainly divided into three parts: light harvesting part, solar-to-thermal conversion part and thermal energy storage part. In the rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs under the irradiation of simulated sunlight, rGO nanosheets and CoO nanoparticles with good light absorption ability absorb the solar energy to become solar excited states (Step A) [[47\]](#page-9-0). During the solar-to-thermal conversion part, the solar energy in the solar excited rGO nanosheets and CoO nanoparticles was converted into thermal energy, which can be transferred by rGO and CoO nanoparticles to their contacted PEG chains with higher enthalpy capacity (Step B). Moreover, as demonstrated by the SEM and TEM results, CoO nanoparticles are well embeded into the rGO nanosheets while part of the CoO nanoparticles are also exposed to the atmosphere. Therefore, during the thermal energy storage part, the highly thermallly conductive CoO nanoparticles can act as the thermal energy transfer intermedicates or bridges between encapsulated PEG and rGO nanosheets, which would reudce the interface thermal resistance between the carriers and PEG and improve the thermal storage efficiency of the composites. When there is no continuing solar radiation, the rGO@Co/PEG composites with high enthalpy energy can release the stored thermal energy for special applications (Step C).

## **4. Conclusions**

The rGO@Co composites with well-distributed CoO nanoparticles and large porosity were synthesized via a facile thermal annealing method and applied as PEG support for solar-to-thermal energy conversion and storage. Our results show that the rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs can effectively store the thermal energy coming from solar energy absorption and conversion. The high thermal conductivity and high light adsorption ability of CoO nanoparticles and rGO nanosheets and their intimate contact improved the thermal conductivity with 51.79%– 152.46% improvement of pure PEG and solar-to-thermal conversion efficiency of composite PCMs. In addition, the obtained rGO@Co/PEG composite PCMs have high energy storage capacities (131.94 J/g) and thermal energy storage/release rates, and outstanding form stability for at least 50 cycles, which could offer promising applications for solar energy harvesting, conversion, and storage as thermal energy.
