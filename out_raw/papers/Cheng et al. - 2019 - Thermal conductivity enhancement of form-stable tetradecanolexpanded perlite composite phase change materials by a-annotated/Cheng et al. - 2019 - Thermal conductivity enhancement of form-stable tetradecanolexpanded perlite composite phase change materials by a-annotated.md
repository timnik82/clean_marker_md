## ABSTRACT

In this study, form-stable tetradecanol (TD)/expanded perlite (EP) composites by adding Cu powder (CuP) and carbon fiber (CF) to enhance thermal conductivities have been prepared via vacuum impregnation method. The thermal conductivity enhancer (TCE) is introduced by two methods of mixing TCE with phase change materials (PCMs) and implanting TCE into matrix materials. The result demonstrates the former is better than the latter in aspects of thermal property, thermal conductivity and controllability, moreover, adding ratio of TCEs gather in more appropriate interval from 2.5% to 3% suitable for the former. For CPCMs, FT-IR results indicate it is no chemical interaction among raw materials but physical combination via two methods. CPCMs prepared by the method of mixing TCE with PCMs have better phase change latent heat and thermal stability by DSC and TGA, while thermal cycling measurements show that form-stable composite PCMs have adequate stability even after being subjected to 200 melting/freezing cycles. Therefore, the method of mixing TCE with PCMs has better thermal property and controllability than that of implanting TCE into matrix materials and the prepared CPCM has great application prospect in solar energy utilization, building material, indoor cooling instrument and so on for thermal energy storage.

## 1. Introduction

Phase change materials (PCMs) are one type of materials that absorb (release) the thermal energy during the melting (freezing) process. Owing to their excellent energy storage property, PCMs have usually been utilized as one of the most widely implemented materials in the fields of solar energy utilization [\[1\]](#page-6-0), energy conservation in buildings [\[2,3\],](#page-6-1) thermal insulation, and thermal regulation [\[4\].](#page-6-2) PCMs are divided into different varieties such as paraffin [\[5,6\],](#page-6-3) fatty acids [\[7,8\]](#page-6-4), polyhydric alcohols [\[9,10\]](#page-6-5), salt hydrate etc. [\[11,12\],](#page-6-6) and among them many studies have been carried out by researchers in this field.

Thermal energy storage capacity is the most basic property, which depends on the characteristic feature of PCMs. Tetradecanol (TD), belongs to the kind of polyhydric alcohols, is the type of PCM that has high phase change latent (over 200 kJ/kg) and lower phase change temperature (around 32 °C), in addition it possesses of the features of non-corrosive, environmentally friendly and no separation of subcooling phase. More importantly, TD is abundantly available and can be

Meanwhile, thermal conductivity is the most important parameter for the PCM. However, the PCM itself has the low thermal conductivity relatively, which causes the slow heat transfer at the period of absorbing thermal energy and the utilization efficiency of PCM will be enormously reduced, it becomes very necessary to improve the thermal conductivity of experimental PCMs. Currently, there are two methods to resolve the problem, one way is that PCMs are impregnated into the porous materials with high thermal conductivity, another is that the high thermal conductivity materials, such as carbon fiber, Cu powder and so on, are added into the PCMs to achieve the enhancement. The porous materials as a supporting material compositing with the PCMs has been researched widely by plenty of scholars, Yu et al. [\[15\]](#page-6-8) prepared new Microencapsulated composite PCM by using calcium carbonate as the shell and n-octadecane as PCM to enhance the thermal conductivity. Yuan et al. [\[16\]](#page-6-9) made use of graphene nanoplatelets and expanded graphite as enhancer to improve the palmitic-stearic acid and gained high thermal conductivity. Mehrali et al. [\[17\]](#page-6-10) composed the nitrogen-doped graphene and utilized it to load palmitic acid to obtain shape stabilized composite phase change material with high thermal conductivity. In addition, both Zhang [\[18\]](#page-6-11) and Zhang [\[19\]](#page-6-12) used expanded graphite as matrix material to enhance the thermal conductivity of PCMs. Despite this, the porous materials with high thermal conductivity have very less variety, and generally have more expensive prices like expanded graphite or grapheme, which has a narrower application and is very difficult to be promoted. Thus, it is particularly important that cheaper and more effective materials are chosen to enhance the conductivity. Due to the reasonable price and high thermal conductivity, Cu powder (CuP) and carbon fiber (CF) usually have been used to implant the PCMs to enhance the thermal conductivity.

In addition, the implantation way of thermal conductivity enhancer (TCE) has important influence on the enhancement effect. Generally, two methods have been used to finish it, the one is by mixing TCE with PCMs, another one is implanting TCE into matrix materials.

In this study, TD is used as thermal energy storage materials, expanded perlite (EP) studied by many researchers as the supporting material, such as Sengul [\[20\]](#page-6-13), Karaipeklin [\[21\]](#page-6-14) and Sari [\[22,23\]](#page-6-15), is used to load the composite PCM via vacuum impregnation method, CuP and CF as the TCE are added into composites with two different methods. By the characterization of microstructure, crystal structure, thermal properties and thermal conductivity, it shows that the method of mixing the TCE with PCMs has better thermal property than that of implanting TCE into matrix materials, and also better than the work of Karaipekli [\[24\]](#page-6-16), Sharma [\[25\]](#page-6-17), Zhang [\[28\]](#page-6-18) and so on.

#### 2.1. Materials

EP and CuP were obtained from Beijing Bo stand Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd. CF was supplied from Shen Zhen Fiber Valley Technology Co. Ltd. #### 2.2. Sample preparation

The preparation process of CPCMs was divided into two parts by different methods. First, TD-CuP/EP and TD-CF/EP CPCMs preparation were conduct by weighing CuP, CF and TD with ratio from 0% to 4% as showed in [Table 2,](#page-2-0) which was transferred into drying oven and stirred evenly for 12 h at 80 °C to obtain homogeneous mixture. EP as the supporting material was added into the mixture, put into vacuum drying oven with the station of vacuum and taken out with repeated process of absorbing to get the final TD-CuP/EP and TD-CF/EP CPCMs. [Fig. 1](#page-2-1) shows the preparation process.

Next, TD/EP-CuP and TD/EP-CF CPCMs were prepared via vacuum impregnation method. CuP and CF were added into ethanol respectively. By the ultrasonic dispersion, CuP and CF were dispersed evenly into ethanol in 12 h. EP was added into the mixture, put into vacuum drying oven with the station of vacuum and EP-CuP and EP-CF composites were obtained. Finally, TD was added into EP-CuP and EP-CF taken out with repeated process of absorbing to get the TD/EP-CuP and TD/EP-CF CPCMs. The preparation process was depicted in [Fig. 1](#page-2-1).

#### 2.3. Sample characterization

The chemical compatibility between the components of the prepared CPCMs was investigated by FT-IR spectroscopy technique. FT-IR spectra were taken on a KBr disk in the frequency range of 4000–400 cm−<sup>1</sup> by using a FT-IR spectrophotometer (Spectrum 100). Measurements of solid liquid phase change temperatures and latent heat capacities of composite PCMs were carried out by using a DSC instrument (DSC214Polyma) under nitrogen atmosphere and at a heating rate of 10 °C/min. Melting points and freezing points were obtained by drawing a line at the point of maximum slope on the DSC peak. The latent heat was calculated by numerical integration of the peak using the inherent software of the DSC instrument. The thermal stability of the composite PCM was determined by using America TA instruments Q5000. In each case, about 10 mg specimens were heated from 25 °C to 700 °C using a linear heating rate of 10 °C/min under nitrogen atmosphere. The thermal conductivity was test by XFA 500 instrument, according to the standard test method by the Flash Method (ASTM E 1461-01), the specimen was made into the pill that corresponding dimension of is ɸ12.7 mm × 2 mm.
