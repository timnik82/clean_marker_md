# Nano additive enhanced salt hydrate phase change materials for thermal energy storage

a Research Centre for Nano-Materials and Energy Technology (RCNMET), School of Engineering and Technology, Sunway University, Petaling Jaya, Selangor Darul Ehsan, Malaysia; <sup>b</sup> College of Engineering, University Malaysia Pahang, Kuantan, Pahang, Malaysia; <sup>c</sup> School of Energy Management, Shri Mata Vaishno Devi University, Katra, Jammu & Kashmir, India

#### ABSTRACT

Energy storage plays a vital role in sustainable development. Focus on energy storage using phase change materials (PCMs) are of current research hotspot due to high latent heat value. Nevertheless, poor thermal conductivity, supercooling, phase separation, corrosive nature of salt hydrate is of great concern. Distress related to properties of PCM is resolved using nano additives. Major research focus on the dispersion of nano additive with PCM depends on (a) technique of preparing a novel composite PCM; (b) improvement of their thermophysical characteristic; and (c) advanced application for human comfort without polluting the environment. This article presents a critical review of hybridization techniques of metal, carbon and polymer additives on salt hydrate PCMs. To facilitate researchers, the significant variation on thermophysical properties of salt hydrate with nano additives are vitally compared, analysed and critically reviewed. This review article also includes the advanced application of nano additivesbased salt hydrate PCMs.

# Thermal energy storage and its significance

<span id="page-1-3"></span>Thermal energy storage (TES) is a technique to store heat or cold energy within materials for later use under different temperatures [\[3](#page-37-2)]. TES system consists of heat storage medium (stores energy in the form of latent or sensible heat), energy transfer method (liberate and absorb the required amount of heat) and a containment system (to provide proper insulation and leak proof) [[4](#page-37-3)]. Sensible heat depicts the amount of heat that is added or removed from a substance during temperature change. Latent heat describes the amount of heat that is added or removed from a substance during phase change at a constant temperature. The storage is a near-isothermal process that can provide significantly high storage density with smaller temperature swings in comparison with sensible storage systems. In addition, latent heat storage has the capacity to store the heat of fusion at a constant or near-constant temperature that corresponds to the phase transition temperature (PTT) of the phase change material (PCM). TES enhances solar power utilization through the storage facility. It also bridges the gap between energy supply and demand by prevailing over the intermittent nature of solar power [[5\]](#page-37-4). When the temperature of materials like water, air, concrete, ceramic, sand, brick, bedrock and cast iron are increased or decreased by external heat source sensible heat storage occurs [[6\]](#page-37-5). Latent heat storage undergoes phase transition of material from solid– liquid on melting and freezing, liquid–gas on evaporation and condensation, solid to gas on sublimation and deposition or solid–solid with change in

<span id="page-1-0"></span><span id="page-1-8"></span><span id="page-1-7"></span><span id="page-1-6"></span><span id="page-1-2"></span>crystalline structure [\[3](#page-37-2),[7\]](#page-37-6) vice versa. Phase changes, such as vaporization and melting, are accompanied by relatively large changes in internal energy and enthalpy as bonds between molecules are broken and reformed [[8\]](#page-37-7). Heat absorbed during a phase change is used to modify the microscopic structure of the substance. Heat absorbed by a solid during melting is used to break the bonds between atoms and a tiny fraction of heat absorbed is converted into work (PdV – where P is the pressure and dV is the change in volume) which is associated with volume change [\[9](#page-37-8)]. Thus the potential of latent heat is highly linked with the number of breakage in chemical bonds. The phase transition from solid to gas and from liquid to gas phase offer higher later heat as they undergo dominant volume change during phase transition, as well they require closed storage containers [\[10](#page-37-9)]. PCM has well-developed technology towards heat liberation and absorption during isothermal phase change and can be actively used for TES [\[11](#page-37-10),[12\]](#page-37-11). They are also on the verge of versatile development over the past decade. The most commonly used PCM falls under the category of solid–liquid phase transition. Integration of PCM for TES is an interesting phenomenon, where PCM are filled within a storage system. High-temperature fluids are passed through the pipeline on the storage system. PCM absorbs heat energy from the high-temperature fluids until it completely transfers its phase from solid to liquid. # Metal additives

Metals with desirable mechanical and thermal characteristics are commonly mixed with salt hydrate PCM to enhance the thermophysical property of salt hydrate. In current trends, metal foams and oxide of metal particles are doped to improve the desirable properties. It is also noted that the inclusion of metal additives (Ag, Cu, Pt, Al2O3 and SnO2) into PCM exhibit better thermal properties and are worth using as carrier materials. Synthesis of metal additives is based on techniques like chemical co-precipitation, emulsion process or phase transition technique. Most desirable metal composite additive is metal foam as it exhibits good strength, stiffness, large surface area and high thermal conductivity. Metal foam reveals a dominant role in improving the heat conduction rate as well, and it weakened the supercooling effect by decreasing the kinetic coefficient [[68\]](#page-39-5).

<span id="page-5-5"></span><span id="page-5-4"></span>Zhou et al. [[69\]](#page-39-6) used CaCl2·6H2O as salt hydrate PCM, and porous material copper foam as a metal additive in his experimental investigation. An attempt was carried out to address the supercooling issue of CaCl2·6H2O. Results show the degree of supercooling to be 5°C for salt hydrate with copper foam matrix and 8°C for salt hydrate without copper foam. Li et al. [\[68](#page-39-5)], in their experimental investigation, used hydrate salt <span id="page-6-0"></span>of SAT as PCM. This investigation was carried out to address the issue of low thermal conductivity, degree of supercooling and phase separation of SAT. Copper foam as supporting matrix, disodium hydrogen phosphate dodecahydrate (DSP) as nucleating agent and carboxyl methyl cellulose (CMC) as thickener were used as the additive. Initially, SAT and DSP were blended at room temperature followed by heating of salt hydrate in a water bath until 80°C. Second, CMC was added to the liquid mixture and the sample was thoroughly mixed using magnetic stirring. Modified SAT PCM was impregnated into the porous space of copper foam to enhance shape stabilization and thermal conductivity. Zn(NO3)2·6H2O salt hydrate is another commonly used PCM for TES due to its low melting temperature of 35°C and high latent heat potential, however, the effect of supercooling has to be solved for wide applications. Zinc oxide was included as a nucleator by ultrasonication process to resolve the supercooling issue. The effect of zinc oxide in addressing the degree of supercooling was studied for 1000 thermal cycles, and it was noted that the modified salt-hydrated effectively stores and releases heat energy up to 750 cycles. Raj et al. [[71\]](#page-39-11) performed a characterization study using manganese organo-metallic salt hydrate (MSS– PCM). In his research work, he studied the chemical stability effect of metal gallium on MSS–PCM after different thermal cycles. #### Carbon additives

Variety of carbon nano additives is widely used with salt hydrates to overcome the problem of low thermal conductivity. The conductive nature of carbon nanoparticles is highly responsible for the thermal conductivity of the composite on hybridization. In addition, the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate is greatly influenced by the type of carbon material, dimension of carbon material, surface area of the nano additive and concentration of doping. CNT, CNF (carbon nano fibre), graphene and graphite are the commonly doped carbon additive with salt hydrate PCM. Thermal property enhancement depends on the surface area of additive; however, graphene and single-walled CNT exhibit high thermal properties due to higher surface energy and surface area. As well, the inclusion of carbon additives make a negative impact on the energy storage density of the composite, as the inclusion of nano additives decreases the amount of active salt hydrate [[75\]](#page-39-12). 2D carbon nanoparticles also act as nucleation agents and contribute towards suppressing of supercooling effect in the salt hydrate. # <span id="page-15-6"></span>Hybridization techniques and spectroscopic evidence

<span id="page-15-8"></span><span id="page-15-7"></span>Hybridization is the inclusion of two nanoparticles with base PCM. Two common categories under which the hybridization of nanoparticles and salt hydrate settle are aqueous stirring and dry blending. In aqueous stirring method, the salt hydrate PCM are heated up to their melting point to transform the PCM to their liquid state followed by dispersion of nanoparticles, whereas in dry blending, the salt hydrates and nanoparticles are mixed in their solidstate. Under aqueous stirring different methods like (a) classical sonication, (b) sonication microprobe, (c) magnetic stirring, (d) impregnation and (e) mechanical agitation are included. Similarly, under dry blending, different techniques like (a) hand-mix, (b) planetary ball mill and (c) freezer/mill are categorized. Few recent research works adopting the above hybridization methods are discussed for better understanding. Eutectic salt hydrates PCM were melted to liquid phase followed by dispersion of α-Al2O3 nanoparticles to enhance the performance of PCM without phase segregation. The mixture was ultrasonicated at 120W for 30 min to ensure proper dispersion [[111\]](#page-40-19). Calcium chloride hexahydrate PCM and boron nitrate nanoparticles were mixed using the sonication microprobe technique at 40000 Hz for 15 min and was cooled in a water bath to avoid rise in temperature [\[112\]](#page-40-20). Nanoactivated carbon and sodium fluoride nanoparticles were dispersed into eutectic salt hydrate PCM using a magnetic stirring technique to suppress the degree of <span id="page-16-1"></span><span id="page-16-0"></span>supercooling [\[113\]](#page-40-21). Calcium chloride hexahydrate was mixed with graphene oxide (GO) using a mechanical agitator for 30 min in a water bath at 40°C temperature [[114\]](#page-40-22). Hand-mix techniques are the commonly adopted dry blending method, which is used for mixing of PCM and nanoparticles for building application, where the quantity is high.

However, CNT/CNF, graphene and graphite nano additives highly contribute towards enhancing thermal conductivity of salt hydrate due to their unique property and structure. #### Thermal conductivity

# Mechanism and factors influencing thermal conductivity

Thermal conductivity is a parameter that replicates the heat transfer rate of PCM, and it indicates the ability of <span id="page-17-1"></span>a material to transmit heat energy. In solid materials, thermal conductivity occurs due to atomic vibration and due to conduction by free electrons. When thermal energy comes in contact with material, the surface atom of the material starts vibrating and transmits the energy to the adjacent atom and the wave proceeds and the energy diffuses till it remains in contact with atom. In ceramic and polymer materials, thermal conductivity due to phonon are predominant, whereas in pure metals, heat transfer by free-electron are higher [\[120](#page-40-28)]. Inorganic salt hydrate have their electrons bound to the ionic crystal lattice and the very few free electrons do not act effectively as heat conduction carriers, thus inorganic PCM depends on the vibration of crystal lattice for heat transfer. #### Melting point

# Mechanism and factors influencing melting point

The selection of inorganic salt hydrate PCM for any application is based on their melting point. The melting temperature of salt hydrate is recommended to be below the temperature of the heat source and above the temperature of ambient condition for TES. Melting temperature or phase change temperature indicates the critical point during phase transfer from solid to liquid and vice versa. For refrigeration application PCM with 21° C, for air conditioning application 21–28°C, for energy saving in buildings 21–60°C and for water heating application 29–80°C are preferred. The higher melting point of salt hydrate contribute towards a longer melting duration and provides better thermal cycle before complete melting of PCM. The major factors influencing melting point are (a) intermolecular forces (b) molecular bonding and (c) shape and size of molecule.

#### <span id="page-23-2"></span>Degree of supercooling

# Mechanism and factors influencing super cooling

<span id="page-23-1"></span>Supercooling is a phenomenon where the PCM is noted to be in a liquid state even below the fusion temperature. Supercooling effects delay the recovery of the heat energy stored within salt hydrate. Almost all inorganic salt hydrate PCM exhibit supercooling effect than organic PCM. On a note nucleation agents are incorporated with PCM to trigger the crystallization process in salt hydrate and reduce the supercooling effect. In PCM research, better understanding of melting and crystallization processes are important. Crystallization takes place by the following three steps (a) induction duration (b) growth phase of crystal and (c) regrowth of crystal. In the induction phase, the nuclei develop and reach a sufficient size; then nucleation initiates followed by diffusion to the nucleus and gets adsorbed with the surface. Supercooling or sub cooling is an intrinsic property of material; in addition inherent and non-pertinent factors also affect the rate of supercooling. The major factors influencing melting point are (a) cooling rate (b) sample size (c) encapsulation medium (d) chemical purity (e) vibration (f) morphology of material and (g) crystallization temperature. The technique to reduce supercooling includes both passive and dynamic methods. In passive methods, supercooling is suppressed by homogeneous and heterogeneous nucleation. #### Phase separation

# Mechanism and factors influencing phase separation

Phase separation deteriorates the TES potential of salt hydrate PCM due to the separation of salt and water molecules. On the propagation of time and favourable conditions, the salt solution prepared by melting transforms back to pure salt. This occurs when salt and water molecules are uniformly diffused and can reestablish their molecular structure. Due to the density difference between salt and hydrate, the anhydrous salt separates from salt hydrate solution under gravity and settles down. This is the mechanism of phase separation occurring in salt hydrate PCM. The factors that influence phase separation are (a) solubility and (b) temperature of the salt hydrate solution.

#### Thermal cycling

# Mechanism and factors influencing thermal cycling

In PCM, one thermal cycle refers to absorbing and releasing heat energy by salt hydrate once. Similarly to number of charging and discharging cycles in electrical batteries, number of heat charging and heat discharging cycles are important parameters for PCM. Thermal cycle reliability indicates the performance of inorganic salt hydrate after many thermal cycles. Similar to physical stability, thermal stability of salt hydrates are crucial parameter for better utilization of TES. By measuring the thermal and physical properties of NDPCM before and after many numbers of thermal cycles, the thermal stability can be determined. In order to ensure the economic feasibility of salt hydrate PCM, thermal cycles must be as high as possible without deterioration in the property. Factors influencing thermal cycling are (a) absorption of moisture, (b) improper crystallization, (c) impurities and defects.

# Effect on thermal cycling with composite additive

On application of pure salt as PCM for TES, there is a sharp decrease in heat storage potential of salt hydrate with an increase in a number of thermal cycles. Dropin heat storage potential are predominant due to the following reasons (a) evaporation of water molecules (b) exudation of fluid and (c) phase segregation. However, the above issues are controlled with salt hydrate mixture due to close space structure, as they prevent the direct interaction of hydrated salt with the external environment and avoid overflow of fluid and evaporation of fluid. Thermal cycling consistent with salt hydrate can be improved by reduction of phase separation as both are directly related. Phase separation of salt hydrate is prevented by adopting the microencapsulation and confinement technique. Based on the stabilizing mechanism, followed by various encapsulation techniques, the thermal reliability also changes. Core-shell encapsulation technique of encapsulation is slightly effective than the incorporation of porous supports and 3D network structure, as core<span id="page-28-0"></span>shell technique completely packs the salt hydrate. Encapsulation of Na2S2O3·5H2O [[142](#page-41-32)] using sol–gel slightly reduced the latent heat, in-situ mini emulsion technique of encapsulation adopted for Mg(NO3)2- ·6H2O salt hydrate [[7](#page-37-6)] ensured better thermal reliability after 100 cycles. An elaborated study on thermal cycling of CaCl2·6H2O was performed by 1000 number of cycles[[135](#page-41-25)]. The salt hydrate was studied without the inclusion of nano additives; results ensured variation in latent heat of salt hydrate by 10.4% for 10 thermal cycles, 6.4% for 100 thermal cycles and the attractive part was drop in 0.3% latent heat after 1000 thermal cycles. The results ensure that there is no particular trend or regular change in a thermal property of salt hydrate with an extended number of thermal cycles, however, reasons justifying the variation in latent heat of fusion were improper crystallization of salt hydrate as the testing quantity were taken in milligrams.

## Application of composite additives for TES

With an intense to meet the future energy needs on a global scale, PCM are integrated with thermal systems to enhance TES. Improved TES tends to increase the potential of sustainable energy sources. Thermal energy changes in salt hydrate PCM during the transition of phase provide the potential to integrate salt hydrate PCM with heating and cooling of buildings. In addition to latent heat, the isothermal phase transition characteristic ensures its application in textile clothing's, BTMS and in electronic cooling applications. The thermal cycling ability of salt hydrate PCM offers TES facility in solar thermal applications like solar water heaters and solar air heaters.

<span id="page-28-1"></span>Energy demands for building heating and cooling, building construction are swelling every year due to the high usage of energy in developing nations. #### Novel smart textiles

Garments are a vital part of all human beings, which are in direct contact with the body. Research hotspot <span id="page-30-2"></span>in the area of smart textile technology focuses on developing smart cloth materials for medical, military, aerospace and industrial applications that can provide warmness during winter and coldness during summer with the help of PCMs [\[177](#page-42-24),[178\]](#page-42-25). PCM are formed into microcapsules for its integration with fabrics. The commonly used technologies for incorporating PCM into textile fabrics are (a) fibre technology, (b) coating, (c) lamination, (d) melt spinning, (e) injection moulding technique and (f) foam technique. The major drawback of incorporating salt hydrate with fibres is that the PCM can be washed away during wiping, abrasion and washing of cloths.

Incorporating salt hydrate PCM with fabrics causes three effects, namely, (a) heating effect, (b) cooling effect and (c) thermo regulating effect. Utilization of salt hydrate PCM in heat-protective gloves can avoid burn injuries to the worker [[178\]](#page-42-25). PCM integrated glove will ensure the temperature of glove till the melting point of PCM is reached, as well it also extends the usage time of glove. Due to the non-combustible characteristic, salt hydrate PCM are encapsulated with silicon rubbers. Active thermal insulation effect are provided using PCM in skin wear clothing in addition to the passive effectives, thereby improving comfort. PCM also ensures moisture management and thermal comfort in chemical protective fabrics used in laboratories. Fire fighters are easily exposed to burn injuries as they face high heat flux during operation. The inclusion of PCM in firefighting garments increases the duration required to cause burn accidents [[179](#page-42-27)]. The time taken by the PCM for its complete melting is the extended duration the firefighter gets before the occurrence of burn. The melting point of salt hydrate PCM are preferred based on the heat flux intensity like 50–150°C for low heat flux exposures and 50–200°C for medium and high heat flux exposure. The major discussion is on the optimal mass of salt hydrate PCM that has to be used in the garment to ensure minimal load of the garment for the comfort of fire fighters.

<span id="page-31-6"></span><span id="page-31-2"></span><span id="page-31-1"></span><span id="page-31-0"></span>Transfer of heat and moisture in porous textile fabrics is a complex phenomenon. In addition to heat transfer by conduction, convection and radiation transfer of moisture through wicking, evaporation and diffusion also take place. Similarly, for PCM fabrics in addition to latent heat, the thermal performance of clothing is influenced by PCM quantity, method of incorporation and size of micro PCM. Thus, to analyse the overall thermal performance of PCM fabric, a number of indices like thermal regulation capability, thermal psycho sensory intensity (TPI) and static thermal insulation are characterized. For a PCM fabric, TPI expresses the thermal perception by the body and it can be measured using a fabric intelligent hand tester. TPI tends to increase with an increase in PCM level in the textiles [[181\]](#page-42-28). ## Battery thermal management system

<span id="page-32-6"></span><span id="page-32-5"></span><span id="page-32-4"></span><span id="page-32-3"></span><span id="page-32-2"></span><span id="page-32-1"></span>Temperature dependence on battery plays a vital role which decides their performance. Operating battery at lower temperature leads to a decline in several parameters like charge acceptance, power capacity, life span and round-trip efficiency [\[186](#page-43-0),[187\]](#page-43-1). On the other hand, operating battery at a higher temperature contributes to several issues like rapid self-discharge and capacity fade [[188\]](#page-43-2). In order to achieve better performance battery should be operated at an intermediate temperature. Lithium-ion batteries are very sensitive to temperature, thus a suitable BTMS system needs to be implemented to ensure the optimum operating range of electrical battery [\[189\]](#page-43-3). Battery thermal management is carried out by air cooling system [\[190\]](#page-43-4), direct and indirect liquid cooling system [\[191\]](#page-43-5), thermo-electric heating–cooling [\[192](#page-43-6)] and PCM [[193\]](#page-43-7). PCM integrated BTMS system due to its passive energy utilization to cool battery seems to be a smart option. PCM absorbs the heat generated during the charging and discharging process due to its phase changes phenomena, and it prevents further temperature rise naturally. Peak temperature loads of lithiumion batteries are reduced by employing PCMs. Performance of organic PCM like paraffin and fatty acids like capric acid, lauric acid, dimethyl sabacate and vinyl stearate are experimentally studied by various researchers compared to inorganic PCM as they offer outstanding thermal reliability. The low thermal conductivity nature of paraffin was modified by employing carbon fibre additives in different proportions. The experimental analysis also reflects that blending 2 mm long carbon fibre with 0.46 wt-% into PCM can effectively reduce the peak temperature rise by 45%. In addition, the corrosive nature, supercooling characteristic and dehydration are some serious issues, which further makes inorganic PCM less competitive to organic PCM to be applied for thermal management. Thermal runaway is a phenomenon during which an exothermal process prompts the other processes, which subsequently result in an unmanageable rise in temperature. Thermal runaway commonly occurs in batteries and leads to fire in several cases.

<span id="page-32-7"></span>Cao et al. [[194\]](#page-43-8) employed expanded graphite doped with SAT to prevent the thermal runaway issue faced by lithium-ion batteries. Due to the potential of SAT to store heat in two stages like (a) latent heat storage up to a temperature of 58°C and (b) thermochemical heat storage up to a temperature of 106–140°C, they predominantly reduce the overheating of the battery and maintains the temperature under control. The effect of supercooling always causes a negative impact on the application of salt hydrates, whereas this particular work has used supercooling of PCM for controlling heat storage and release. Experimental result shows the heating of lithium-ion battery at a rate of 7.5°C min−<sup>1</sup> . Composite PCM with expanded graphite impregnated over the surface of salt hydrate with a mixture of MgCl2- ·6H2O and Mg(NO3)2·6H2O at 41.3:58.7 wt-% were used to perform an elaborative study on electrical cycling and thermal runway penetration of lithiumion batteries [[197\]](#page-43-11). Salt hydrates PCM were filled into the battery pack for enhanced thermal management. A comparative analysis of the system was carried out using organic and inorganic salt mixture shows that inorganic salt hydrates successful restrict thermal runaway propagation in lithium-ion batteries. Ramandi et al. [[198\]](#page-43-12) carried out a numerical study to understand the thermal performance of Na2(SO4- )·10H2O and Zn(NO3)2·6H2O. The analysis model shows effective performance of the battery when double-shell PCM insulations are adopted. ## Conclusions and future challenges

## Conclusions

Nano additive-based salt hydrates can effectively prevail over the drawback of thermal conductivity, supercooling and phase separation of conventional PCMs, which makes them highly capable for TES applications. The significance of salt hydrates has attracted numerous researches in the recent decade. In this review article, the authors specifically summarized the advances in nano doped salt hydrate, thermal performance and their smart applications. Following are the conclusions and authors' comments on nano additives dispersed salt hydrate PCMs;

- . Regarding doping of nano additive into salt hydrate, mainly three materials are considered (a) metal additive which exists in the form of powder and metal foams; (b) carbon additive which is classified based on their dimension as 0D, 1D, 2D and 3D under which fullerene, CNT/CNF, graphene and graphite nano additives are used; (c) polymer additives. Metal nano composite in the form of metal foam reveals a dominant role in improving the heat conduction rate as well it weakens the super cooling effects of salt hydrate. Graphene, due to its higher thermal conductivity and surface area, makes a notable impact on doping with PCM. Carbon additives are the major nano additives that are doped with most of the salt hydrate PCMs achieving enhanced thermal properties. However, the lack of studies on the hydrophilia nature of multi-dimensional carbon additives with salt hydrate PCMs are not scientifically discussed by various authors.
- . Numerous hybridization techniques exist for the inclusion of metal, polymer and carbon-based nano additives into salt hydrate; however, the highly adoptable technique for dispersion of additive into salt hydrate PCMs are; (a) melt and direct mixing; (b) vacuum impregnation method; (c) direct mixing followed by ultrasonication and magnetic stirring. For preparing solid pills, the sintering process is mostly followed by researchers. All the research works carried out so far in regard to characterization and enhancement of thermophysical properties are only for lab-scale application, and there is a variation in supercooling value as reported by different authors with respect to different binding agents and additives.
- . Very limited research on the influence of morphological structure, size of nano additive, the concentration of nano additive on the thermophysical characteristic of salt hydrate have been performed.
- . Thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCMs was observed to increase by almost 2000% on the dispersion of EG, however, research work by certain authors shows a decrease in thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCMs with a dispersion of EP. Similarly, latent heat of salt hydrate PCMs dropped by a maximum of 54%, however, majority of nano additives reduced the enthalpy change by 10–20% which was under permissible value. Variation in melting point of salt hydrates were between −20% and +20%. Complete suppression of supercooling

effect was observed with the inclusion of α-Fe2O3 with SAT, but there is a lack of work with the same metal additive doped with other salt hydrates to study the supercooling effect. But supercooling effect increased by 600% on the dispersion of nano-Cu with salt hydrate PCM as they are nonnucleating agents. Most of the studies highlighting the reduction of supercooling in salt hydrate with nucleating agents have limited their testing with less than 50 numbers of thermal cycles, ensuring a lag in the reliability of nucleating agents for longterm application. In addition, the crystal structures are yet to explore in detail.

. An application point of view, nano-salt hydrate PCM is widely used in building heating and cooling application due to the low cost of salt hydrate. Owing to the corrosive nature of salt hydrate, they are yet to be explored with electronic gadgets. Every research work is initiated to transfer the demerits in every theory and application into merits. One such drawback of salt hydrate PCM is the degree of supercooling. Heat storage and release of PCMs are always temperature-dependent. On the contrary, utilization of supercooling effect of salt hydrate PCMs for enhanced thermal management of electrical battery are an outstanding research work which operated by a trigger mechanism to initiate the heat release and storage as per the need.

## Future challenges

Despite several advanced research progress in the synthesis and development of nano additive salt hydrate PCMs, quite a few aspects of future research are highly recommended to pursue the betterment of the research community as given below;

- . Current researches are mainly focused on integrating nano additive with salt hydrate PCM in a laboratory scale and performing characterization testing of their thermal property. However, nano additives PCM are used in building materials for thermal comfort of human beings. In addition, many real-time experimental application-oriented research works are to be carried out to propel the commercialization of salt hydrate PCMs.
- . On the emerging hybridization techniques, nano additives are doped with salt hydrate using techniques like impregnation, direct mixing and ultrasonication. But no hybridization techniques or methods have been reported yet on doping of nano additives with PCM for large-scale application. The major problems associated with such hybridization techniques are (a) non-uniform distribution of additives over PCM; (b) possibility of

- hydrate to get evaporated at atmospheric condition; (c) highly feasible for corrosion reaction due to the atmospheric oxygen content. Considering the above-mentioned factors lab-scale hybridization techniques need to be explored. As per the analysis and literature review, the authors suggest that a direct mixing technique with a stirring process is preferable for large-scale applications. Alongside with fast-growing graphene industries worldwide, salt hydrate PCM with graphene additive is expected to make a remarkable contribution.
- . Though GO nano sheets make a remarkable contribution towards enhancing the thermal property of salt hydrate PCM. GO nano sheets have poor dispersion ability and get easily agglomerated. To overcome agglomeration of nanoparticles, additives like surfactants are added. However, this technique may change the base property of salt hydrate NDPCM and may affect the performance. Thus, there is a need for external mechanism to avoid agglomeration and the poor hydrophilia nature of carbon additives.
- . Dispersion of CNT, SWCNT and MWCNT with salt hydrates are an important aspect for the enhancement of thermal properties of PCM. The hydrophobic characteristic of carbon material makes them less dispersion in salt hydrate and gets entangled with other carbon particles. Thus CNT, SWCNT and MWCNT additives are made hydrophilic by functionalization using macromolecule; however, the studies carried out so far are only with organic PCMs, whereas inorganic PCMs are yet to be discussed.
- . Higher manufacturing costs of nano additives are notable issues that hinder the commercial application of salt hydrate. Economic studies considering the thermal performance, environmental benefits over conventional systems and overall cost of the system with net pay back period would be an insight for attracting high investing stakeholders. So far, no special article provides detailed economic benefits with proper accuracy considering all the operational, economic and environmental parameters.
- . Lack of theoretical modelling on nano structured PCM to yield empirical relations to predict the thermal properties of NDPCM are limited for the combination of only certain nano additives and inorganic salt hydrates. Literature also lags theoretical mechanisms for understanding the enhancement and deterioration of properties like supercooling, thermal conductivity, latent heat and viscosity on doping of nano additives.
- . Salt hydrate PCMs are applied for long-term applications that are justifiable with thermal cycling tests. However, recent studies on thermal cycling performance of nano additive salt hydrate are

- very minimal. Thermal cycling analyses are performed for only few hundreds of cycles to ensure thermal and chemical stability which is very far away from real-time applications. Better thermal stability with a low degree of supercooling and less leakage are required for more exploration.
- . Similar to the trigger mechanism adopted in lithium-ion battery thermal management, a desirable heating control strategy of energy storage and release from salt hydrate PCM can be applied to building application, solar thermal cooking application, smart textiles and electronic gadgets based on Gibbs function, which is discussed.
- . Existing advanced research work ensures confinement of organic PCM for offering enhanced stability over leakage issues and low thermal conductivity; on the other hand, this technique is not adopted for inorganic salt hydrate PCM due to evaporation of water from the pores during phase transition. The evaporation of water affects the reversibility of thermal cycling.
