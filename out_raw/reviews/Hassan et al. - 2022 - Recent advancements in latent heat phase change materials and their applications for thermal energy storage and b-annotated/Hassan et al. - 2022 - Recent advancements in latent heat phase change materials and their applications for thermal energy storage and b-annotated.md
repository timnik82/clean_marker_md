# ABSTRACT

Phase change materials (PCMs) have received substantial interest for their ability to store and release latent heat for energy conservation and thermal control purposes. PCMs are available in a variety of latent heat and melting points but their performance is low due to low value of thermal conductivity which limits its usage. The addition of highly thermal conductivity nanoparticles, porous metal foams and encapsulation methods have been used to address this issue and try to fix the low thermal conductivity of PCMs which is broadly discussed in this manuscript. The ability of PCM to store and release the thermal energy prompted the researchers to use it in potential applications. The energy retained and emitted by PCMs may be used for a variety of purposes, such as in photovoltaic (PV) panels, thermoelectric generators, building air-conditioning, air and water heating systems, heat exchangers, desalination solar stills, textiles, thermal management of electronic equipment and batteries and food packaging. Therefore, in this review, first the advancements in thermal properties of PCMs are thoroughly discussed in terms of enhancement in melting and solidification rates. After that, the use of PCMs in various applications is then explored, and conclusions are drawn accordingly. Based on analysis of recent literature, it was discovered that the phase transition temperature, phase transition enthalpy and thermal conductivity are three important parameters for the selection of an appropriate PCM for use in various applications. The current status of these advanced energy storage materials is also presented in this review. Lastly, some challenges and future recommendation are also proposed for future researchers which will bring a revolution in thermal management field.

# **Various advancements in PCMs**

PCMs have drawbacks like low thermal conductivity, leakage, volume change during phase transition, flammability, super cooling, corrosiveness, and poor stability. Thermal conductivity is a critical property of PCM that influences heat transfer capacity and its low value reduces thermal response rate [\[75\]](#page-30-0). A variety of techniques have been investigated in literature to improve the performance of PCMs that include addition of NPs, adding expanded graphite [\[76,77\],](#page-30-0) and preparing eutectic PCMs [\[20\]](#page-29-0), porous metal foams [\[78,79\],](#page-30-0) heat pipes [\[80,81\]](#page-30-0) and capsulation of PCM as represented in Fig. 3. Encapsulation, or microencapsulation, is the process of completely covering and isolating the core from its surroundings. The thermal stability and leakage resistance of PCMs increased significantly after encapsulation, greatly expanding the application domains of PCMs. Microencapsulation is described as a physical or chemical reaction that produces a homogenous and stable shell material enveloping the core material. On the basis of the formation mechanism and shell state, microencapsulation methods may be divided into three categories: physical, chemical, and physicochemical [\[82\]](#page-30-0).

The initial stage in producing microencapsulated PCM and nano encapsulated PCMs is often the production of an emulsion (oil/water). The dispersed phase is primarily comprised of one or more PCM. The composition and synthesis technique of the coating around the PCM droplets vary depending on the synthesis methodology. The emulsion polymerization, suspension polymerization, and coacervation encapsulation techniques are primarily used to synthesize PCM encapsulated in organic polymers. Interfacial polymerization and in situ polymerization techniques are used to synthesize PCM encapsulated in organic and inorganic coatings. PCM encapsulated in inorganic coatings is usually produced by the sol–gel technique [\[83\]](#page-30-0). The various capsulation methods of PCM are summarized in Table 1.

Therefore, the above discussed are some ways to increase PCM thermal performance that recently gained significant attention.

#### *Addition of nanoparticles in base PCM*

The addition of high thermal conductivity NPs improves the heat transfer within PCMs. NPs function as a link for thermal energy transfer since they have a low thermal resistance. Nano enhanced PCMs (NEPCMs) are being studied due to high thermal conductivity of NPs and their effect of enhancement of heat storage and retrieval rate of PCMs. There are a lot of studies which explore the influence of NPs parameters like size, shape, materials and concentration on the PCM thermal properties. It is reported that the heat transfer rate and thermal reliability of PCMs have improved with the addition of NPs. The nonmetallic NPs have better influence on thermal characteristics of PCMs than metallic NPs [\[86\].](#page-30-0) Thermal conductivity of NEPCMs is reported to enhance by 20% to 100% as compared pristine PCM. The NPs addition in salt hydrates PCM improve their sub cooling, phase segregation and thermal conductivity properties [\[87\].](#page-30-0)

Nizetic et al. [\[88\]](#page-30-0) critically reviewed studies of NPs enhanced fluid in terms of preparation, applications and thermal properties. Findings showed that the use of NPs in PCMs increased the thermal conductivity but marginally lowered latent heat. In TES and cooling systems, NEPCMs are mainly used. Authors concluded that literature does not provide the optimal preparation process for nanofluids, economic and environmental assessments, safety concerns and handling guidance.

## *Shape-stabilized PCMs*

Shape-stabilized PCM is another process in which molten PCM is held in place by capillary action in porous material. Shape-stabilized materials include mineral materials like diatomite, vermiculite and perlite, as well as polymeric materials such as polyethylene, polyurethane, or acyclic resins. Mineral porous materials are mostly used to hold organic PCMS, while polymeric porous materials are used to contain both organic (mainly paraffins) and inorganic PCMs. The methods adopted to prepare the shape-stabilized PCMs include physical adsorption, electrospinning, centrifugal spinning, and intercalation. The selection of suitable porous material is influenced by porosity, thermal conductivity, availability, chemical consistency with the PCM, and preparation techniques [\[144,145\]](#page-32-0).

Atinafu et al. [\[146\]](#page-32-0) developed a composite PCM of n-dodecane PCM that used biochar-CNTs as a supporting material. According to the findings, biochar-CNTs and CNT assisted PCM had PCM loading capacities of 70.2% and 83.2%, as well as latent heat of 127.4 J/g and 152.3 J/ g, respectively. Besides composite PCM showed better chemical uniformity, form stability, and thermal stability up to 600 C with considerable weight loss. Also, Atinafu and colleagues [\[147\]](#page-32-0) investigated the shape-stabilized PCM of eicosane and biochar harvested from wheat straw and softwood. According to the results, the composite PCM of eicosane-wheat straw biochar had the highest TES capacity of 75 J/g and 37.1% encapsulation capacity, while the composite PCM of eicosane-softwood biochar had the lowest TES capacity of 52 J/g and 26.4% encapsulation capacity.

Li et al. [\[148\]](#page-32-0) used silicon carbide powder, silicon carbide whisker, and silicon carbide nanofiber supports to prepare composite stearic acid PCMs by vacuum impregnation and ethanol techniques. According to findings, the thermal conductivities of composite PCMs were 61.80% to 169.29% moreas compared to pure PCM. Besides, the composite PCM demonstrated improved charging/discharging behavior and TES capacity. Chriaa et al. [\[149\]](#page-32-0) prepared the shape-stabilized PCM by using hexadecane and various styrene-b-(ethylene-co-butylene)-b-styrene (SEBS) copolymer concentrations with polyethylene coating. The composite PCM was reported to be melted at 25 ◦C with latent heat of 162.3 J/g. With good form stability, PCM loading approached 80% and thermal deterioration of the composite PCM commenced above 200 C.

Shen et al. [\[150\]](#page-32-0) produced shape-stabilized PCM by impregnating cellulose sponge with an emulsified hydrated salt/paraffin composite. According to the findings, shape stabilized PCM increased thermal stability, provided high storage density, decreased super cooling, and had negligible leakage. The phase shift enthalpy of shape stabilized PCM was found to be 227.3 J/g. Xie et al. [\[151\]](#page-32-0) prepared shape-stabilized composite PCM by physically mixing the eutectic mixture (Na2HPO4-12H2O and Na2CO3-10H2O) with diatomite and coating it with polyurethane acrylate. # *Performance enhancement using capsulation of PCM*

Addition of NPs increases the viscosity and causes agglomeration in the base fluid due to a stabilization problem [\[69,86\].](#page-30-0) One approach to increase PCM performance include encapsulation of PCM [\[152\].](#page-32-0) PCM encapsulation is a recent advancement in the field of TES that addresses problems in PCMs such as phase change leakage, low thermal conductivity, low thermal stability, corrosion, super cooling, and environmental deterioration. There are several kinds of encapsulation based on capsule size, such as macroencapsulation, microencapsulation, and nanoencapsulation, although the microencapsulation is the most researched size.

The shell material selection is influenced by several factors, including the operating temperature range, thermal conductivity, toxicity, the encapsulation method, permeability, and compliance with the base PCM. Organic shell materials include melamine–formaldehyde, urea–formaldehyde, polystyrene, polyurethane, and polymethyl methacrylate, among many others. Inorganic shell materials include silica, calcium carbonate, and titania. Some of the methods to prepare encapsulated PCMs are polymerization, coacervation, sol–gel, ionic gelation, spray drying, electrostatic, and solvent evaporation. Each these PCM encapsulation methods has its own combination of benefits and drawbacks, the encapsulation approach should be chosen based on the type of PCM, cost, desired encapsulation efficiency, thermal stability, and reagent accessibility [\[152,153\].](#page-32-0)

## *Usage of advanced PCMs in thermoelectric generator*

To limit emissions and the use of fossil fuels, researchers are exploring and improving clean energy technologies. One of the emerging technologies to transfer heat energy into electricity is the TEG. Small size, light weight, no moving parts, no noise, no pollution, and a long service life are some of the benefits. TEG is made up of thermoelectric units wedged between a heat source and a cooling source. TEG modules produce seeback voltage because of temperature differences. The energy produced can be stored in a battery and used to power small electronic devices [\[432\]](#page-38-0). TEG can utilize waste heat from various sources e.g. automotive [\[433\],](#page-38-0) waste heat in industries [\[434\],](#page-38-0) solar energy [\[435\],](#page-38-0) to generate electricity. #### **Major challenges and future recommendations**

The use of phase change materials (PCM) in energy storage applications has received a lot of attention. Following are some challenges recommendations for careful consideration based on this study. These suggestions can be summed up as follows:

- Even though a great deal of research is being conducted on the thermophysical and chemical properties of PCMs for their use in different applications, there is still no international standard for PCM testing and analysis. Furthermore, the thermophysical properties of PCMs are the subject of much debate in the literature.
- The materials with the best thermal properties, such as paraffin wax or fatty acids, are highly flammable.
- Organic PCMs have major drawback of low thermal conductivity which negatively effects its utilization in applications.
- The cost of nanomaterials, instability, and agglomeration are all significant obstacles to the widespread use of nanomaterials based PCM.
- In recent studies, nanoparticles have demonstrated to have promising as a PCM additive. However, there is a shortage of studies into hybrid nanomaterials' implementations. As a result, further research into the thermal properties and performance analysis of hybrid NPs based PCM is needed.
- NEPCM preparation is time-consuming and expensive processes. Although studies have shown that NEPCMs have higher thermal conductivity than single PCMs, it has also been found that the phase enthalpy of PCMs decreases when nanoparticles are added. Their long-term stability is also difficult to maintain compared to initial stabilization.
- Encapsulation of PCMs is costly and time consuming, give poor thermal characteristics. Further research needs to be done to optimize the PCM encapsulation process.
- Although most PCMs and NEPCMs have defined melting points, but thermophysical properties of liquids and solids vary amongst studies. A standard test procedure and database should be established to yield correct results and records.
- For several years, researchers have tested and analyzed the thermal conductivity of PCMs using a variety of techniques. The usage of various values of PCM thermophysical properties revealed a major problem with research criteria.
- To maintain stable performance of the storage systems, future work must avoid phase separation and supercooling of PCMs.
- It is suggested that the use of PCMs in various sectors should be assessed in terms of their effect on the atmosphere and human health.
- The fundamental feature of an energy storage system is phase transition enthalpy. So, detailed examination of the existence of interactions between PCM and nanostructures, as well as their potential impact on the performance of these systems is needed.
- Techniques for preparing composite PCMs are complex and sensitive, and they have a direct effect on performance improvement. As a result, it is strongly advised to report the preparing procedures and methods in as much depth as possible.

# **Conclusion**

Researchers are eager to come up with a variety of solutions to address the global energy crisis. High energy consumption levels, along with the depletion of traditional energy resources, have compelled the world to investigate green energy technologies. PCMs are classified as a category of special materials with excellent potential in latent heat storage technologies, as they can retain and release a significant amount of energy when experiencing phase transition process. The major concluded points are:

- PCMs have a high phase transition enthalpy but have a poor thermal conductivity. Various methods have been used to solve the problem of poor thermal conductivity, and studies have shown that dispersing different high thermal conductivity nanoparticles to PCMs accelerate the phase transition rate and improve the energy storage/release capacity of PCMs.
- In most studies, adding nanoparticles lowers the melting temperature and raises the freezing temperature.
- Composite PCMs are developed by impregnating PCMs with porous materials such as metallic foams, carbon foams, and expanded

- graphite. Prepared composite PCMs are important for improving the PCM's thermophysical properties and preventing leakage issue.
- Despite composite PCMs' high thermal efficiency, there is leakage issue of PCM, particularly at high temperatures. As a result, it is suggested that PCMs be encapsulated before being used. However, further research is needed to improve current encapsulation techniques and make them commercially available.
- Recent studies have focused on improving performance of PCMs for both residential and industrial applications. The selection of PCM for a specific application is dependent on latent heat, thermal conductivity, sensible heat, melting point, and chemical, kinetic properties.
- Due to better properties of advanced PCMs such as high "k" and high HT, these are being utilized in broad range of thermal applications that desalination, building envelopes, solar heaters, smart textiles, and food packaging applications, for improving thermal and electrical efficiency in PV thermal systems, thermoelectric generators, and lithium-ion batteries as well as glazing in for building applications.
- According to recent studies on the use of PCM for heat energy storage, energy consumption was reduced in all conditions and the utilization of advanced PCMs to retain thermal energy significantly improve the performance. More type of advanced PCMs should be explode by researchers to bring new areas of applications for their usage for good thermal performance in that field.
- However, still leakage and little better performance of performance of advanced PCMs compared to conventional PCMs is still the bottleneck of PCMs which should be considered by future researchers, which will bring dawn in thermal management field.
