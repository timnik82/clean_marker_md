# Nano additive enhanced salt hydrate phase change materials for thermal energy storage


#### ABSTRACT

Energy storage plays a vital role in sustainable development. Focus on energy storage using phase change materials (PCMs) are of current research hotspot due to high latent heat value. Nevertheless, poor thermal conductivity, supercooling, phase separation, corrosive nature of salt hydrate is of great concern. Distress related to properties of PCM is resolved using nano additives. Major research focus on the dispersion of nano additive with PCM depends on (a) technique of preparing a novel composite PCM; (b) improvement of their thermophysical characteristic; and (c) advanced application for human comfort without polluting the environment. This article presents a critical review of hybridization techniques of metal, carbon and polymer additives on salt hydrate PCMs. To facilitate researchers, the significant variation on thermophysical properties of salt hydrate with nano additives are vitally compared, analysed and critically reviewed. This review article also includes the advanced application of nano additivesbased salt hydrate PCMs.

# Thermal energy storage and its significance

Thermal energy storage (TES) is a technique to store heat or cold energy within materials for later use under different temperatures [\[3](#page-37-2)]. TES system consists of heat storage medium (stores energy in the form of latent or sensible heat), energy transfer method (liberate and absorb the required amount of heat) and a containment system (to provide proper insulation and leak proof) [[4](#page-37-3)]. Sensible heat depicts the amount of heat that is added or removed from a substance during temperature change. Latent heat describes the amount of heat that is added or removed from a substance during phase change at a constant temperature. The storage is a near-isothermal process that can provide significantly high storage density with smaller temperature swings in comparison with sensible storage systems. In addition, latent heat storage has the capacity to store the heat of fusion at a constant or near-constant temperature that corresponds to the phase transition temperature (PTT) of the phase change material (PCM). TES enhances solar power utilization through the storage facility. It also bridges the gap between energy supply and demand by prevailing over the intermittent nature of solar power [[5\]](#page-37-4). When the temperature of materials like water, air, concrete, ceramic, sand, brick, bedrock and cast iron are increased or decreased by external heat source sensible heat storage occurs [[6\]](#page-37-5). Latent heat storage undergoes phase transition of material from solid– liquid on melting and freezing, liquid–gas on evaporation and condensation, solid to gas on sublimation and deposition or solid–solid with change in

crystalline structure [\[3](#page-37-2),[7\]](#page-37-6) vice versa. Phase changes, such as vaporization and melting, are accompanied by relatively large changes in internal energy and enthalpy as bonds between molecules are broken and reformed [[8\]](#page-37-7). Heat absorbed during a phase change is used to modify the microscopic structure of the substance. Heat absorbed by a solid during melting is used to break the bonds between atoms and a tiny fraction of heat absorbed is converted into work (PdV – where P is the pressure and dV is the change in volume) which is associated with volume change [\[9](#page-37-8)]. Thus the potential of latent heat is highly linked with the number of breakage in chemical bonds. The phase transition from solid to gas and from liquid to gas phase offer higher later heat as they undergo dominant volume change during phase transition, as well they require closed storage containers [\[10](#page-37-9)]. PCM has well-developed technology towards heat liberation and absorption during isothermal phase change and can be actively used for TES [\[11](#page-37-10),[12\]](#page-37-11). They are also on the verge of versatile development over the past decade. The most commonly used PCM falls under the category of solid–liquid phase transition. TES using PCM provides numerous notable significances, as shown in [Figure 1.](#page-1-0) Thermal energy is used widely in domestic and industrial applications for heating. Integration of PCM for TES is an interesting phenomenon, where PCM are filled within a storage system. High-temperature fluids are passed through the pipeline on the storage system. PCM absorbs heat energy from the high-temperature fluids until it completely transfers its phase from solid to liquid. During thermal energy need, lowtemperature fluids are passed through the storage system, the PCM liberate heat and raises the fluid temperature. TES using PCM is accompanied with a storage

With the rise in surrounding temperature of PCM's, the PCM slowly reaches its melting point, at this junction, the chemical bonds start to break with the endothermic process and absorb heat energy with a change in state from solid to liquid. This process is called PCM charging. Similarly, with a decrease in the surrounding temperature of PCM's, the PCM reaches its freezing point, at this junction, the chemical bonds regenerate with the exothermic process and liberate heat energy with change in state from liquid to solid. This process is called as PCM discharging. The effectiveness of TES using inorganic PCM highly depends on thermophysical, chemical and economical properties. Salt hydrate PCM faces a major issue of supercooling, phase separation, lack of resistance to corrosion and low thermal conductivity. The phenomenon during which the salt hydrate PCM exists in a liquid state even after reaching their fusion temperature or freezing temperature is known as supercooling or degree of supercooling, commonly supercooling is represented in terms of temperature. Thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM normally ranges between 0.2 and 0.3 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> ; the low thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM is a drawback for high-powerdensity energy harvesting, and as thermal conductivity value is too low, there is a delay with the phenomenon of thermal energy charging and discharging; however, these problems do not exist with metal PCM. Based on the application to improve the thermal and chemical properties of PCM, composite additives are doped into PCM.

# Overview of salt hydrate PCM

Salt hydrates are denoted as AxBy·nH2O, and n represents the number of water molecules; AxB<sup>y</sup> represents the type of salt (carbonate, sulphate, phosphate, nitrate and chloride). Ion-dipole bonds consisting of anion and polar molecules are the ones that are present in salt hydrate to bound salt and hydrate. Salt hydrates PCM are widely used for medium (130–250°C) and low (0–120°C) temperature heat storage applications. During the heating process, the salt hydrate desorbs the water molecule and absorbs heat, and on cooling, salt and the hydrate molecule recombine to form salt hydrate. Heating and cooling cycles are repeated continuously during heat storage application. The heat storage and heat release process of salt hydrate are shown in [Figure 2\(](#page-2-0)a). In addition, [Figure 2](#page-2-0)(b) also represents the effect of supercooling and the nucleation phase where the solidification takes place in salt hydrate PCM during phase transition. It is evident from the graph that supercooling effect decreases the heat storage capacity and affects the heat storage performance of salt hydrate. The latent heat value of inorganic salt hydrate PCM is 17.5% higher compared to the organic PCM, consequently, they store excess thermal energy and performs better than organic PCM [\[13,](#page-37-12)[14\]](#page-37-13).

# Composite additive salt hydrate PCM and hybridization techniques

Composite additive to be dispersed in salt hydrate predominantly depends on the thermophysical property that has to be improved or modified. Dispersion of nano additive of high thermal conductivity will tend to improve the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM, e.g. thermal conductivity of multi-walled carbon nano tube (MWCNT) and graphene nanoparticles is about 3000 and 5300 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> [[61](#page-39-1)[,62](#page-39-2)]. Nano additive with better corrosion inhibition properties can resolve the issue of corrosion. A better nucleating agent would tend to reduce the supercooling effect; similarly a proper binding agent will rectify the issue of phase separation. Metal additives include metals, metal oxides and metalloids like zinc, aluminium, iron, copper, titanium oxide, zinc oxide, aluminium oxide and copper foam. Carbon nano additives include carbon nano tube (CNT), nanofibres, graphene and graphite [\[63](#page-39-3)]. Polymer additives composites include polyaniline (PANI), polyester, polyether and polyamides [\[64](#page-39-4)]. Few other notable additives include ceramics and semiconductor-based composite materials. This section particularly discusses different dispersion methods, nanoparticles, proportion of nanoparticles, and the purpose of dispersing the particular nano particle with salt hydrate PCM. The mechanism of salt hydrate and nanocomposite interaction on improving the thermal conductivity of the composite mixture is shown in [Figure 4](#page-5-0) (a). Initially, on dispersion of nanocomposite over the salt hydrate PCM, the nanoparticles disperse independently over the complete surface of salt hydrate. The nanoparticles reduce the thermal interfacial resistance between the salt hydrate molecules and improve the heat flow, thereby improving the thermal conductivity of PCM, as shown in [Figure 4](#page-5-0)(a). With a higher concentration of nanoparticles into PCM, they interact among themselves and form a big group of cluster, with poor distribution and form agglomeration, and hardly increases the thermal conductivity of the nanoparticles dispersed PCM. The next important problem with salt hydrate PCM is phase separation, which is solved by a form-stable structure. The intermolecular interaction between silica nanoparticles and sodium acetate trihydrate (SAT)–urea mixture is shown in [Figure 4\(](#page-5-0)b). Here the SiO2 acts as a supporting material that allows salt hydrate to get dispersed over the surface and avoids phase separation during phase change. Another similar interaction between salt hydrate and EV is depicted in [Figure 4\(](#page-5-0)c). [Figure 4](#page-5-0)(d) depicts the interaction between salt hydrate PCM and nucleating agent while suppressing the supercooling behaviour of salt hydrates. Nucleation agents increase the free energy of the nucleating surface and increase the growth of the PCM crystal nucleus above the nucleating agent. As a result of the above interaction, the heterogeneous nucleation rate increases in salt hydrate and reduces the degree of supercooling.

This section also critically analyses the ease towards small, medium and large-scale synthesis and dispersion methods for effective commercialization. A separate section on various dispersion techniques with spectroscopic evidence to ensure hybridization of nanoparticles with salt hydrate PCM is included.

# Metal additives

Metals with desirable mechanical and thermal characteristics are commonly mixed with salt hydrate PCM to enhance the thermophysical property of salt hydrate. In current trends, metal foams and oxide of metal particles are doped to improve the desirable properties. It is also noted that the inclusion of metal additives (Ag, Cu, Pt, Al2O3 and SnO2) into PCM exhibit better thermal properties and are worth using as carrier materials. Synthesis of metal additives is based on techniques like chemical co-precipitation, emulsion process or phase transition technique. Most desirable metal composite additive is metal foam as it exhibits good strength, stiffness, large surface area and high thermal conductivity. Metal foam reveals a dominant role in improving the heat conduction rate as well, and it weakened the supercooling effect by decreasing the kinetic coefficient [[68\]](#page-39-5).

Zhou et al. [[69\]](#page-39-6) used CaCl2·6H2O as salt hydrate PCM, and porous material copper foam as a metal additive in his experimental investigation. An attempt was carried out to address the supercooling issue of CaCl2·6H2O. Results show the degree of supercooling to be 5°C for salt hydrate with copper foam matrix and 8°C for salt hydrate without copper foam. Li et al. [\[68](#page-39-5)], in their experimental investigation, used hydrate salt of SAT as PCM. This investigation was carried out to address the issue of low thermal conductivity, degree of supercooling and phase separation of SAT. Copper foam as supporting matrix, disodium hydrogen phosphate dodecahydrate (DSP) as nucleating agent and carboxyl methyl cellulose (CMC) as thickener were used as the additive. Initially, SAT and DSP were blended at room temperature followed by heating of salt hydrate in a water bath until 80°C. Second, CMC was added to the liquid mixture and the sample was thoroughly mixed using magnetic stirring. Modified SAT PCM was impregnated into the porous space of copper foam to enhance shape stabilization and thermal conductivity. Zn(NO3)2·6H2O salt hydrate is another commonly used PCM for TES due to its low melting temperature of 35°C and high latent heat potential, however, the effect of supercooling has to be solved for wide applications. Kumar et al. [[70\]](#page-39-10) experimentally characterized the performance of Zn(NO3)2·6H2O salt hydrate by mixing nucleation agent. Zinc oxide was included as a nucleator by ultrasonication process to resolve the supercooling issue. The effect of zinc oxide in addressing the degree of supercooling was studied for 1000 thermal cycles, and it was noted that the modified salt-hydrated effectively stores and releases heat energy up to 750 cycles. Raj et al. [[71\]](#page-39-11) performed a characterization study using manganese organo-metallic salt hydrate (MSS– PCM). In his research work, he studied the chemical stability effect of metal gallium on MSS–PCM after different thermal cycles. Initially, metal gallium was encapsulated into pill in weight fraction of 5% and 1%. The synthesized nano metal gallium pills were dispersed into MSS–PCM to analyse their characteristics. [Figure 5\(](#page-7-0)a) depicts the encapsulation technique adopted for preparing nano metal gallium pills. The synthesis technique of MSS–PCM using magnetic stirring is shown in [Figure 5](#page-7-0)(b).

All dispersion and hybridization of metal additive with salt hydrate PCM are carried out by a combination of techniques like (a) blending and melting, (b) melting and impregnation and (c) melting and magnetic stirring followed by ultrasonication. The author would like to comment that, in all hybridization techniques, uniform dispersion of composite additive occurs when they are added to the liquid state PCM. And there is always a need for a dual technique for hybridization. It has been observed from the above-discussed research works that metal additives are actively used with salt hydrate PCM for enhancing thermal property and thermal stabilization. Nevertheless, none of the above research works discusses the technique to hybridize metal additives for large-scale application or for industry. All synthesis, hybridization technique, testing and characterization are designed only for a lab scale. Another major point that is not discussed by the authors is the corrosion effect of salt hydrate on impregnating with metal foams. Though metal foam offers better shape stabilization and enhanced thermal properties, the corrosive nature of salt hydrate with metal need to be discussed. For the above issue in regard to corrosion, plenty of research works are carried out by blending metal additive with organic PCM rather than inorganic salt hydrate PCM. #### Carbon additives

Variety of carbon nano additives is widely used with salt hydrates to overcome the problem of low thermal conductivity. The conductive nature of carbon nanoparticles is highly responsible for the thermal conductivity of the composite on hybridization. In addition, the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate is greatly influenced by the type of carbon material, dimension of carbon material, surface area of the nano additive and concentration of doping. CNT, CNF (carbon nano fibre), graphene and graphite are the commonly doped carbon additive with salt hydrate PCM. Thermal property enhancement depends on the surface area of additive; however, graphene and single-walled CNT exhibit high thermal properties due to higher surface energy and surface area. As well, the inclusion of carbon additives make a negative impact on the energy storage density of the composite, as the inclusion of nano additives decreases the amount of active salt hydrate [[75\]](#page-39-12). 2D carbon nanoparticles also act as nucleation agents and contribute towards suppressing of supercooling effect in the salt hydrate. Depending on the thermal property and structure, the author has rated each type of additives for doping with salt hydrates.

#### Zero dimensional (fullerenes and carbon dots)

Closed convexity molecules formed by carbon atoms are fullerenes. Fullerene groups are made of pentagonal and hexagonal faces using carbon atoms based on Cn cluster, where n value is greater than 20 [\[76](#page-39-13)]. Owing to the unique spherical structure and due to the ease of modification of structure in the molecular-level fullerene are extremely used in energy-related applications like energy transformation, energy storage and photo electronics [\[77](#page-39-14)]. Fullerene is electron affluent carbon material, thus it exhibits good electronic conductivity, strong structure and good chemical stability. Fullerenes are actively used in super capacitors [\[78](#page-39-15)] for storing electrical energy as well fullerenes are used as electro catalysis in fuel cells [\[79](#page-39-16)] for satisfying the energy demand. However, due to the low thermal conductivity of fullerene, they still lag their footprint in TES applications and are yet to be explored with salt hydrate PCMs.

## One dimensional (CNTs/CNFs)

CNT are well arranged hollow cylindrical nanostructures of carbon atom. CNT exhibit outstanding mechanical, electrical and thermal properties due to their surface area, thermal conductivity and morphology [\[76](#page-39-13)]. They are basically categorized based on structure as (a) single-walled carbon nano tube (SWCNT) (b) double-walled carbon nano tube (DWCNT) and (c) MWCNT. The property of CNT varies with respect to the degree of chirality and diameter of the nanotubes. CNF are formed by graphene nano sheets. It has been observed using transmission electron microscope (TEM) that the graphene layers are not arranged along the axis of fibre, and depending on their angle of arrangement, CNF are classified as graphene layers (a) perpendicular to fibre axis (b) piled at angle to the fibre axis. CNF are inorganic materials and about 90% of their composition is carbon element. Inorganic material with carbon elements above 99% is graphite fibre. The length and diameter of CNF are about 100 μm and 2 nm. Generally, the microstructure of nanomaterials influences the thermochemical properties of PCM. Doping nanoparticles, nanotubes as well as nanofibres improves the thermal characteristics of colloidal suspension PCM [[80\]](#page-39-17). KNO3 molten salts are doped with nanoparticle oxide like Al2O3 and SiO2 of size 2–100 nm; however, the PTT of the colloidal suspension PCM remains unaltered. Rabady and Malkawi [[81\]](#page-39-18) experimentally evaluated the performance of sodium thiosulphate pentahydrate (Na2S2O3·5H2O) with CNT nano additive. Salt hydrate PCM was melted till it attains liquid phase using a water beaker on a hot plate. A measured proportion of CNT was added into salt hydrate and was thoroughly mixed using a magnetic stirrer followed by sonication for 50 min, in addition to the nano additives; nucleation agent CMC and sodium dodecyl sulphate as a surfactant was mixed with the composite. The molten mixture of composite PCM was made into a rectangular plate using a hydraulic plate for analysing these thermal properties. Based on the characterization results it is observed that the composite salt hydrate has higher thermal conductivity compared to the base salt. CNT effectively decreases the charging and discharging time for heat storage.

#### Two dimensional (graphene)

Graphene are made of a hexagonal chain of carbon layer with adjacent carbon atoms connected strongly using a covalent bond. Thickness of graphene atom is equal to one carbon atom as it is a two-dimensional material with high stability. Owing to their inimitable molecular-level structure, graphene possesses thermal conductivity about 3000–5000 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> and this value is quite higher than many metallic elements. Thus, doping of graphene material into the networks of salt hydrate PCM would effectively improve the thermal conductivity for TES. Nano sheets of graphene molecule improve the stability of salt hydrate and contribute to overcoming the issue of phase separation; however, the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate on doping nano sheets of graphene once again depends on molecular structure. Wang et al. [\[82](#page-39-22)] prepared a foam stable PCM using Fe3O4 and graphene nano sheets. The composite preparation mechanism is well illustrated in [Figure 7](#page-10-0)(a). Results show the prepared composite to exhibit outstanding thermal stability with high PTT and effectively absorbs sunlight for better heat storage. Liu et al. [\[83](#page-39-23)] investigated on thermal management of salt-hydrated hydrogels using 2D carbon nanoparticle. Modified hydrate salt (EHS) Na2CO3·10H2O and Na2- HPO4·12H2O were used as PCM. GO and polyacrylamide-co-acrylic-acid (PAAAM) were used as carbon additive and copolymer hydrogel. The phase transition behaviour of Na2CO3·10H2O and Na2- HPO4·12H2O was studied by the interaction between GO and PAAAM. The foam stable co-polymer hydrogel salt was prepared to address the phase separation issue. The unique preparation technique and the mechanism of PAAAM and GO binding with salt hydrate are explained in [Figure 7\(](#page-10-0)b). Results show 54% improvement in thermal conductivity of GO-EHS/PAAAM compared to EHS/PAAAM composite. The copolymer hydrogels contribute towards adjustable phase change temperature of the inorganic salt hydrate. By product of many chemical processes is CaCl2·6H2O, and it possesses a latent heat capacity of 209 J g−<sup>1</sup> with a melting point of 29°C; however, they need to overcome the supercooling effect to be efficiently used for TES. Xu et al. [\[84](#page-39-24)] developed a novel composite PCM for building heating applications. Calcium chloride hexahydrate CaCl2·6H2O was used as salt hydrate PCM, nano sheets of GO as nano additive at 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 wt-% along with SrCl2·6H2O as nucleation agent for suppressing degree of supercooling. 50 g of CaCl2 salt was initially mixed with deionized water in a water bath at heated till 40°C to ensure liquid phase, now graphene nano additives are dispersed. In order to avoid agglomeration of nano additives, mixing was carried out using ultrasonicator. Zhang et al. [[85\]](#page-39-25) carried out his research investigation using CaCl2·6H2O as salt hydrate PCM. A new nano composite salt hydrate r-GO/CaCl2·6H2O was prepared by doping reduced GO. The investigation was focused on phase-change enthalpy, thermal reliability and thermal conductivity of the composite prepared. Hydrate salts were doped into the aqueous graphene followed by stirring and mixing using ultrasonicator for 30 min. An elaborated schematic view of the nano composite salt hydrate is shown in [Figure 7](#page-10-0) (d), and it also depicts the phase change enthalpy of CaCl2·6H2O with nano additives during crystallization and melting. Results depict improvement in thermal conductivity of the composite by 80% with 2.7% drop in latent heat. The composite was tested for 100 thermal cycles and noticed to have drop in latent heat of composite with a significant limit. However, in real-time application, the PCM is considered to be economically beneficial only when it offers reliable property for a higher number of thermal cycles.

Thermochemical materials (TCM) are one that has greater potential to store energy by reversible chemical reaction without any thermal losses. For medium-temperature TES applications, new TCM were synthesized by mixing MgSO4 and MgCl2 with graphene sheets [[87\]](#page-39-26). Three different TCM composite (a) graphene–MgSO4·7H2O, (b) graphene–MgCl2·6H2O and (c) graphene–MgSO4·7H2- O/MgCl2·6H2O were synthesized by impregnation of hydrated salt within graphene sheets. Owing to latent heat value of 130–150 kJ·kg−<sup>1</sup> and low-cost magnesium nitrate hexahydrate Mg(NO3)2·6H2O make themselves highly potential to be used in buildings. Wang et al. [[88\]](#page-39-27) experimentally studied the thermal performance of Mg(NO3)2·6H2O by incorporating graphene at 1, 3 and 5 wt-%. With graphene as a nano additive, CMC was doped as a surfactant. The mixing was carried out by sonication after dispersing carbon nano additive with salt hydrate in the liquid phase. The problem of poor hydrophilia of carbon materials especially multi-dimensional carbon nano particles with salt hydrate PCM are a notable issue to rectify. Mehrali et al. #### Polymer additives

Polymer nano composite (PNC) is a matrix, reinforced by various nanofiller having long-chain molecules formed by repeated identical structures [\[102](#page-40-9)]. They enhance the property of the base material when loaded in a small quantity. Due to the anticorrosive properties of polymer, they are predominantly used as an anticorrosive coating layer. Typically polymers are grouped based on their structure as homochain and hetereochain, based on processing characteristics as thermosets or thermoplastic and on their polymerization mechanism as addition polymers or condensation polymers. # Hybridization techniques and spectroscopic evidence

Hybridization is the inclusion of two nanoparticles with base PCM. Two common categories under which the hybridization of nanoparticles and salt hydrate settle are aqueous stirring and dry blending. In aqueous stirring method, the salt hydrate PCM are heated up to their melting point to transform the PCM to their liquid state followed by dispersion of nanoparticles, whereas in dry blending, the salt hydrates and nanoparticles are mixed in their solidstate. Under aqueous stirring different methods like (a) classical sonication, (b) sonication microprobe, (c) magnetic stirring, (d) impregnation and (e) mechanical agitation are included. Similarly, under dry blending, different techniques like (a) hand-mix, (b) planetary ball mill and (c) freezer/mill are categorized. Few recent research works adopting the above hybridization methods are discussed for better understanding. Eutectic salt hydrates PCM were melted to liquid phase followed by dispersion of α-Al2O3 nanoparticles to enhance the performance of PCM without phase segregation. The mixture was ultrasonicated at 120W for 30 min to ensure proper dispersion [[111\]](#page-40-19). Calcium chloride hexahydrate PCM and boron nitrate nanoparticles were mixed using the sonication microprobe technique at 40000 Hz for 15 min and was cooled in a water bath to avoid rise in temperature [\[112\]](#page-40-20). Nanoactivated carbon and sodium fluoride nanoparticles were dispersed into eutectic salt hydrate PCM using a magnetic stirring technique to suppress the degree of supercooling [\[113\]](#page-40-21). Calcium chloride hexahydrate was mixed with graphene oxide (GO) using a mechanical agitator for 30 min in a water bath at 40°C temperature [[114\]](#page-40-22). Hand-mix techniques are the commonly adopted dry blending method, which is used for mixing of PCM and nanoparticles for building application, where the quantity is high.

## Advances in enhancement and deterioration of thermal properties

The thermal property of inorganic salt hydrate PCM decides its application for storing thermal energy in various sectors. TES using salt hydrate are widely attracted among researchers due to their apparent benefits of energy storage density, cost, non- inflammable and non-toxic in nature. Nevertheless, salt hydrate has to overcome major issues like supercooling, low thermal conductivity, phase separation and corrosive nature to propel their application in commercial products. Techniques like doping of nano additive, impregnation into porous medium, encapsulation using polymer materials are adopted to overcome the drawbacks. This section critically reviews the advancement and deterioration of thermal properties of salt hydrate on hybridizing with nano additives.

#### Thermal conductivity

# Mechanism and factors influencing thermal conductivity

Thermal conductivity is a parameter that replicates the heat transfer rate of PCM, and it indicates the ability of a material to transmit heat energy. In solid materials, thermal conductivity occurs due to atomic vibration and due to conduction by free electrons. When thermal energy comes in contact with material, the surface atom of the material starts vibrating and transmits the energy to the adjacent atom and the wave proceeds and the energy diffuses till it remains in contact with atom. In ceramic and polymer materials, thermal conductivity due to phonon are predominant, whereas in pure metals, heat transfer by free-electron are higher [\[120](#page-40-28)]. Inorganic salt hydrate have their electrons bound to the ionic crystal lattice and the very few free electrons do not act effectively as heat conduction carriers, thus inorganic PCM depends on the vibration of crystal lattice for heat transfer. [Figure 12\(](#page-19-0)a) illustrates the mechanism of thermal conductivity propagation by lattice vibration and transfer of phonon. The SEM image in [Figure 12\(](#page-19-0)b) shows the low thermal conductivity region of disodium phosphate salt hydrate PCM and [Figure 12](#page-19-0) (c) depicts the CNF network for thermal conductivity improvement. Thermal conductivity by particle collision is well depicted in [Figure 12\(](#page-19-0)d). The major influencing factors of thermal conductivity are (a) nature of material, (b) size and fraction of material, (c) morphology, (d) heat capacity, (e) phonon group velocity and (f) phonon mean free path [[121\]](#page-40-29). Plastic does not conduct as its electrons are not free and are bound to the parent atom. The size of the materials decides the time of travel, the thicker the material more is travel time taken. Thermal conductivity of salt hydrate with CNT and GP increase for two reasons (a) presence of high thermal conductive carbon nano additive where graphite has a thermal conductivity of 200–250 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> and CNT has a thermal conductivity of 2500–3000 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> (b) even dispersion of nano additives into the salt hydrate.

# Effect on thermal conductivity with composite additive

High thermal conductivity shows the potential to enhance the heat transfer rate and consistent temperature distribution in thermal energy storage units. The thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM is extremely improved with the hybridization of nano additives. The thermal conductivity of salt hydrate not only depends on the property and concentration of doping additives but also on the morphological structure of the doped additive. Irrespective of the matrix of additives dope, the microstructure of salt hydrate is affected and the thermophysical properties are varied. Even with an increase in the number of thermal conduction channels and reduction in phonon scattering, the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate is improved.

Metal foam, due to their larger surface area per unit volume, their skeleton structure and high porosity voids, make themselves competitive with carbon additives to improve the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate on hybridization. Presently aluminium foam, copper foam, titanium oxide foam and nickel foam are hybridized with PCM to improve thermal properties. Copper foam with their individual high thermal conductivity of 410 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> and owing to their cellular structure, they are widely used [\[122](#page-40-30)]. Open-hole aluminium foam, due to their low density, individual high thermal conductivity of 167 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> and large surface area they are also used to improve thermal conductivity [\[123](#page-40-31)]. Mustaffar et al. [[74\]](#page-39-21) ensured irregular aluminium foam to improve thermal conductivity of salt hydrate by 24 times owing to better heat transfer characteristic. Nickel foam exhibit three-dimensional network structure, high porosity and high surface area making themselves suitable to advance the thermal conductivity of salt hydrate [[124](#page-41-0)]. Titanium dioxide metal foam is environmentally friendly, cheaper and fireproof material with high thermal conductivity. Among carbon nano additives, CNF/CNT and graphene are preferable for obtaining high improvements in thermal conductivity, though there are economically costlier. As CNF have large axial dimensions, it can spread comfortably within the salt hydrate medium; as well, the smaller diameter of CNF ensures easy preparation from EG. In addition, rather than single EG support, a combination of CNF/EG additives is preferred to enhance the thermal energy transfer at a higher rate. Similarly, DSP [\[116](#page-40-24)] was doped with CNF/EG, where the characterization results showed an increase in thermal conductivity of pure salt hydrate by 3.69 times, which is achieved due to the presence of CNF/EG in the low thermal conductivity spots of salt hydrate. Though supporting materials with porous supports improves the thermal stability and thermal conductivity of salt hydrates, few porous structures of EG exhibits more number of low thermal conductivity spots, and this major issues are of high research spot to optimize the porous structure to improve the thermal conductivity. An increase in spacing between layers of graphite oxide sheets also increases the thermal conductivity of hydrate salts and eutectic salts [\[125](#page-41-1)].

The variation taken for the comparison are the best results obtained; however, their no constant trend in the increase or decrease of property with respect to loading concentration of nano additives in salt hydrate PCM. Thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM was observed to increase by almost 2000% on the dispersion of EG; nevertheless, research work by certain authors shows a decrease in thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCM with a dispersion of EP. It is clearly observed that the maximum thermal conductivity for nano additive salt hydrate PCM obtained is 10.93 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> , however experimental research work by Wu et al. [[126\]](#page-41-2) gives us an understanding of the technique to load 40% of additives with PCM to obtain thermal conductivity in the range of 10– 35 W m−<sup>1</sup> ·K−<sup>1</sup> . #### Latent heat and heat capacity

# Mechanism and factors influencing latent heat and specific heat

Latent heat is the amount of heat that can be stored within a defined mass of material while melting and can be liberated during solidification. Latent heat stores energy within the particles of the material, phase transfer occurs during the transfer of heat and potential energy within a substance, resulting in storage of thermal energy without significant change in temperature [\[127\]](#page-41-3). Latent heat indicates the amount of thermal energy that can be stored within one kilogram of the substance. The higher the latent heat value of the substance, the lesser is the mass of the substance required. Less mass occupies less space, which reduces the storage system cost. So salt hydrate PCM with higher latent heat is considered to be very effective for storing thermal energy. In addition, specific heat indicates the amount of thermal energy required to raise the temperature of 1 g of substance by 1°C. Latent heat and specific heat capacity are directly proportional. For TES system, salt hydrate PCM with high latent heat and high specific heat capacity are preferred, as they can store more energy. Factors affecting latent heat are (a) mass of the substance and (b) material morphology.

And it is quite obvious that latent heat values of inorganic salt hydrates are higher than most organic PCM [\[128](#page-41-4)]. Greater the latent heat, the greater is the TES potential. The inclusion of nanoparticles with salt hydrate to improve the thermal property tends to make a negative impact on latent heat and deteriorates due to changes in size and surface characteristics [\[129](#page-41-5)]. It is also noted that supporting material with narrow pores affects the crystalline behaviour of the material and will result in the decline of latent heat as the material will not be able to reach the lowest energy state.

#### Melting point

# Mechanism and factors influencing melting point

The selection of inorganic salt hydrate PCM for any application is based on their melting point. The melting temperature of salt hydrate is recommended to be below the temperature of the heat source and above the temperature of ambient condition for TES. Melting temperature or phase change temperature indicates the critical point during phase transfer from solid to liquid and vice versa. For refrigeration application PCM with 21° C, for air conditioning application 21–28°C, for energy saving in buildings 21–60°C and for water heating application 29–80°C are preferred. The higher melting point of salt hydrate contribute towards a longer melting duration and provides better thermal cycle before complete melting of PCM. The major factors influencing melting point are (a) intermolecular forces (b) molecular bonding and (c) shape and size of molecule.

# Effect on melting point with composite additive

As the PTT of most inorganic salt hydrate ranges between 20°C and 140°C they are widely used in low- and medium-temperature TES system. Due to the variation of thermal conductivity and due to interaction in bonds between support and hydrate salt, there exist a small deviation in the melting point of pure salt and the composite salt hydrate. The inclusion of nano additives into salt hydrate modifies the force of attraction between the molecules and ions of salt hydrate and varies the melting point slightly. It has been noted that additives with high thermal conductivity have a small decreasing tendency of melting point and vice versa. Zhang et al. [[133\]](#page-41-23) mixed EG with aluminium potassium sulphate dodecahydrate K.Al(SO4)2·12H2O and observed melting point of pure alum dive to 87.92°C from 91.11°C, from the DSC curve results, it was ensured that the EG provides heat conduction path in the alum and it decreases the melting point of salt hydrate. Similar results with a decrease in melting point on the inclusion of high thermal conductivity nano additive are seen in [Table](#page-20-0) [2](#page-20-0). As well, nano additives that express a strong affinity with salt hydrates exhibit a minor increasing tendency of melting point. In addition, encapsulation of salt hydrate within a narrow space of supporting additive the melting point of PCM greatly varies. Encapsulation technique and encapsulation material used by Liu et al. [[134](#page-41-24)] improved the PTT of composite PCM by 13.7°C, however, encapsulation research work of Fu et al. [\[65](#page-39-7)] depicts a drop in PTT by 14.09°C. The justification provided for the variation in melting point of salt hydrate due to confinement was the presence of irregular pore configuration of the supporting material and the resistance provided by the capsule. The most attractive results obtained were stability in melting point of CaCl2·6H2O after 1000 thermal cycles without the inclusion of nano additives and thickening agent [[135\]](#page-41-25). [Figure 15](#page-25-0) shows the variation in melting point of salt hydrates due to doping of nano additives, and it clearly depicts variation in melting point by −20% to +20%.

#### Degree of supercooling

# Mechanism and factors influencing super cooling

Supercooling is a phenomenon where the PCM is noted to be in a liquid state even below the fusion temperature. Supercooling effects delay the recovery of the heat energy stored within salt hydrate. Almost all inorganic salt hydrate PCM exhibit supercooling effect than organic PCM. On a note nucleation agents are incorporated with PCM to trigger the crystallization process in salt hydrate and reduce the supercooling effect. In PCM research, better understanding of melting and crystallization processes are important. Crystallization takes place by the following three steps (a) induction duration (b) growth phase of crystal and (c) regrowth of crystal. In the induction phase, the nuclei develop and reach a sufficient size; then nucleation initiates followed by diffusion to the nucleus and gets adsorbed with the surface. Supercooling or sub cooling is an intrinsic property of material; in addition inherent and non-pertinent factors also affect the rate of supercooling. The major factors influencing melting point are (a) cooling rate (b) sample size (c) encapsulation medium (d) chemical purity (e) vibration (f) morphology of material and (g) crystallization temperature. [Figure 16](#page-25-1) points up few key techniques commonly adopted for the elimination of supercooling in salt hydrate PCM.

# Effect on degree of supercooling with composite additive

The technique to reduce supercooling includes both passive and dynamic methods. In passive methods, supercooling is suppressed by homogeneous and heterogeneous nucleation. In heterogeneous nucleation, the suspended particles requires a high melting temperature compared to operating temperature, whereas during homogeneous nucleation thickening agents and surfactants are added. The suspended particles have a propensity to precipitate with a number of thermal cycles, as they prevent precipitation and ensure homogenous distribution. Though nucleating agent reduces supercooling, the level of mitigation varies with respect to different nucleation agents. [Figure 17](#page-26-0) shows salt hydrate and their corresponding nucleating agents used by researchers. Numerous research works to suppress supercooling using nucleating agents and nano additives are summarized in [Table 2](#page-20-0). Keen observation ensures that the effect on the concentration of nucleating agents with salt hydrate to mitigate supercooling is nonlinear. Effective nucleating agent concentration is 1–3% for reducing supercooling. The dynamic method includes crystallization of salt hydrate PCM by induced methods like agitation, supply of shock waves, ultrasonic vibration and manual shaking. Passive methods with nucleation agents are very efficient for incorporating salt hydrate for TES. On treating CaCl2·6H2O, CH3COONa·3H2O and Na2SO4·10H2O salt with diatomite, the degree of supercooling of the pure salt was greater than 10°C, 40°C and 10°C and declined to 5.4°C, 7.8°C and 5.4° C, respectively. The suppression in the degree of supercooling was owing to the fact that the surface of diatomite has better hydrophilicity and tough wettability with hydrate molecules and propelled the contact area between salt hydrate PCM and diatomite, thereby offering more nucleation spots [\[67](#page-39-9)]. Aluminium nitride nanoparticles as nucleation agent and CMC as a thickening agent, abolish the supercooling effect of pure CH3COONa·3H2O [\[136](#page-41-26)].

Supercooling or degree of supercooling is a negative effect that mainly occurs in inorganic salt hydrate PCM. Depending on the concentration of nanoparticles deteriorates the supercooling effect. It is also observed that organic nanoparticles make a remarkable reduction in supercooling of salt hydrate compared to the inorganic nanoparticles. In addition to [Table 2](#page-20-0), [Figure 18](#page-27-0) shows the supercooling variation due to doping of nano additives. Complete suppression of supercooling effect was observed with inclusion of α-Fe2O3; on the contrary; supercooling effect increased by 600% on the dispersion of nano-Cu with salt hydrate PCM as they are the non-nucleating agent.

#### Resistance to corrosion

# Mechanism and factors influencing corrosion

Depending on salt anion, corrosion mechanisms in salts are basically divided into (a) oxyanionic-based salt and (b) halide-based salt [\[137](#page-41-27)]. Similar to aqueous corrosion, corrosion mechanism in both types of salt usually occurs based on acid/base model. Molten salt hydrate carries negatively charged H ions where in aqueous solutions carries positively charged H ions. Carbonate, nitrates and sulphate salts fall under oxyanionic salt, during heating and melting dissociates. The propagation of oxyanionic salt highly depends on environmental conditions. In the case of halide salts like fluoride and chloride, corrosion involves halogenating transitional metals and is less corrosive due to the low activity of fluorine ions. Impurities and moisture content contribute towards corrosion due to the presence of oxide in the salt. The corrosion issues associated with hydrated salt can be mitigated with addition of corrosion inhibitors [\[138,](#page-41-28)[139](#page-41-29)]. Factors that predominantly influence corrosion mechanism are (a) anion and cation of salt (b) temperature (c) pressure (d) thermal cycling rate (e) impurity concentration (f) environmental condition (g) moisture (h) microstructure and (i) composition.

# Effect on corrosion with composite additive

Salt hydrate faces a major concern on affinity to corrode metals that are in contact with and the corroded metal diffuse into storage media of salt hydrates and disrupts the thermal property of salt hydrate also weakens the product. Corrosion involves the oxidation of metals and reduction of protons or oxygen. Protecting metals from corrosion improves their application and economic benefits. Conventional coating using paints, polyvinyl butyral and zinc chromate are familiar techniques followed to protect the metal. The coating layer acts as a barrier between oxygen and water resists the ionic movement between the salt and metal interface [[87\]](#page-39-26). In some cases, coatings and additive doped to resist corrosion in metals with salt hydrate contact tends to create degradation in melting temperature, latent heat and thermal conductivity. The coating layer reduces corrosion, on the contrary, they acts as an insulator and reduces the tendency to transfer heat energy. As well, nucleating agents and additives incorporated into salt hydrate for enhancement of thermal properties have adverse effects on corrosion rate. Na2HPO4·12H2O and Na2- SO4·10H2O were mixed to form a composite PCM to overcome the corrosive nature of salt hydrate [[72\]](#page-39-19). Corrosion inhibitor 2-(2-pentadecyl-4, 5dihydro-1H-imidazol-1-yl) ethanol was added up to an optimal limit of 0.5 mM. It's been observed that the corrosion inhibitor ethanol added to the salt hydrate reduced the mass transportation at the edge of Cu/PCM and reduced the corrosion rate of copper. Using Na2SO4·10H2O as salt hydrate PCM the corrosion nature of different aluminium alloys like Al2024, Al3003, Al6063 and Al1050 were studied [[140\]](#page-41-30). Among the four salts, Al 2024 results in the formation of NaAlCO3·(OH)2 due to the presence of atmospheric air, thereby indicating its non-compatibility for better corrosion resistance. Corrosion studies on salt hydrates like LiNO3·3H2O, CaCl2·6H2O and Zn(NO3)2·6H2O with additives like zinc hydroxyl nitrate, zinc oxide/zinc hydroxyl nitrate and strontium chloride, respectively, were carried out [[141\]](#page-41-31). The salt hydrates were made in contact with stainless steel and aluminium for 6–18 weeks and their resistance property was studied on 6th week, 12th week and 18th week in detail. As aluminium metals undergo pitting with salts of chloride, aluminium metal sample with CaCl2·6H2O and its additive produced bubbles and changed from white cloudy solution to dark bluish over a period of time, and corrosion rate was about 127 mg cm−<sup>2</sup> ·yr−<sup>1</sup> , whereas the same salt hydrate with its additive on stainless steel metal showed a corrosion rate 2.4 mg cm−<sup>2</sup> ·yr−<sup>1</sup> highlighting its application due to corrosion resistance property.

Several research were carried out to study the corrosive nature of pure salt hydrate PCM without the inclusion of additives with metals like stainless steel, aluminium, copper and carbon steel a decade ago. Recent work focuses on corrosion study and characterization of thermal properties of salt hydrate with additives suppressing other major issues like supercooling, phase separation and low thermal conductivity. However, the corrosion studies on salt hydrate with carbon nano additives and polymer additives are yet to explore.

#### Phase separation

# Mechanism and factors influencing phase separation

Phase separation deteriorates the TES potential of salt hydrate PCM due to the separation of salt and water molecules. On the propagation of time and favourable conditions, the salt solution prepared by melting transforms back to pure salt. This occurs when salt and water molecules are uniformly diffused and can reestablish their molecular structure. Due to the density difference between salt and hydrate, the anhydrous salt separates from salt hydrate solution under gravity and settles down. This is the mechanism of phase separation occurring in salt hydrate PCM. The factors that influence phase separation are (a) solubility and (b) temperature of the salt hydrate solution.

# Effect on phase separation with composite additive

Phase separation due to incongruent melting of salt hydrate has a negative impact on its application to store energy. The inclusion of thickening and binding agent with salt hydrates avoids further phase segregation. On the contrary supporting materials incorporated to improve the thermal properties of salt hydrate also contribute towards mitigation of phase separation, due to the excess surface of supporting materials nucleating sites are developed. Supporting material acts as micro container resembling coreshell structure and hydrogels or resins and prevents salt hydrate solution from severe phase separation. Micro containers ensure the needy amount of water molecules around salt to improve uniform mixing. Mainly studied salt hydrate PCM is calcium chloride hexahydrate. With a reiterate number of thermal cycles, CaCl2·6H2O experiences constant phase separation and forms CaCl2·4H2O. Supply of excess water to initiate the formation of the hydrate by increasing solubility and by addition of nucleating agents, phase separation issues in CaCl2·6H2O salt hydrate are diminished until 1000 thermal cycles.

#### Thermal cycling

# Mechanism and factors influencing thermal cycling

In PCM, one thermal cycle refers to absorbing and releasing heat energy by salt hydrate once. Similarly to number of charging and discharging cycles in electrical batteries, number of heat charging and heat discharging cycles are important parameters for PCM. Thermal cycle reliability indicates the performance of inorganic salt hydrate after many thermal cycles. Similar to physical stability, thermal stability of salt hydrates are crucial parameter for better utilization of TES. By measuring the thermal and physical properties of NDPCM before and after many numbers of thermal cycles, the thermal stability can be determined. In order to ensure the economic feasibility of salt hydrate PCM, thermal cycles must be as high as possible without deterioration in the property. Factors influencing thermal cycling are (a) absorption of moisture, (b) improper crystallization, (c) impurities and defects.

# Effect on thermal cycling with composite additive

On application of pure salt as PCM for TES, there is a sharp decrease in heat storage potential of salt hydrate with an increase in a number of thermal cycles. Dropin heat storage potential are predominant due to the following reasons (a) evaporation of water molecules (b) exudation of fluid and (c) phase segregation. However, the above issues are controlled with salt hydrate mixture due to close space structure, as they prevent the direct interaction of hydrated salt with the external environment and avoid overflow of fluid and evaporation of fluid. Thermal cycling consistent with salt hydrate can be improved by reduction of phase separation as both are directly related. Phase separation of salt hydrate is prevented by adopting the microencapsulation and confinement technique. Based on the stabilizing mechanism, followed by various encapsulation techniques, the thermal reliability also changes. Core-shell encapsulation technique of encapsulation is slightly effective than the incorporation of porous supports and 3D network structure, as coreshell technique completely packs the salt hydrate. Encapsulation of Na2S2O3·5H2O [[142](#page-41-32)] using sol–gel slightly reduced the latent heat, in-situ mini emulsion technique of encapsulation adopted for Mg(NO3)2- ·6H2O salt hydrate [[7](#page-37-6)] ensured better thermal reliability after 100 cycles. An elaborated study on thermal cycling of CaCl2·6H2O was performed by 1000 number of cycles[[135](#page-41-25)]. The salt hydrate was studied without the inclusion of nano additives; results ensured variation in latent heat of salt hydrate by 10.4% for 10 thermal cycles, 6.4% for 100 thermal cycles and the attractive part was drop in 0.3% latent heat after 1000 thermal cycles. The results ensure that there is no particular trend or regular change in a thermal property of salt hydrate with an extended number of thermal cycles, however, reasons justifying the variation in latent heat of fusion were improper crystallization of salt hydrate as the testing quantity were taken in milligrams.

## Application of composite additives for TES

With an intense to meet the future energy needs on a global scale, PCM are integrated with thermal systems to enhance TES. Improved TES tends to increase the potential of sustainable energy sources. Thermal energy changes in salt hydrate PCM during the transition of phase provide the potential to integrate salt hydrate PCM with heating and cooling of buildings. In addition to latent heat, the isothermal phase transition characteristic ensures its application in textile clothing's, BTMS and in electronic cooling applications. The thermal cycling ability of salt hydrate PCM offers TES facility in solar thermal applications like solar water heaters and solar air heaters.

#### Thermal conditioning of buildings

Energy demands for building heating and cooling, building construction are swelling every year due to the high usage of energy in developing nations. On an average, about one-third of the energy generated is consumed in the energy sector, and they contribute to 40% of CO2 emissions globally. In order to improve the effective utilization of energy, PCMbased thermal storage systems are employed. Abundant salt hydrates such as chlorides and sulphates [[169](#page-42-15)] are actively used in retaining the thermal performance of building heating and cooling. Application of salt hydrate in buildings for TES is analysed based on the development of materials, feasibility, thermal performance characteristic and net heat transfer capacity. Buildings are integrated with PCM in building sections like walls, floors, windows and roofs, they are also integrated using a portable module and PCM composite boards.

# Wall

Na2SO4·10H2O/expanded vermiculite shape stable PCM was combined with magnesium oxychloride cement (MOC), in three different methods such as multilayer board, pre-coated particles and direct mixing block on a lab scale [[170\]](#page-42-16). Expanded vermiculite (EV) offers elevated heat storage potential to Na2SO4- ·10H2O salt hydrate in terms of heating and cooling. It extends the holding time in MOC with Na2SO4·10H2- O/EV PCM model house by 93 min during heating process and 83 min during the cooling process compared to the ambient condition. In another research work, Na2SO4·10H2O–Na2CO3·10H2O was used as salt hydrates[[66](#page-39-8)]. The salt hydrate PCM was impregnated into EV. The maximum capacity of EV to hold the eutectic salt was 60%. The samples were characterized using TGA and DCS to determine the thermal properties. The performance of the test room was evaluated by placing composite PCM in the form of wall boards. Due to the TES potential of the wall board, the maximum room reduced by 4.8° C. Owing to the impregnation of salt hydrate into EV, the chemical stability of composite increased and provided resistance to phase separation and leakage.

#### Floor

Salt hydrate PCM with PTT of 25–45°C can be actively used for improving the thermal performance of building and to save energy costs. Heat fluctuation in buildings due to solar radiation can be reduced using salt hydrate PCM for TES. CaCl2·6H2O are restricted in real-time applications due to the issue of supercooling and phase separation [[94\]](#page-40-2). Flake graphite is an effective nucleating agent that reduces the supercooling effect; in addition, it also improves the thermal conductivity of the PCM. Along with flake graphite, absorbent polymers are employed to reduce phase separation in salt hydrate PCM. Phase separation and the supercooling issue of Na2SO4·10H2O salt hydrate PCM were suppressed by doping expanded graphite, the thermal characteristics analysis of nanocomposite PCM ensured suppression of supercooling and improved thermal conductivity [[96](#page-40-4)]. Furthermore, the PCM were effectively used in floor heating applications by TES. Magnesium nitrate hexahydrate Mg (NO3)2·6H2O with a latent heat value of 130–150 KJ kg−<sup>1</sup> and low cost are extremely suitable for building heating applications [\[88](#page-39-27)]. A numerical computational simulation model was developed to analyse the heat transfer in building envelope considering CaCl2·6H2O as salt hydrate PCM and graphene with a varying fraction of volume as nano additive [[171\]](#page-42-17).

#### Roof

Fu et al. [[161\]](#page-42-18) prepared a form-stable salt hydrate PCM with non-combustible characteristics to enhance the safety measure while integrating with buildings. Salt hydrate of CaCl2·6H2O (98%) and SrCl2·6H2O (02%) were adsorbed on layers of expanded perlite (EP). It was noted that perlite sheets effectively absorb 55% of salt hydrate to a maximum capacity. Thermal cycling analysis was performed for 1000 cycles to ensure the thermal stability of the composite. PCM boards were fabricated by placing a perlite sheet absorbed with salt hydrate into a bag followed by compression. Later the PCM boards were embedded within the brick and were used with the building roof to maintain the thermal stability of the buildings. The PCM-based roof provided better insulation as their thermal conductivity was lower than pure CaCl2- ·6H2O (98%) and SrCl2·6H2O (02%) salt hydrate PCM. Building energy consumption varies with respect to season; hence to improve the performance of TES in buildings for better energy-saving potential, Ye et al. [[172\]](#page-42-19) experimentally studied the thermal performance of building using PCM. Both eutectic salts obtained were combined with EG to form PCM panels and were experimentally studied by placing the panels in the roof and southern wall. Buildings with PCM panels showed better indoor temperature and reduction in the fluctuation of indoor conditions.

#### Novel smart textiles

Garments are a vital part of all human beings, which are in direct contact with the body. Research hotspot in the area of smart textile technology focuses on developing smart cloth materials for medical, military, aerospace and industrial applications that can provide warmness during winter and coldness during summer with the help of PCMs [\[177](#page-42-24),[178\]](#page-42-25). PCM are formed into microcapsules for its integration with fabrics. The commonly used technologies for incorporating PCM into textile fabrics are (a) fibre technology, (b) coating, (c) lamination, (d) melt spinning, (e) injection moulding technique and (f) foam technique. The major drawback of incorporating salt hydrate with fibres is that the PCM can be washed away during wiping, abrasion and washing of cloths.

Incorporating salt hydrate PCM with fabrics causes three effects, namely, (a) heating effect, (b) cooling effect and (c) thermo regulating effect. Utilization of salt hydrate PCM in heat-protective gloves can avoid burn injuries to the worker [[178\]](#page-42-25). PCM integrated glove will ensure the temperature of glove till the melting point of PCM is reached, as well it also extends the usage time of glove. Due to the non-combustible characteristic, salt hydrate PCM are encapsulated with silicon rubbers. Active thermal insulation effect are provided using PCM in skin wear clothing in addition to the passive effectives, thereby improving comfort. PCM also ensures moisture management and thermal comfort in chemical protective fabrics used in laboratories. Fire fighters are easily exposed to burn injuries as they face high heat flux during operation. The inclusion of PCM in firefighting garments increases the duration required to cause burn accidents [[179](#page-42-27)]. The time taken by the PCM for its complete melting is the extended duration the firefighter gets before the occurrence of burn. The melting point of salt hydrate PCM are preferred based on the heat flux intensity like 50–150°C for low heat flux exposures and 50–200°C for medium and high heat flux exposure. The major discussion is on the optimal mass of salt hydrate PCM that has to be used in the garment to ensure minimal load of the garment for the comfort of fire fighters.

Transfer of heat and moisture in porous textile fabrics is a complex phenomenon. In addition to heat transfer by conduction, convection and radiation transfer of moisture through wicking, evaporation and diffusion also take place. Similarly, for PCM fabrics in addition to latent heat, the thermal performance of clothing is influenced by PCM quantity, method of incorporation and size of micro PCM. Thus, to analyse the overall thermal performance of PCM fabric, a number of indices like thermal regulation capability, thermal psycho sensory intensity (TPI) and static thermal insulation are characterized. For a PCM fabric, TPI expresses the thermal perception by the body and it can be measured using a fabric intelligent hand tester. TPI tends to increase with an increase in PCM level in the textiles [[181\]](#page-42-28). Numerical analysis on garments with a hybrid cooling system integrated with PCM and ventilation fans were carried out by Wan et al. [[180\]](#page-42-29). [Figure 20](#page-32-0) shows the designed garment with a hybrid cooling model and the different layers considered while numerical analysis and human body temperature with respect to PCM materials. Garments with PCM exhibited lower body and core temperature compared to garments with ventilation fans, due to the wide surface spread area, however, the hybrid system were much effective than the individual cooling techniques. The garments also exhibited issues with absorb of heat from the environment and condensation of moisture on the PCM surface. In addition to the above application, PCM has also left footprint in (a) space suits for the thermal comfort of astronauts, (b) sportswear like cycling underwear, snowboard gloves and athletic wears, (c) bedding accessories like pillows and mattresses for thermal comfort, (d) polyethylene glycol treated cloths are used in medical applications, (e) shoes and accessories of militants to overcome cold climate and (f) in automobile seat covers [[182](#page-42-30)].

#### Electronic gadgets

Major issues with electronic gadgets are heat generation and dissipation, as the high temperature of the devices directs to burnout of circuits, failure of integrated circuits and accidents leading to improvised safety systems [[183\]](#page-42-31). However, research's on integrating desirable PCM with electronic equipment's has been the hotspot as they provide better thermal protection and they are a passive cooling technique. Alimohammadi et al. [[184\]](#page-42-32) carried out an experimental analysis to understand the performance of salt hydrate PCM Mn(NO3)2 by doping Fe3O4 as a nano additive. The nanoparticle was effective to decrease the electronic chip temperature to 10.5°C, whereas for pure PCM, it was 14°C. The presence of nano particle increases the thermal conductivity of the composite and increases the heat dissipation rate for effective cooling. The numerical study was carried out using salt hydrate S44 to analyse the thermal performance in a heat sink. In the model generated salt hydrate S44 composite was compared with the lauric acid nanoparticle, as S44 composite transferred high heat per fluid pumping power, it exhibited better thermal performance [[185\]](#page-42-33). It is very keenly noted that due to the corrosive nature of salt hydrate and uncertainty toward long-term reliability, application of salt hydrate PCM for thermal heat dissipation of electronics are less preferred compared to the organic and metal PCM's. Glauber's salt is a common PCM used in heat sinks and micro-channels of electronic devices but are limited to devices where the number of thermal cycling is very less unless it's encapsulated. Further research works on integrating nano additives dispersed salt hydrate PCM are expected to study the effect of corrosion inhibitors with salt hydrate for reliable use of PCM with electronic gadgets. Phase separation has to be completely addressed for real-time application.

## Battery thermal management system

Temperature dependence on battery plays a vital role which decides their performance. Operating battery at lower temperature leads to a decline in several parameters like charge acceptance, power capacity, life span and round-trip efficiency [\[186](#page-43-0),[187\]](#page-43-1). On the other hand, operating battery at a higher temperature contributes to several issues like rapid self-discharge and capacity fade [[188\]](#page-43-2). In order to achieve better performance battery should be operated at an intermediate temperature. Lithium-ion batteries are very sensitive to temperature, thus a suitable BTMS system needs to be implemented to ensure the optimum operating range of electrical battery [\[189\]](#page-43-3). Battery thermal management is carried out by air cooling system [\[190\]](#page-43-4), direct and indirect liquid cooling system [\[191\]](#page-43-5), thermo-electric heating–cooling [\[192](#page-43-6)] and PCM [[193\]](#page-43-7). PCM integrated BTMS system due to its passive energy utilization to cool battery seems to be a smart option. PCM absorbs the heat generated during the charging and discharging process due to its phase changes phenomena, and it prevents further temperature rise naturally. Peak temperature loads of lithiumion batteries are reduced by employing PCMs. Performance of organic PCM like paraffin and fatty acids like capric acid, lauric acid, dimethyl sabacate and vinyl stearate are experimentally studied by various researchers compared to inorganic PCM as they offer outstanding thermal reliability. The low thermal conductivity nature of paraffin was modified by employing carbon fibre additives in different proportions. The experimental analysis also reflects that blending 2 mm long carbon fibre with 0.46 wt-% into PCM can effectively reduce the peak temperature rise by 45%. In addition, the corrosive nature, supercooling characteristic and dehydration are some serious issues, which further makes inorganic PCM less competitive to organic PCM to be applied for thermal management. Thermal runaway is a phenomenon during which an exothermal process prompts the other processes, which subsequently result in an unmanageable rise in temperature. Thermal runaway commonly occurs in batteries and leads to fire in several cases.

Cao et al. [[194\]](#page-43-8) employed expanded graphite doped with SAT to prevent the thermal runaway issue faced by lithium-ion batteries. Due to the potential of SAT to store heat in two stages like (a) latent heat storage up to a temperature of 58°C and (b) thermochemical heat storage up to a temperature of 106–140°C, they predominantly reduce the overheating of the battery and maintains the temperature under control. Lithium-ion BTMS was integrated with EG and organosilicon blended inorganic SAT as PCM [\[195\]](#page-43-9). EG improved the thermal conductivity of PCM and organosilicon ensures proper leakproof for longterm application. As inorganic PCM are highly nonflammable, they provide high safety to battery systems than the organic PCM. Ling et al. [[196\]](#page-43-10) presented an innovative method of heat up lithium-ion battery using CaCl2·6H2O salt hydrate with negligible energy utilization. Heat storage and release from the salt hydrate PCM is initiated using a specially designed triggering device which is based on Gibbs function. The local pressure of PCM was used developed using a stick and spring mechanism in the triggering device. CMC is used as a thickening agent to analyse the stability of supercooling. The effect of supercooling always causes a negative impact on the application of salt hydrates, whereas this particular work has used supercooling of PCM for controlling heat storage and release. Experimental result shows the heating of lithium-ion battery at a rate of 7.5°C min−<sup>1</sup> . Composite PCM with expanded graphite impregnated over the surface of salt hydrate with a mixture of MgCl2- ·6H2O and Mg(NO3)2·6H2O at 41.3:58.7 wt-% were used to perform an elaborative study on electrical cycling and thermal runway penetration of lithiumion batteries [[197\]](#page-43-11). Salt hydrates PCM were filled into the battery pack for enhanced thermal management. A comparative analysis of the system was carried out using organic and inorganic salt mixture shows that inorganic salt hydrates successful restrict thermal runaway propagation in lithium-ion batteries. Ramandi et al. [[198\]](#page-43-12) carried out a numerical study to understand the thermal performance of Na2(SO4- )·10H2O and Zn(NO3)2·6H2O. The analysis model shows effective performance of the battery when double-shell PCM insulations are adopted. Hybridizing PCM salt hydrated (HM-S29), heat pipe and a spray loop, a cooling system for thermal management of LiFePO4 battery was carried out by Lie et al. [\[199\]](#page-43-13). During day time operation of the battery, the heat generated was actively absorbed by salt hydrate for effective functioning. [Figure 21](#page-34-0) shows the application of inorganic salt hydrate PCM, SAT and CaCl2·6H2O along with their additives for proper thermal management of lithium-ion batteries. Safety test evaluations of SAT in the presence of fire shown in [Figure 21\(](#page-34-0)d) ensure its fire resistance property. Due to the nonexplosive and non-flammable nature of salt hydrate, they find themselves to be tested and used in electric vehicles. Organic PCM like paraffin's are most commonly used in BTMS owing to high heat capacity and availability of organic PCM materials with a different melting point. Though inorganic salt hydrate PCM exhibits enhanced safety properties and advanced thermal properties than organic PCM, the supercooling and phase separation nature of salt

hydrate makes it unpopular among researchers for

BTMS application. However, nanoparticle doped salt hydrate with reduced supercooling effects are numerically tested.

#### Solar thermal system

Owing to the fact of inexhaustible and eco-friendly nature of solar power, they are considered to be the most abundantly available renewable energy; however, challenges due to climate, weather and cyclic day and night, uninterrupted functioning of solar appliances are a research hotspot. Solar thermal systems integrated with PCM provide a facility to store thermal energy. Thermal energy stored using salt hydrate PCM during the day time in the presence of sunlight can be utilized during late night or in the absence of solar power without further dependence on electrical energy produced by conventional fossil fuels for heating application [\[200](#page-43-14)]. Extended usage of the solar thermal system during the intermittent period of solar energy by salt hydrate PCM reduces the usage of fossil fuels which, on the contrary, reduces environmental pollution [[5\]](#page-37-4). Application of PCM with the solar thermal system is considered to be a reliable technique to provide the continuous power supply. PCM are very attractively incorporated with a solar thermal system of all operating temperature ranges. The selection of PCM depends on its melting point and latent heat. Micro and nano additive PCM are becoming a significant part in solar thermal appliances. These include the application of salt hydrate with solar domestic and solar industrial systems, which is discussed in this section.

Varol et al. [[201](#page-43-15)] incorporated Na2CO3·10H2O as PCM on solar flat plate collectors and performed both experimental and numerical investigation on its performance. Results ensured enhanced performance of solar water heated with salt hydrate PCM compared to the conventional flat plate collector. Using CaCl2- ·6H2O and Mg(NO3)2·6H2O as PCM experimental comparative analyses on the performance of solar concentric parabolic cooker were studied [[202\]](#page-43-16). A new design was made with the cooker receiver with 25 PCM tubes to fill salt hydrate. Novel research focused on solar thermal power generation with thermal energy storage for the temperature range of 120– 300°C was carried out by Tamme et al. [\[203](#page-43-17)]. Alkali metal nitrate and its mixtures are tested for its thermal performance with the expanded graphite in three different design enclosures (encapsulated metal tubes, expanded graphite heat exchanger tubes, graphite foiled heat exchanger tubes). Using eutectic salt hydrate mixture of NaNO3 and KNO3 in the annular space of the receiver tube of solar parabolic trough collector (PTC), experimental analyses were carried out [\[204](#page-43-18)]. The salt hydrate PCM provide supplementary thermal energy from the PCM materials at the absence of solar radiation. It was observed that the temperature and working fluid and thermal efficiency of solar PTC improved with integrating thermal batteries. From numerous literature searches, it is very clear that integration of the solar thermal system with organic PCM has covered about 90% of the solar domestic systems and eutectic PCM have covered 80% of the industrial system like solar parabolic and solar dish power plants. Solar thermal system requires salt hydrate that is high stable for a minimum of 1000 number of thermal cycle for better operation, hence research on nano additive doped salt hydrate with stability for high number of thermal cycle are highly impactful.

Though the use of solar thermal system enhances environmental protection, the use of PCM with the solar thermal system is not often economically feasible, especially when it comes to the integration of nanoparticle dispersed PCM. The above issues are due to the high price of PCM and nanoparticles. The higher cost of PCM will continue to exist until largescale markets are developed for the preparation of PCM and for hybridization of nanoparticle with PCM on a large scale. In addition to the above, government subsidy could be another effective way to reduce the cost of PCM and its additives for improving TES. Plenty of research work on PCM integrated solar thermal system are carried out, never the less there is still a lack of research work discussing on the economic analysis of nanoparticle dispersed inorganic salt hydrate for the solar thermal system.

In this section, the authors have discussed on the advanced application of nano additive-enhanced salt hydrate PCMs in the field of building heating and cooling, novel textile materials, electronic gadgets, battery thermal management and solar thermal systems. From the above analysis, it has been observed that for thermal management of buildings, the quantity of salt hydrate required is of high due to large volume of space cooling and thus, any salt hydrate PCM with higher latent heat, within melting point temperature range of 28–35°C are highly preferable. Low-cost nano additives that provide better thermal stability and lower degree of super cooling are highly recommended. On an average, the number of thermal cycles that would take place in building units is expected to be 2 cycles per day, hence any salt hydrate with better thermal stability for 1800 cycles is expected to provide better performance for almost 3 years. Whereas salt hydrate PCMs in textile materials need high resistance to solubility, and they are expected not to react with sweat. The need for thermal stability for the nano enhanced salt hydrate PCMs can be rather lower since people use numerous cloths, and the same cloth is expected to be used repeatedly only in rare cases. For electronic application, salt hydrate must not cause corrosion as they would be subjected to direct contact with metal products, the thermal conductivity of the composite PCM used for electronic gadgets are expected to higher, to ensure better cooling of the gadgets. Similarly, BTMS units are one, which are widely explored in recent days, salt hydrates are yet to be explored as for major BTMS units, paraffin and organic PCM are highly preferred due to their well-developed technique. The last application discussed is the solar thermal systems, which make use of medium and higher temperature salt hydrate PCM, as they contribute towards thermal energy storage. Higher the thermal stability nature of nano composite salt hydrate PCM, higher is the application with solar thermal systems.

## Conclusions and future challenges

## Conclusions

Nano additive-based salt hydrates can effectively prevail over the drawback of thermal conductivity, supercooling and phase separation of conventional PCMs, which makes them highly capable for TES applications. The significance of salt hydrates has attracted numerous researches in the recent decade. In this review article, the authors specifically summarized the advances in nano doped salt hydrate, thermal performance and their smart applications. Following are the conclusions and authors' comments on nano additives dispersed salt hydrate PCMs;

- . Regarding doping of nano additive into salt hydrate, mainly three materials are considered (a) metal additive which exists in the form of powder and metal foams; (b) carbon additive which is classified based on their dimension as 0D, 1D, 2D and 3D under which fullerene, CNT/CNF, graphene and graphite nano additives are used; (c) polymer additives. Metal nano composite in the form of metal foam reveals a dominant role in improving the heat conduction rate as well it weakens the super cooling effects of salt hydrate. Graphene, due to its higher thermal conductivity and surface area, makes a notable impact on doping with PCM. Carbon additives are the major nano additives that are doped with most of the salt hydrate PCMs achieving enhanced thermal properties. However, the lack of studies on the hydrophilia nature of multi-dimensional carbon additives with salt hydrate PCMs are not scientifically discussed by various authors.
- . Numerous hybridization techniques exist for the inclusion of metal, polymer and carbon-based nano additives into salt hydrate; however, the highly adoptable technique for dispersion of additive into salt hydrate PCMs are; (a) melt and direct mixing; (b) vacuum impregnation method; (c) direct mixing followed by ultrasonication and magnetic stirring. For preparing solid pills, the sintering process is mostly followed by researchers. All the research works carried out so far in regard to characterization and enhancement of thermophysical properties are only for lab-scale application, and there is a variation in supercooling value as reported by different authors with respect to different binding agents and additives.
- . Very limited research on the influence of morphological structure, size of nano additive, the concentration of nano additive on the thermophysical characteristic of salt hydrate have been performed.
- . Thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCMs was observed to increase by almost 2000% on the dispersion of EG, however, research work by certain authors shows a decrease in thermal conductivity of salt hydrate PCMs with a dispersion of EP. Similarly, latent heat of salt hydrate PCMs dropped by a maximum of 54%, however, majority of nano additives reduced the enthalpy change by 10–20% which was under permissible value. Variation in melting point of salt hydrates were between −20% and +20%. Complete suppression of supercooling

effect was observed with the inclusion of α-Fe2O3 with SAT, but there is a lack of work with the same metal additive doped with other salt hydrates to study the supercooling effect. But supercooling effect increased by 600% on the dispersion of nano-Cu with salt hydrate PCM as they are nonnucleating agents. Most of the studies highlighting the reduction of supercooling in salt hydrate with nucleating agents have limited their testing with less than 50 numbers of thermal cycles, ensuring a lag in the reliability of nucleating agents for longterm application. In addition, the crystal structures are yet to explore in detail.

. An application point of view, nano-salt hydrate PCM is widely used in building heating and cooling application due to the low cost of salt hydrate. Owing to the corrosive nature of salt hydrate, they are yet to be explored with electronic gadgets. Every research work is initiated to transfer the demerits in every theory and application into merits. One such drawback of salt hydrate PCM is the degree of supercooling. Heat storage and release of PCMs are always temperature-dependent. On the contrary, utilization of supercooling effect of salt hydrate PCMs for enhanced thermal management of electrical battery are an outstanding research work which operated by a trigger mechanism to initiate the heat release and storage as per the need.

## Future challenges

Despite several advanced research progress in the synthesis and development of nano additive salt hydrate PCMs, quite a few aspects of future research are highly recommended to pursue the betterment of the research community as given below;

- . Current researches are mainly focused on integrating nano additive with salt hydrate PCM in a laboratory scale and performing characterization testing of their thermal property. However, nano additives PCM are used in building materials for thermal comfort of human beings. In addition, many real-time experimental application-oriented research works are to be carried out to propel the commercialization of salt hydrate PCMs.
- . On the emerging hybridization techniques, nano additives are doped with salt hydrate using techniques like impregnation, direct mixing and ultrasonication. But no hybridization techniques or methods have been reported yet on doping of nano additives with PCM for large-scale application. The major problems associated with such hybridization techniques are (a) non-uniform distribution of additives over PCM; (b) possibility of

- hydrate to get evaporated at atmospheric condition; (c) highly feasible for corrosion reaction due to the atmospheric oxygen content. Considering the above-mentioned factors lab-scale hybridization techniques need to be explored. As per the analysis and literature review, the authors suggest that a direct mixing technique with a stirring process is preferable for large-scale applications. Alongside with fast-growing graphene industries worldwide, salt hydrate PCM with graphene additive is expected to make a remarkable contribution.
- . Though GO nano sheets make a remarkable contribution towards enhancing the thermal property of salt hydrate PCM. GO nano sheets have poor dispersion ability and get easily agglomerated. To overcome agglomeration of nanoparticles, additives like surfactants are added. However, this technique may change the base property of salt hydrate NDPCM and may affect the performance. Thus, there is a need for external mechanism to avoid agglomeration and the poor hydrophilia nature of carbon additives.
- . Dispersion of CNT, SWCNT and MWCNT with salt hydrates are an important aspect for the enhancement of thermal properties of PCM. The hydrophobic characteristic of carbon material makes them less dispersion in salt hydrate and gets entangled with other carbon particles. Thus CNT, SWCNT and MWCNT additives are made hydrophilic by functionalization using macromolecule; however, the studies carried out so far are only with organic PCMs, whereas inorganic PCMs are yet to be discussed.
- . Higher manufacturing costs of nano additives are notable issues that hinder the commercial application of salt hydrate. Economic studies considering the thermal performance, environmental benefits over conventional systems and overall cost of the system with net pay back period would be an insight for attracting high investing stakeholders. So far, no special article provides detailed economic benefits with proper accuracy considering all the operational, economic and environmental parameters.
- . Lack of theoretical modelling on nano structured PCM to yield empirical relations to predict the thermal properties of NDPCM are limited for the combination of only certain nano additives and inorganic salt hydrates. Literature also lags theoretical mechanisms for understanding the enhancement and deterioration of properties like supercooling, thermal conductivity, latent heat and viscosity on doping of nano additives.
- . Salt hydrate PCMs are applied for long-term applications that are justifiable with thermal cycling tests. However, recent studies on thermal cycling performance of nano additive salt hydrate are

- very minimal. Thermal cycling analyses are performed for only few hundreds of cycles to ensure thermal and chemical stability which is very far away from real-time applications. Better thermal stability with a low degree of supercooling and less leakage are required for more exploration.
- . Similar to the trigger mechanism adopted in lithium-ion battery thermal management, a desirable heating control strategy of energy storage and release from salt hydrate PCM can be applied to building application, solar thermal cooking application, smart textiles and electronic gadgets based on Gibbs function, which is discussed.
- . Existing advanced research work ensures confinement of organic PCM for offering enhanced stability over leakage issues and low thermal conductivity; on the other hand, this technique is not adopted for inorganic salt hydrate PCM due to evaporation of water from the pores during phase transition. The evaporation of water affects the reversibility of thermal cycling.
