#### ABSTRACT

This paper thoroughly reviews the development and characterization of carbon-based form stable organic phase change materials (FS-OPCMs) for latent heat storage applications. The O-PCMs such as paraffin, fatty acids, polyethylene glycols (PEGs), etc., suffer from poor thermal conductivity and flow ability in their molten state, which restricts their many applications. Carbon-based materials are seen as a promising and viable way to overcome these challenges. They have a very high thermal conductivity and can hold liquid phase change materials (PCMs) in their pores. This review provides comprehensive coverage of the carbon structures and their classifications, including carbon nanotubes (CNT), carbon nanofiber (CNF), expanded graphite (EG), graphene, graphene oxide (GO), carbonized industrial solid wastes, and other carbon-based materials that can be used as supporting porous materials in developing FS-OPCMs for thermal energy storage (TES) applications. In addition, the thermal and chemical performance of carbon-based FS-OPCMs is extensively investigated and given. The applications of such composites are also discussed and summarized. Finally, the potential of these materials for thermal energy storage is presented. This review provides an in-depth insight into the potential of carbon-based materials for latent heat thermal energy storage (LHTES).

## **1. Introduction**

In recent years, energy storage technologies have gained huge attention worldwide because of the increasing energy shortage, human population, and habitat pollution. Altogether, incorporating energy storage into a system can improve reliability, efficiency, lower environmental footprint, and economic benefits [[1](#page-22-0)]. The most potential method for TES is the utilization of PCMs as latent heat energy. The reason is that PCMs could supply excellent heat storage capacity while keeping the temperature almost unchanged during the phase transition process. This behaviour can be considered isothermal, which is impossible in other heat storage methods. The detailed classification of PCMs is shown in [Fig. 1](#page-1-0).

The PCMs are mainly classified into organic and inorganic. Among organic PCMs, Paraffin and fatty acids are widely used for latent heat storage purposes. Also, organic PCMs have significant advantages over

inorganic ones, particularly the supercooling and corrosion effects are negligible in the case of organic PCMs. However, the poor thermal conductivity of commonly used organic PCMs like PEGs, paraffin wax (PW), fatty acids (lauric acid, palmitic acid, capric acid, etc.) restricts its utilization in many real TES applications [[3](#page-22-0)–8]. Hence, this problem needs to be resolved by adding a significant amount of materials with high thermal conductivity properties. At the same time, it also resolves the problem of leakage of PCMs when it is in the molten state, which is also a major drawback of organic PCMs in LHTES. Many studies [9–[15](#page-22-0)] have already been conducted to improve thermal conductivity and reduce organic PCM leakage. To prevent the leakage of molten PCMs, various techniques like the melt impregnation method [\[16](#page-22-0)–19], vacuum impregnation method [20–[23\]](#page-22-0), hot pressing method [[24\]](#page-23-0), melt adsorption compression method [\[25](#page-23-0)–27], etc. have been employed to prepare the novel form-stable PCMs (FSPCMs). ## **2. Carbon structure and ability to host PCM**

Carbon materials can significantly reduce the poor thermal conductivity of organic PCMs when utilized in the application of latent heat storage. The carbon elements exist in a variety of crystalline forms. It has a broad variety of allotropes, including expanded graphite (EG), carbon nanotubes (CNT), graphene, graphite fibres, amorphous carbon, etc. The TES media include low temperature, medium-temperature, and hightemperature materials. The organic phase change materials, i.e., paraffins, polyethylene glycols (PEGs), fatty acids, etc., are highly suitable for low to medium range thermal energy applications. However, the utilization is limited due to the low thermal conductivity and shape stabilization problem of molten O-PCMs. As a result, carbon-based matrix materials such as EG, CNT, graphene, CNF, GO, graphitic foams, etc., are used to improve the thermal conductivity while also stabilizing the shape of molten O-PCMs. Carbon has various allotropes [[63\]](#page-23-0) due to its distinctive capability to amalgamate and form infinite molecular

organizations, which gives industrial significance, as shown in Fig. 4. Many forms in all the arrangements have exhibited higher thermal conductivity and excellent porosity, making them ideal as a porous matrix or an additional material to alleviate composite conductivity in TES. This section of the review anticipates the essential framework of carbon materials along with its characterization to scrutinize past studies on carbon-based composites in TES. Additionally, a detailed analysis of the utilization of different forms of carbon materials in multiple applications of TES is provided in this review.

Graphene, nanotube, and fullerene have unique characteristics, explicit from graphite, even though they all have similar sp2 nuclear configurations. # *2.2. Classification of carbon-based supporting materials for developing FS-OPCMs*

Graphitic carbons have established extensive usage in the thermal field of composites which is determined by several factors, and the foremost is the aspect ratio: extended narrow particles with a large aspect ratio prevent verge loss more frequently than 3-D symmetrical particles. Hence, low loading is required to get the equivalent increment in thermal conductivity, mainly in a selected direction. The other factor is stability because graphite carbon is a thermodynamically preferred arrangement under normal conditions. The classification of carbonbased materials is shown in [Fig. 7](#page-5-0). Out of all carbon-based materials, the widely used materials are EG, CNT, graphene, and graphite fibres, which are capable of increasing the thermal conductivity of O-PCM with considerable amounts. At the same time, they have outstanding porous structures and have great capacity to hold the molten PCMs during phase change process. Natural graphite and synthetic graphite are not being used directly as porous materials in the formation of FS-OPCMs due to their multilayer and planar chemical structure. The distance between the planes of graphite is very small, so incorporating PCM into it is very difficult. Therefore, graphite is converted into expanded graphite form with chemical treatments to prevent this issue. Graphene is also one of the potential carbon materials for developing FS-OPCMs; it is the single layer graphite that is evolved from the multilayer graphite crystal structure. Carbonized waste materials are also very useful in

preparing the FSPCMs for TES applications. They are inexpensive and environmentally friendly materials as compared to other carbon-based supporting materials.

## **3. CNT based FS-OPCMs**

Nanotubes can be referred to as graphene rolled sheets, forming long cylinders by joining together with edges, creating a 1-D lattice crystal. The carbon nanotubes can comprise of single layer called single-walled nanotube (SWNT) or several multiple layers called multi-wall nanotube (MWNT). For length to 3 mm, the minimum attainable diameter for a single wall CNT is approximately 0.4 nm due to strain restrictions [\[66](#page-23-0)]. Furthermore, CNTs are classified as per their chirality, i.e., the arrangement of carbon atoms. The chirality or arrangement of carbon atoms can be symmetric or non-symmetric, as shown in [Fig. 8,](#page-5-0) which influences their characteristics. A CNT can be constructed by folding up a single graphite sheet such that two hexagonal lattices coincide, which has a particular chirality or arrangement. CNT is commonly synthesized at high temperatures ranging from 600 ◦C to 1000 ◦C. The overall properties of CNTs are critically affected due to the present impurities. Moreover, a wide variety of thermal conductivity reported for CNTs ranges from 1100 to 7000 W/mK [\[67](#page-23-0)].

The various works on carbon nanotube (CNT) based FS-OPCMs are available in the research domain. This section of the paper reviews the development and study of thermal properties of CNT based FS-OPCMs, which gives comprehensive knowledge about the absorption capacity of CNT and its contribution to enhancing the thermal conductivity of base O-PCMs. Liu et al. [[69](#page-23-0)] investigated the ternary system consisting of capric acid (CA), CNTs, diatomite (DT) and in order to prepare an FS-OPCMs for thermal heat energy storage by impregnation technique. The DT was taken as the supporting porous material that retains molten CA in its porous structure and avoids leakage. It was found that the 54% loading of CA can be obtained in the CA/DT composite successfully. Moreover, CNTs were infused into the prepared FSPCM, which enhances the thermal conductivity of CA/DT binary system. The thermal properties were measured using TGA and DSC analysis. The DSC results show that the fusion latent of FSPCM was 79.09 J/g and 31.38 ◦C liquefying temperature. By adding 7 wt % of CNTs, the conductivity of the ternary system enhanced from 0.15 to 0.48 W/mK, which is 1.56 times that of CA/DT. As a result, CNT's contribution is restricted to boosting thermal conductivity at the expense of latent heat reduction, whereas DT was used as a support material to alleviate the leaking problem. Yang et al.

[[70\]](#page-23-0) fabricated the FS-OPCM composed of stearic acid as organic PCM and carbon nanotubes as porous media and thermal conductivity enhancer. The FTIR and SEM results showed that the material has good morphology and microstructure characteristics. Thermal cycle tests revealed that the produced composite's thermal stability is likewise good. With contents of CNTs as 2 wt%, 6 wt%, and 10 wt%, the thermal conductivity was boosted by 61.5%, 92.3%, and 119.2%, respectively. The DSC results also revealed that the phase transition temperature changes minutely, whereas the heat of phase change decreases as CNT concentration increases, which is attributable to variations in the specific heats of CNT and PCM. Hence, the prepared materials can be significantly utilized in thermal management. Wang et al. [[71\]](#page-23-0) prepared the different FS-OPCMs, which are PA/Oleylamine (G18)-CNT, octanol (G8)-CNT/PA Oleylamine, Paraffin/G8-CNT, and Paraffin/G18-CNT. The experimental result shows that the developed composites are thermally stable even after 80 melt/freeze cycles. The heat capacity of paraffin/G18-CNT in solid-liquid phase change was larger in comparison to paraffin wax. The PA-based FSPCMs have larger thermal conductivity than that of pure PA. Meng et al. [[72\]](#page-23-0) prepared the various FSPCMs with fatty acids and CNTs by infiltration process. FS-OPCMs comprise eutectics of CA-PA-LA as O-PCMs and multiwalled CNTs as a porous matrix material. The SEM images of FSPCMs indicated that the ternary eutectics were effectively adsorbed into the CNTs' porous matrix. Furthermore, the DSC result revealed that the prepared FSPCM exhibits excellent phase change characteristics, and the sample latent heat during melting is recorded as 101.6 J/g. Additionally, the composite thermal conductivity substantially increased to 0.6661 W/mK (around 400% increment) with the addition of CNTs, having excellent thermal conductive characteristics. Feng et al. [\[73](#page-23-0)] prepared the FS-OPCM consisting of CNTs as shape stabilizers and LA as O-PCM. The characterization of prepared material has been done in order to identify its structural feature and thermal properties. The DSC results indicated that the melting temperature and melting heat in the process were lesser than bulk material. Also, both reduce as CNT diameters and filling ratios of LA decrease. It has also been observed that the LA/CNTs thermal conductivity was four times more than that of LA. The analysis will comprehend the design of FS-OPCMs with better thermal parameters by selecting the compatible porous media and manipulating their pore design. Lu et al. [\[74](#page-23-0)] prepared a paraffin wax/carbon nanotube sponge (PW@CNS) composite with simultaneous high-efficient thermal management and electromagnetic interference shielding functions via the simple vacuum impregnation method. The PW component functions as the PCM for thermal management applications. In contrast, the three-dimensional electrically conductive CNS component forms the matrix supporting the PW and a shielding structure against electromagnetic waves. The properties of PW@CNS are characterized by FT-IR, XRD, DSC and TGA. Its latent heat and relative enthalpy efficiency during melting and freezing process are as high as 248.8 J/g and 247.7 J/g, and 96.2% and 96.3%, respectively. The latent heat after 100 thermal cycles remained nearly constant, and the prepared PW@CNS exhibited excellent thermal management behaviour and electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding effectiveness (SE). The results indicate that this PW@CNS has great potential for electronic devices. The preparation method, FTIR, XRD, TGA, and thermal conductivity variations of the developed composite are presented in [Fig. 9](#page-6-0)(a–h).

Liu et al. [[75\]](#page-23-0) prepared novel FS-OPCM comprised of CA, calcination iron tailings (CIT), and CNT by simple impregnation method, and various tests have been performed to analyze their properties. The leakage test indicated that CIT could hold CA about 20 wt% without experiencing any leakage of molten PCM. In addition, the SEM technique was used to explore the morphology, the FTIR technique was used to confirm the chemical stability, and the DSC and TGA techniques were used to investigate the composite thermal properties and stability. As a result, the phase change temperature was noted as 29.70 ◦C, and latent energy was observed as 22.69 J/g, with 5 wt% of CNT. Additionally, the time taken to melt and solidify CA/CNT/CIT FS-OPCM is composed of 5 wt% CNT decreased by 42.86% and 54.55%, respectively, compared to CA, and they give better heat exchange capacity. The preparation method and leakage test results obtained at 60 ◦C of novel composite are shown in [Fig. 10](#page-7-0)(a and b). Xiaobin et al. [\[76](#page-23-0)] identified the maximum O-PCM incorporation in composite of PEG/halloysite nanotube/graphene. He developed and investigated a series of halloysite nanotube-based FS-OPCMs with different mass fractions of PEG. The SEM pictures Fig. [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950061822002422) **(c**–**f)** indicated that the O-PCM was properly mixed with a porous matrix. The maximum PCM loading in the prepared FSPCM is 55 wt% without any leakage during the phase transition process.

Sarı et al. [\[77](#page-23-0)] developed and investigated the FSPCM composed of CA-PA eutectics and silica fume as porous structures. The thermal, chemical and morphological characterizations were performed using FTIR, XRD, SEM, and DSC analysis. The results of DSC analysis indicated that FS-OPCM has phase transition temperatures of 19 ◦C–26 ◦C and a large energy capacity of 46 J/g – 49 J/g. They indicated that the developed material could be considered a potential composite for LHTES in the buildings. The FS-OPCM indicated good thermal energy storage reliability and chemical compatibility and produced outstanding

durability till 140 ◦C. CNTs adding process results in a significant improvement in thermal conductivity. Consequently, the FSPCM with 5 wt% CNTs is considered a very promising material for building envelopes. Chen et al. [\[78](#page-23-0)] reported the findings of a composite composed of paraffin as O-PCM and CNT sponge as porous media and thermally conductive filler in the phase transition process. The result showed that the prepared composite had alleviated thermal conductivity and improved latent heat energy compared to pure paraffin. So practically, this composite can store sunlight energy or be used in lithium batteries. Sari et al. [\[79](#page-23-0)] prepared a composite with PEG600 as O-PCM and impregnated in raw diatomite (RD) and CNTs porous structure. Without revealing melt seepage, PEG was successfully confined in porous structures of RD and CNT. The FTIR and SEM technique was used to identify the chemical and morphological studies, respectively, of prepared FS-OPCM. The DSC technique indicated that the FS-OPCM has melting temperatures are 7◦C-8◦C and melt heat energy between 53.8 J/g and 62.9 J/g. Moreover, compared to the RD/PEG composite, the conductivity of FSPCM composite was improved between 73% and 93%. Consequently, the loading of CNTs into RD/PEG composite gives advantageous outputs like enhanced impregnation ratio without any leakage, decreasing heat energy absorbing/releasing time depending on improved thermal conductivity, without hampering thermal durability and chemical stability. Because of these advantages, the FS-OPCM may be combined with simple structural elements to create various building

segments that absorb and release solar energy. Karaipekli et al. [\[80](#page-23-0)] focused his study on the preparation and characterization of FSPCM composed of paraffin and expanded perlite. To promote the thermal conductivity of novel composite, CNT is one of the best materials being

[[81\]](#page-24-0) conducted an experimental study on CNT and carbon nanofiber (CNF) as porous supporting materials and paraffin wax as O-PCM filled phase change materials to boost their conductivities. ## **4. EG based FS-OPCMs**

Graphite is the form of carbon, which is black and soft in nature and has planar and multi-layered structure. The carbon molecules arranged in each layer are systematically in the form of a honeycomb lattice. The lattice planes are placed at 0.335 nm distance from each other. The graphite structure emerges with two different arrangements, hexagonal (alpha) and rhombohedral (beta). The shape of alpha form could be buckled or flat. With mechanical treatment, alpha and beta can be converted with each other. Due to the very small distance between the planes of graphite, it is difficult to incorporate the PCMs into the layer of graphite during the development of FSPCMs. This difficulty can be prevented by preparing EG through the addition of graphite flakes into acid mixtures (H2SO4, HNO3, CH3COOH, etc.) and oxidants (H2O2, KMNO4, etc.) and heating it at 45 ◦C and for half an hour accompanied by cleaning, drying and increasing the temperature at 950 ◦C for 25 s [[93\]](#page-24-0). EG has been proposed as a porous supporting media for FS-OPCMs, including organic PCMs such as fatty acids, paraffins, and other organic PCMs, to avoid molten PCM leakage during the phase transition [[94,95](#page-24-0)]. Various groups have included EG in fatty acids such as MA [\[95](#page-24-0)], SA [\[96](#page-24-0)], LA [[97\]](#page-24-0), and PA [\[98](#page-24-0)] and acquired FSPCMs with higher thermal parameters. A small amount of EG remarkably decreases the leakage problem in the molten state and regulates the relative volume modification of phase change materials depending on eutectic fatty acid mixtures throughout the phase change process [\[99\]](#page-24-0). [Fig. 11](#page-9-0) (a) shows the EG particles, which are thermally shocked and liquid intercalated. The microstructure of the EG molecules does not depend on the technique used but rather identified by the construction of the pure graphite [[100](#page-24-0)]. The material is having a considerable porous percentage and can have direct impregnation with TES materials. SEM results indicate EG's morphologies and the FSPCM developed from CA-OA-LA and EG as represented in [Fig. 11](#page-9-0)(b–g). It is obtained that the EG has the worm-type microstructure, as shown in [Fig. 11](#page-9-0) (b). Additionally, various pores of the interconnected web-like construction at the microscale level have been noticed, as shown in [Fig. 11](#page-9-0) (c). This figure also shows the smooth layer-like feature at the edge of EG. When it is impregnated into the pores of raw EG, [Fig. 11](#page-9-0)(d–g) shows the impregnation of CA-OA-LA into the micropores of EG. [Fig.11](#page-9-0) (h) and [Fig.11](#page-9-0) (i) represents the DSC results and effect of addition of EG to enhance the thermal conductivity of prepared samples of novel composite respectively.

A substantial number of research articles have been published on EG based FS-OPCMs, which indicates that it has great potential to stabilize the shape of organic PCMs as well as to enhance the thermal conductivity characteristics. Tang et al. [[101](#page-24-0)] investigated the FS-OPCMs consisting of Stearyl alcohol (SAL) as PCM and high-density polyethylene (HDPE) and EG mixture as supporting material. Further addition of EG is done, which improves the heat exchange property of the composite. In the composites, HDPE contributed to leakage prevention of SAL, but EG acts as both supporting material and thermal conductivity booster. In DSC analysis, almost constant melt temperatures of FS-OPCM around 57 ◦C and higher latent heat of around 200 kJ/kg were recorded. It was found that the leakage rate of the SAL phase change materials decreased when EG was introduced to the FSPCM. Furthermore, the conductivity of FS-OPCM with 3% EG improves from 0.1966 W/mK to 0.6698 W/mK. Finally, the FS-OPCM with EG could possess substantial TES capability and sizeable thermal conductivity for latent heat storage. Karaipekli et al. [[102](#page-24-0)] evaluated the effect of EG and CF as thermal conductivity promoters of stearic acid as an O-PCM. The thermal conductivity of stearic acid (0.30 W/mK) was enhanced by 266.6% by introducing 10%wt of EG. Moreover, DSC technique determined the latent heat capacities of the SA-EG and SA-CF (90-10 wt%) composite PCM and compared with individual SA. Based on the results, it was found that EG use is a considerable method to enhance the conductivity of SA without affecting much in their latent energy storage capacity. The experimental setup used for thermal conductivity measurements and the changes in thermal conductivities with EG addition are shown in [Fig. 12](#page-10-0)  (a and b). Tian et al. #### **8. Applications**

The carbon-based FS-OPCMs are potential candidates for latent thermal energy storage due to their several benefits and unique characteristics. They have the capability to absorb the PCMs and hold it in their pores during solid to liquid phase change processes due to their excellent porous structure. Besides this, the carbon supporting materials have been considered high thermal conductive elements, which helps the O-PCMs improve their conductivity and improve heat transfer capability in the phase transition process. In this review section, some applications based on carbon supporting materials with O-PCMs candidates have been discussed thoroughly [\[105\]](#page-24-0). The heat storage properties of paraffin and paraffin/expanded graphite as FS-OPCMs in a latent TES system were explored. Additionally, the energy storage performance was analysed, and the optimum amount of expanded graphite in the FSPCM for the latent TES system was regulated based on energy storage capability and heat transfer performance. The schematic of the latent TES system is represented in [Fig. 18](#page-19-0) (a). An electric heater and cold-water tank were utilized for heating during the storage and cooling during the heat extraction, respectively. The heat storage element depicted in [Fig. 18](#page-19-0) b is made up of a vertical shell and tube heat exchanger with fluid flowing in a copper tube with an outside diameter of 18 mm and a thickness of 1 mm, and the PCM surrounded in the space between the stainless steel tube and the shell with an inside diameter of 120 mm and a thickness of 8 mm. In addition, to prevent heat loss, the shell's outer surface is thermally insulated. Four K-kind of thermocouples were used to determine the PCM temperature and were installed close to the shell's inner surface, as shown in [Fig. 18](#page-19-0) (b).

Goli et al. [[205](#page-26-0)] present another application of carbon based FS-OPCM in which the reliability and thermal management of Li-ion battery can be efficiently improved by incorporating the PCMs with graphene as a high thermal conductive filler. These batteries are critical devices for advancement in transport field technologies and mobile communications. The main drawback of Li-ion batteries is that they suffer from high auto heating issues, which restricts their durability and creates environmental and reliability issues. As PCM has the quality to store the energy with almost negligible temperature change, the conventional energy system of batteries system relies on this latent heat only, which reduces the temperature hike in the battery. The graphene incorporation in the whole system is beneficial to increasing the heat transfer rate. ## **9. Future prospects and challenges**

However, despite having various advancements in development, thermal conductivity improvement, and applications of FS-OPCMs in recent years, numerous issues require further investigations:

- 1. It has been seen in many studies that although carbon-based porous materials enhance the thermal conductivity significantly at the cost of latent heat. It requires further technical investigation.
- 2. The latent heat of developed O-PCM composites is usually lower than the pure O-PCMs due to inactive additives, so the optimum designs of carbon-based FS-OPCMs can enhance this latent heat capability.
- 3. New cost-effective techniques are required to prepare the carbonbased FS-OPCMs with good thermal, chemical stability, and energy storage capability.
- 4. 3-D carbon porous materials indicated a larger potential for upgrading the thermal conductivity and form stability as compared to 1-D and 2-D carbon materials. Particularly, developing an extensive, oriented network of heat transfer inside the 1-D and 2-D based composite can be a potential way to improve thermal conductivity along with maintaining the energy storage density.
- 5. The research works on the application-based carbon porous matrix are very few in the research domain. So, more research on the

- application of carbon-based FS-OPCMs is recommended for the future.
- 6. Because there is a lack of study on the combined effects of two or more carbon-based supporting materials in FS-OPCMs, this field has to be further investigated.

Even though the carbon materials are most versatile in energy storage applications, some applications still encounter challenging issues when incorporating them into the system. One of the critical issues is to transfer the basic knowledge to technological applications during the incorporation of carbon materials into the components to design functional or structural materials. Although carbon-based functional materials have been extensively explored for energy storage, synthetic strategies for achieving better collaborations are still in their infancy. Many challenging aspects, such as the development of novel carbon materials with distinct textures and structural properties, as well as the alteration and understanding of surface characteristics, will be present in the future to ensure effective research in carbon-based materials and the development of new applications. Research work on carbon-based components is a very strong and expanding subject of study with limitless possibilities. We still have abundant problems to be understood the thermal, chemical, and structural properties of carbon-based materials. Evidently, the situation inspires the researchers to work on this astonishing group of materials.

#### **10. Conclusions**

Substantial utilization of organic PCMs can assist in creating the proper balance between energy supply and demand. However, the common restriction of O-PCMs, such as seepage of liquid PCM and lower thermal conductivity, hamper their extensive applications in LHTES. Hence, exceptional attempts have been devoted to preparing the FS-OPCMs consisting of a supporting matrix that prevents both restriction of low thermal conductivity as well as problem of leakage. In this study, we came up with a comprehensive outline of carbon structure, carbonbased supporting materials, development of carbon-based FS-OPCMs, thermal conductivity enhancement of developed carbon-based FS-OPCMs and reviewed the applications based on the developed carbonbased composites for thermal energy storage management. Based on this comprehensive review following conclusions can be drawn:

• The carbon-based FS-OPCMs exhibit prominent benefits compared to O-PCMs such as high thermal conductivity, form stability, and tremendous thermal and chemical stability. In addition, the carbonbased supporting materials have excellent porous characteristics, which can be seen in SEM images, indicating that they have excellent morphology structures.

- • The carbon structure indicates that there are various allotropes of carbon-based materials that can be utilized as supporting porous material in FS-OPCMs because of their excellent morphological structures and higher thermal conductivity and those are classified as 1-D, 2D and 3-D carbon porous structures.
- The most commonly used carbon-based supporting porous structures are CNT, EG, graphene, GO, etc. These materials are suitable as additives in FS-OPCMs due to their excellent thermal stability and low density as compared to other metal-based supporting materials. In addition, it has also been observed that the high dimensional carbon porous such as EG, graphene aerogel, graphite foam, etc., provides superior thermal conductivity and allows higher incorporation of O-PCMs.
- CNF/CNT based FS-OPCMs have been reviewed, SWNT and MWNT carbon nanofibers are commonly used porous matrix which is being used to prepare the novel FS-OPCMs. It is concluded that CNT is the potential carbon material for preparing O-PCM composites for LHTES applications because it has an excellent porous structure to hold the molten O-PCMs without any leakage. Additionally, they also act as a good thermal conductivity enhancer to increase the poor thermal conductivity of O-PCMs. As per the data available in this work, CNF can enhance the thermal conductivity of various O-PCMs like paraffin wax, fatty acids, etc., by 137%–4360%.
- EG is 3-D carbon porous structure which is having excellent morphology. They can incorporate more O-PCMs in their pores as compared to carbon nanofibers in the development of FS-OPCMs for thermal storage applications. They are also capable of raising the thermal conductivity of O-PCMs up to 18000%
- Carbon-based materials like graphene, reduced graphene oxide, graphene nanoplatelets, etc., are utilized widely as a supporting material to prepare the O-PCM composite. The characterization results show excellent thermal and chemical stability with outstanding morphology structure. Moreover, they have the capability of enhancing the rate of heat transfer by enhancing the thermal conductivity of developed O-PCM composites up to 18000%.
- Carbonized industrial waste materials such as biological porous carbon, raw fly ash, carbonized paper straw, carbonized sunflower straw, etc., have a good porous structure with the capability to improve the heat transfer rate during the phase transition process. The main advantages of utilizing these materials as porous matrices in the FS-OPCMs are low cost and easy availability of solid wastes.
- The other forms of commonly used carbon porous materials in developing FS-OPCMs are carbon foam, carbon aerogel, graphite foam, carbon sponge, etc. Carbon aerogel is found to be superior supporting material because graphite foam and graphite fiber can enhance the thermal conductivity up to 52000% in paraffin wax composite.
- However, the studies based on carbon-based FS-OPCMs applications are limited, although this paper includes a few applications based on carbon-based FS-OPCMs.
- In comparison to the ceramic materials, carbon-based porous materials offer numerous advantages such high porosity, low corrosion, highly economical and easy to be modified.
- Overall, the carbon-based supporting materials are the promising materials in the development of FS-OPCMs for TES systems because they have excellent capability to hold the molten O-PCMs as well as they can improve the heat transfer rate during the melting and solidifying process by increasing the thermal conductivity of composites.
