## **Correlation study of thermal charging, discharging, and efficiency of graphene oxide-reinforced paraffin wax for thermal energy storage system**

Amit Kumar, Gaurav Bharadwaj, Aayush Gupta , and Kamal Sharma

#### **ABSTRACT**

Nanocomposites, produced through mechanical blending, serve to enhance thermal performance of heat exchangers, solar collectors, and photovoltaic-thermal systems. This work employs an experimental methodology to assess the thermal charging (TC) and thermal discharging (TDC) of a composite generated by mixing graphene oxide (GO) and paraffin wax. TC and TDC of the GO-reinforced paraffin wax-based phase change material (PCM) have been examined across different flow rates (0.375, 0.75, and 1.5 L/min) and concentrations of GO (ranging from 0.25 to 1.00 vol%). The results exhibit a direct correlation between TC, TDC and efficiency, and reaction parameters (flow rate and volume concentration of GO). The experimental and predicted values of TCs, TDCs, and efficiencies fell ±15% (for TC and TDC), and ±9% for thermal efficiency, demonstrating a satisfactory level of agreement. This holds a practical application and valuable predictive tool for estimating TC, TDC, and thermal efficiency on known flow rates and concentrations of GO in wax-based PCMs.

#### **KEYWORDS**

Correlation study; graphene oxide; paraffin wax; thermal charging; thermal discharging

## **1. Introduction**

In a world increasingly reliant on intermittent renewables, thermal energy storage (TES) systems are vital for effective management of energy resources, enhancing reliability, and decreasing dependence on fossil fuels (Guangul and Chala [2019)](#page-6-0). TES comprises a variety of methods, including temperatures adjustment for sensible heat storage, phase shifts in latent heat storage, reversible chemical reactions in thermochemical storage, and heat pump systems for effective heat extraction (Gonzalez-Nino et al. [2018](#page-6-0); Pisani [2020;](#page-6-0) Seddegh et al. [2018](#page-6-0)). The application of PCMs (e.g., paraffin wax and fatty acids, etc.) in TES systems enhances the effectiveness of energy storage and release, resulting in increased energy efficiency and reduced reliance on conventional sources. This is especially advantageous for tasks like regulating building climate and incorporating renewable energy (Javadi et al. [2020;](#page-6-0) Jiang et al. [2019;](#page-6-0) B. Xu et al. [2015)](#page-7-0). The integration of PCMs with GO forms a composite with enhanced thermal properties and great potential for various industries contributing to a more sustainable and energy-efficient future (Wang et al. [2014;](#page-6-0) Zeng et al. [2012](#page-7-0)). Jiang et al. ([2023](#page-6-0)), Liu et al. ([2016,](#page-6-0) [2020](#page-6-0)), and Liu and Yang [(2017)](#page-6-0) investigated the hydrate salt-based PCMs for thermal applications which were modified by using TiO2 and GO to enhance its chemical and thermal stability in TES systems. While Kalidasan et al. ([2022,](#page-6-0) [2023](#page-6-0)) reported the effect of graphene on chemical, thermal, and optical properties of two different types of PCMs (i.e., organic paraffin and eutectic inorganic salt hydrate) for TESS. Conducting a meticulous correlation study of TES systems is crucial for validating models and ensuring efficient energy capture and release. Brousseau and Lacroix ([1998)](#page-6-0) introduced a computational model predicting the thermal behavior of multi-layer latent heat storage units, unveiling intricate isothermal distributions, and highlighting crucial factors impacting heat load and energy storage. Motahar et al. ([2017)](#page-6-0) discussed the solidification process of n-octadecane with different TiO2 concentrations, underscoring their viability as TES

mediums for enhancing building comfort. Guo and Zhang ([2008)](#page-6-0) proposed a mathematical approach to measure the effectiveness of Al foils to enhance heat transfer and discharging processes in high-temperature latent heat thermal energy storage systems (LHTES). A novel correlation has also been reported for quick and accurate simulation of discharging time in plate-based LHTES systems, addressing a key challenge in their commercial deployment and design complexity (Diarce et al. [2018)](#page-6-0). Also, a novel dimensionless total melting time concept for LHTES units has been proposed, with a correlation formula that accounts for various parameters and natural convection effects, demonstrating lowerorder nonlinearity compared to existing methods (Y. Xu et al. [2019)](#page-7-0). Mekrisuh et al. ([2020)](#page-6-0) examined flow and geometric factors influencing total melting time in a concentric double-pipe TES system using PCMs, developing a comprehensive correlation with ±15% accuracy. Gautam and Saini ([2021)](#page-6-0) established correlations for Nusselt number and friction factor in a spherical-shaped packing element-based packed bed storage system for low-temperature solar thermal energy applications, offering predictive accuracy of around 6.7–7.5%.

Till date, several articles have addressed TC, TDC, and overall efficiency in TES systems, often using different materials to improve the properties of paraffin wax (Jiang et al. [2023;](#page-6-0) Kalidasan et al. [2022,](#page-6-0) [2023;](#page-6-0) Liu et al. [2016,](#page-6-0) [2020](#page-6-0)). But the mathematical correlation between thermal performance (TC, TDC, and overall efficiency) and process parameters of wax-based PCMs have not been reported yet. Therefore, the manuscript describes the step wise correlation study as an effect of flow rate (heat transfer fluid [HTF]) and GO concentration in paraffin wax on the TC, TDC, and thermal efficiency.

#### *2.2. Experimental setup and calculations*

The thermal study was conducted with an experimental setup as depicted in Figure 1. The setup included a storage cylinder (as heat exchanger) filled with prepared PCM-based composites, facilitating two distinct heat cycles: TC and TDC. In the charging (TC) cycle, water served as the HTF, heated to 85 �C through electric heaters, and circulated through heat exchanger to transfer heat from HTF to composite samples. The successful melting of paraffin wax, at 65 �C, confirmed the completion of charging cycle. The discharging (TDC) cycle involved cold HTF to extract heat energy from the heat exchanger. The cycle was concluded after achieving 35 �C temperature of heat exchanger. Assumptions for analysis included incompressible, steady, and laminar fluid flow, negligible heat losses, constant thermal resistance of PCM cylinder wall, and temperature-independent properties of PCM and HTF. All the temperature sensors, shown in [Figure 1](#page-2-0) provided the data with *<*3.0% of error and the time measurements were recorded with the help of stopwatch. #### **4. Conclusion**

The correlations between thermal performance (charging time, discharging time, and thermal efficiency) and process parameters (concentrations of GO in composites; 0.25–1.00 vol%, and mass flow rates; 0.375–1.5 L/min) have successfully been established.

- � For charging of TES system, a regression analysis was conducted to derive two correlations. [(i)](#page-3-0) linear relationship between 'tc and flow rate', and [(ii)](#page-3-0) 'tc' can be expressed as a polynomial function of flow rate and GO concentration, both. Following the similar approach, the td can be expressed with the help of polynomial function (order ¼ 2) in terms of flow rate and GO concentrations.
- � For thermal efficiency, a polynomial equation relating it to GO concentration was established followed by a regression analysis incorporating a linear term for flow rate alongside GO concentration.
- � Experimental and predicted values of TCs, TDCs, and efficiencies fell within ±15% for charging and discharging times, and within ±9% for thermal efficiency, demonstrating a satisfactory level of agreement.
- � These correlations hold significance for practical applications within the specified range of concentrations (0.25–1.00 vol%) of GO in paraffin wax-based composite. They offer a valuable predictive tool for estimating charging time, discharging time, and thermal efficiency based

- on known mass flow rates and volume concentrations.
- � The study's meticulous step-wise methodology ensured robustness in the development of these correlations, providing a reliable framework for further investigations and real-world applications in systems involving similar parameters.
