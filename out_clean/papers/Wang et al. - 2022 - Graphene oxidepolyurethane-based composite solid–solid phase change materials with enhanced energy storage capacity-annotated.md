#### RESEARCH ARTICLE

# Graphene oxide/polyurethane-based composite solid–solid phase change materials with enhanced energy storage capacity and photothermal performance

#### Correspondence

Zihua Wu, School of Energy and Materials, Shanghai Polytechnic University, Shanghai 201209, China. Email: [wuzihua@sspu.edu.cn](mailto:wuzihua@sspu.edu.cn)

#### Summary

The preparation of phase change materials (PCMs) with high energy storage, thermal conductivity, and photothermal conversion capability is essential for improving solar energy conversion and storage. In this study, graphene oxide (GO) was incorporated into polyurethane (PU) prepared from 4,4<sup>0</sup> diphenylmethane diissyanate (MDI) and polyethylene glycol (PEG). PU/GO composite PCMs exhibit enhanced thermal conductivity (0.972Wm-<sup>1</sup> K-1 ) because the isocyanate in MDI can covalently graft with GO. The composite solid–solid PCMs has outstanding stability and thermal reliability. It can stay stable at 80C for more than 15 min without leaking and has good thermoregulation regulation performance. The thermal energy storage density and photothermal conversion efficiency of PU/GO increase as the amount of PEG and GO increases. The PU/GO composite with 89 wt% PEG and 1.72 wt% GO has a phase change enthalpy of 150.7 J/g and a high photothermal conversion efficiency (95.3%). The composite PU/GO solid–solid PCMs have great potentials for effective energy storage and solar energy utilization.

#### KEYWORDS

different soft segment content, energy storage capacity, photothermal conversion performance, solid–solid phase change materials, thermal conductivity

# 1 | INTRODUCTION

Excessive energy consumption in human production activities has accelerated the emission of greenhouse gases and the depletion of fossil fuels, which may eventually lead to serious ecological problems. Solar energy, the one of the most abundant and cleanest renewable energy sources, attracted extensive attention.[1,2](#page-11-0) Photothermal conversion is an important method for capturing, converting, and storing solar energy,[3](#page-11-0) and its ability is affected by the thermophysical properties of photon capture and heat storage materials.[4,5](#page-11-0) Photothermal conversion is an effective way to utilize the solar energy, but it is also severely restricted by intermittent solar radiation.[2](#page-11-0) The latent heat of the phase change materials (PCMs) could collect and store the heat energy which is converted by solar energy and the heat energy conversion of PCMs is easy to operate and has great economic feasibility.

A facile strategy to fabricate PCMs with enhanced photothermal performance is to incorporate light-absorbing fillers to the PCMs system.[21](#page-11-0)

Metals and metal oxides such as silver,[22](#page-11-0) titanium black,[23](#page-11-0) iron oxide,[24](#page-11-0) and cobalt oxide[25](#page-11-0) can be used as fillers because of their effective light-absorbing ability and high thermal conductivity. Zhang et al[26](#page-11-0) prepared an efficient silver nanoparticles-graphene nanosheets/ polyethylene glycol(Ag-GNS/PEG) composite. Ag has high visible light selective absorption and infrared reflection based on the local surface plasmon resonance effect, which can enhance the light absorption capacity of Ag-GNS and reduce thermal radiation. The silver nanoparticles and GNS of Ag-GNS/PEG composites improve the thermal conductivity of the composite due to Ag nanoparticles and GNS-functioned synergistic effect (from 0.212 Wm-<sup>1</sup> K-<sup>1</sup> to 0.414 Wm-<sup>1</sup> K-1 ), and at the same time, the photothermal conversion efficiency is high (η = 88.7% to 92.0%). Graphene oxide (GO) is a typical layered carbon material with high electrical conductivity, high thermal conductivity, large specific surface area, and high surface activity.[27,28](#page-12-0) GO has high effective light absorption in a wide range to achieve energy conversion and storage.[29](#page-12-0) Moreover, compared with other fillers, GO can interact with isocyanate (– NCO) in PU because of its –CO, –COOH, and –OH groups on the surface.[30](#page-12-0) Researchers have found that GO can be combined with solvent-based polyurethane (soft PU).[31](#page-12-0) In case of GO and water-based polyurethane composite PCMs,[32](#page-12-0) the photothermal properties and thermal stability would be improved after adding GO at a certain extent. Cai et al[33](#page-12-0) first synthesized a linear PU with –NCO group-terminated, mixed with GO solution with good dispersibility and reacted, and finally obtained GO/PU composites. The addition of 4 wt% GO can increase the decomposition temperature approach to 50C. This is due to the covalent interface formed between GO and PU molecular chains. Zhou et al[34](#page-12-0) synthesized crosslinked reduced graphene oxide/ polyurethane solid–solid phase change material (RGO/PUSSPCM) by a simple solvothermal treatment using PEG served as soft segment and hexamethylene diisocyanate biuret (HDIB) served as crosslinking agent. Different from the direct physical mixing method, the reduction of HDIB and GO realized the physical and chemical crosslinking, so that the samples have good stereotypes, and the phase transition enthalpy is increased by 46.7%. Because GO is reduced in situ, the photothermal conversion efficiency can reach 78.7%. Tang et al[35](#page-12-0) reported polyethylene glycol/graphene oxide aerogel (PEG/GA) composite PCMs by vacuum impregnation. The PEG/GA composite PCMs have high storage density, shape stability, and more effective photothermal energy conversion. Li et al[36](#page-12-0) chose GO with photothermal conversion characteristics as the photothermal conversion material and prepared GO/PEG composite PCMs by ultrasonic-assisted physical blending. The results show that the photothermal conversion efficiency of GO/PEG composite PCMs was as high as 87.3%. This is mainly due to the effective light absorption characteristics of GO in a wide wavelength range. But the enthalpy values of GO/PEG composites dropped from 135.81 J/g to 84.27 J/g after adding GO. Because the hydrogen bond interaction between GO and PEG molecules would limit the extension of PEG molecules. This results in incomplete crystallization of PEG and the decrease of latent heat of phase change. Therefore, in order to obtain a composite material with higher photothermal efficiency, more specific gravity light-absorbing fillers are needed, but the energy storage capacity would decrease because the interaction between additive and matrix may affect the enthalpy value of composite material. Bao et al[37](#page-12-0) showed that the phase change enthalpy of PU synthesized with 4,4<sup>0</sup> diphenylmethane diissyanate (MDI) and PEG increases with the increase of soft segment content. This mainly shows that the content of the soft segment has a certain influence on the enthalpy of phase change in PU. However, only PU with less than 60 wt% PEG content remained in solid state above phase change temperatures and the phase change enthalpy is only 65.95 J/g. Zheng et al[38](#page-12-0) successfully prepared isocyanated graphene oxide nanosheets/waterborne polyurethane (NCO-GO/WPU). The composites show significant improvements in performance of tensile strength, initial thermal degradation and hydrophobicity, and its initial degradation temperature increased from 249C to 288C. These enhancements in thermal stability were mainly attributed to the two-dimensional structure of NCO-GO and the good interfacial adhesion between NCO-GO and WPU. Kong et al[39](#page-12-0) obtained MDI-GO by covalently grafting of GO and MDI, and then added it into PU to prepare MDI-GO/PU composites. The results show that the thermal conductivity of the composites is greatly improved for the interaction of GO and MDI. The thermal conductivity of MDI-GO/PU composites is 51.7% higher than that of PU. As mentioned above, doping GO into PEG would improve the light absorption and shape stability of the composites. At the same time, the thermal stability of the composites could be improved for the interaction of GO with NCO in MDI, and the grafting modification of GO with MDI would improve the thermal conductivity of the composites.

In this work, in order to obtain composite PCMs with the strong light absorption and heat storage capacity at the same time, a novel GO-doped PU (which is prepared by MDI and PEG) composites solid–solid PCMs were prepared by in-situ polymerization (Figure 1). The thermal storage properties, stability, and photothermal properties of PU/GO composites were investigated in a relatively systematic way. The obtained PU/GO composites have high thermal conductivity, superior shape stability, high storage density (150.7 J/g) and outstanding photothermal efficiency (95.3%), which are higher than that (78.7% to 92%) of other PEG-based photo-thermal conversion PCMs. This novel photothermal storage composite PCMs can bring new application prospects for the storage and utilization of solar energy.

# 2 | EXPERIMENTAL

# 2.1 | Materials

Flake graphites (NFG, Particle size:300 mesh), potassium permanganate (KMnO4), and polyethylene glycol (PEG, M<sup>n</sup> = 10 000) were all purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. Concentrated sulfuric acid (H2SO4), hydrochloric acid (HCl), concentrated nitric acid (HNO3), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), and N, N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) were all obtained from Beijing Chemical Works, Co. Ltd. 4,4<sup>0</sup> -diphenylmethane diissyanate (MDI) was from Shanghai Aladdin Bio-Chem Technology Co. Ltd. And 1,4-butanediol (BDO) was supplied from Shanghai Medical Chemical Reagent Co. Inc.

# 2.2 | Synthesis of PU/GO composites

GO was first prepared from flake graphite according to modified Hummers' method.[40](#page-12-0) The preparation of PU/GO composites were carried out in two steps (Figure [2](#page-3-0)). Firstly, different masses of MDI, PEG, and DMF were mixed and stirred at 70C for 1 h to prepare an NCO-terminated prepolymer. Then, GO dispersion was added to the DMF mixture for further reaction for 2 h. In the second step, a stoichiometric amount of the chain extender BDO was slowly put into the solution under continuous stirring and the mixture was maintained at 80C for 2 h. Finally, the samples were vacuumdried in a vacuum drying oven at 80C for 48 h. PU/GO composite PCMs was obtained after vacuum drying. The PCMs with different PEG content (without GO) were fabricated and labeled as PEG, PU1, PU2, and PU3. And the obtained samples of PU/GO with the same GO, PU content, and different PEG content were labeled as PU1GO1, PU2GO1, and PU3GO1, respectively. In addition, the samples of PU/GO composite PCMs with the same PEG content (same as PU2) and different GO content were labeled as PU2GO1, PU2GO2, and PU2GO3, respectively. The basic recipes were listed in Table 1.

# 2.3 | Characterization

The morphology and structure of the samples were determined by scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi S-4800) and x-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker D8-Advance). The chemical structure of the samples was evaluated by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR, Brugg Tensor27). Thermal properties were studied by differential scanning calorimeter (DSC, PerkinElmer, at a heating and cooling rate of 10C/min under 25 mL/min N2 flow). Heating process and cooling process of samples were conducted on an Agilent-34970A temperature recorder with thermocouple (Agilent Technolo gies [China] Co., Ltd.) and carried out on plate heating. The optical property of the samples was recorded by ultraviolet–visible–near infrared spectrophotometer (UV– Vis–NIR, Cary 5000) at room temperature. In photothermal conversion test, xenon arc lamps (Zolix Co., Ltd.) were applied to simulated sunlight and the NOVAII optical power meter (OPHIR, Israel) recorded light intensity. The photothermal performance evaluation process was performed under simulated solar illumination at a controlled intensity of 130 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> .

These results indicate that the thermal energy storage capacity of PU/GO composites can be greatly improved by reasonably modulating the content of GO and soft segments.

#### 3.3.2 | Thermal stability of PU/GO

The thermal stability of PCMs can prevent leakage and encapsulation problems during the phase transition process, so it is critical in practical application. Figure 7 shows a photograph of samples heated on a heating plate at different times. Pure PEG gradually melts and flows when the heating time reaches 5 min. This indicates that when the sample is heated, the soft segments of the crystalline part begin to melt into irregular amorphous segments and tend to move freely. PU1 starts to melt while PU2 remains its form in 5 min. This is mainly because the hard segments can hold soft segments and limit their free movement in a certain region by the action of chemical bonds and physical restriction. Hence, during the melting process, the system appears as a macroscopic solid state and more hard segments content will lead to less leakage. All PU/GO composites maintain their original shapes in 15 min. This is mainly due to the addition of MDI and GO restricting the fluidity of PEG. PU2GO1 has no leakage while PU1GO1 melts and infiltrates the filter paper slightly. The result indicates that soft segment with 89 wt% content has better form-stability, which can be demonstrated by the SEM image (Figure [5D,E](#page-5-0)). Therefore, we chose PU2 as the main sample of the solid–solid PCMs for further research.

#### 3.4 | Photothermal energy conversion

The PU/GO composites can achieve photothermal energy conversion and thermal energy storage owing to the light-absorbing property of GO and the latent energy storage of PEG. As shown in the UV–vis absorption spectra (Figure [12A)](#page-9-0), the addition of low content of GO would greatly improve the light absorption capacity of PU2GO2 in a wide wavelength range, which is important for effective light capture and photothermal conversion. Under simulated solar radiation, temperature of the samples increased after solar radiation. When the temperature reached their melting point, a phase change platform of PU/GO appeared, corresponding to the solid– liquid phase transition and the storage of solar energy. After turning off the light, the temperature of composites PCMs dropped sharply. When the temperature reached the crystallization point, another platform emerged. It shows that PU/GO starts to solidify and release energy. It can be seen from Figure [12B](#page-9-0) that the heating rate increases with the increase of PEG content in the PU(1-3) GO1 composites. However, the temperature of PU2GO1 rises from 30C to 70C in 975 s, which is 50s faster than PU1GO1 and 190 s faster than PU3GO1. This indicates that the best photothermal performance of PU(1-3)GO1 is achieved when the content of PEG is 89 wt%. As mentioned above, PU2GO1 with 89 wt% of PEG content is also the most suitable ratio for solid–solid phase transition performance. This is due to the thermal conductivity influence on heat transfer performance, and the heat transfer performance decreases with the increase of PEG content.[48](#page-12-0) Furthermore, the photothermal conversion of PU2/GO composites with different contents of GO (0.86, 1.72, and 3.33 wt%) is shown in Figure [12C.](#page-9-0) It can be found that the temperatures of the PU2/GO composites increase faster with increasing GO content. And the temperature plateau becomes shorter with increasing GO content. However, the heating rate of PU2GO2 is faster than that of PU2GO3, which is consistent with the previous results of thermal conductivity and phase change enthalpy. #### 4 | CONCLUSION

In this study, PU/GO composites with high molecule weight PEG as soft segment were synthesized by two steps. With the addition of MDI and GO, the thermal conductivity and photothermal efficiency of PU/GO composites were significantly increased, while the phase change enthalpy also improved due to the increase of soft segment content. Thus, the energy storage capability, stability, thermal reliability, and photothermal conversion efficiency of PU/GO composites solid–solid PCMs were all enhanced. The results show that the thermal conductivity of PU/GO is 53.4% to 188.4% higher than that of PEG. It took 3.8 times longer for PU/GO from 60C to 30C than PEG, indicating PU/GO has great thermoregulation performance. And the introduction of low content of GO also can help the PU/GO composite PCMs realize WANG ET AL. 22755

strong light absorption ability, which helps to achieve efficient photothermal conversion. The PU/GO composite doped with 89 wt% PEG and 1.72 wt% GO shows the highest heating rate and photothermal conversion efficiency is 95.3%. The 2D structure of NCO-GO can provide a new way for the solar energy storage and conversation.
