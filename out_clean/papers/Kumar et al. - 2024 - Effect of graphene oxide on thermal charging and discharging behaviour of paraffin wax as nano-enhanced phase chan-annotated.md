# **Efect of graphene oxide on thermal charging and discharging behaviour of parafn wax as nano‑enhanced phase change materials**

**Amit Kumar1 · Aayush Gupta1 · Kamal Sharma1 · Mahendra Singh1**

Received: 3 April 2024 / Accepted: 30 May 2024 / Published online: 14 June 2024 © The Author(s), under exclusive licence to The Materials Research Society 2024

## **Abstract**

This study investigates the impact of adding graphene oxide (GO) to parafn wax as a phase change material (PCM) on its thermal behaviour. GO addition, confrmed visually, enhances thermal conductivity by 60%. Structural features of both GO and the prepared PCM are reported. The investigation considers parameters such as GO concentration and heat transfer fuid fow rate. Increasing GO concentration (0.25–1.00 vol%) decreases PCM's thermal charging and discharging due to improved heat transfer. Moreover, increasing fow rate (0.375–1.5 L/min) reduces charging time. Higher fow rates correspond to improved efciency in the thermal energy storage system. These fndings suggest GO is an efective additive for enhancing parafn wax as a PCM, benefcial for energy storage, thermal management, and building insulation applications. The study implies practical implications for utilizing GO-infused PCM in diverse thermal management systems, highlighting its potential in enhancing thermal properties and overall system efciency.

## **Introduction**

The world is witnessing a signifcant surge in energy demand driven by ongoing industrial expansion and a growing global population. Unfortunately, heavy reliance on fossil fuels as primary energy source has resulted in severe consequences such as climate change, global warming, and ecological damage [[1\]](#page-5-0). As a result, it has become imperative to address these issues by transitioning towards clean and environmentally friendly energy solutions. Renewable energy like geothermal, solar, and wind power have garnered immense attention as sustainable alternatives to fossil fuels, ofering the advantage of being eco-friendly and their abundance. Among these options, solar energy stands out due to the exceeding immediate demand. However, seasonal variations of solar energy may restrict its utility. Nevertheless, solar energy remains a highly promising alternative energy source that can maintain the demand and supply circularity [[1\]](#page-5-0). Therefore, to develop and design an efcient solar thermal energy storage system (TESS) is today's need-of-the-hour. TESS is energy storage device in which a working substance absorbs or emits energy with respect to temperature variations. Majorly, the popular utility of TESS deals with the temperature-controlled atmosphere in various industries such as drug transportation, swimming pools and centrally air-conditioned buildings. Among all of the systems, latent heat storage systems (LHSS) works on principle of energy storage in form latent heat of working substance during isothermal phase change (solid ⇌ liquid) [\[2\]](#page-5-1). LHSS has two major advantages over other systems; (i) high storage density, and (ii) smooth temperature variations [\[3](#page-5-2)]. In this view, phase change materials (PCMs) were found to be excellent candidates as working substance to store solar thermal energy such as parafn wax (organic), salt hydrates (inorganic) and fatty acids (organic) [\[4](#page-5-3)]. PCM-based LHSSs have advantages of high energy storage density, chemical stability, constant heat release temperature, and low degree of supercooling [\[5\]](#page-5-4). However, low thermal conductivity (TC), easy phase leakage and poor light absorption of traditional organic PCMs hinder its performance by restricting their scalability and commercialization [\[6](#page-5-5)]. Therefore, the chemistry of traditional parafn wax has been modifed by mixing other components for enhanced thermal performance of TESSs [\[7](#page-5-6)]. Zeng et al. [[8\]](#page-5-7) recorded a 356.25% increment in TC of organic PCM (i.e. 1-tetradeconal) by addition of Ag nanowires (62.73 wt%). Li et al. Till date, several papers have been published focusing on thermal CH, DCH and overall efciency of TESS using variety of materials enhancing the characteristics of parafn wax. Still, the incorporation of graphene oxide (GO) into parafn wax is a state of art in this regard. The present study describes systematic data acquisition and analysis of selfdesigned horizontal cylinder solar reactor for thermal energy storage applications of parafn wax.

## **Experimental section**

Figure [1a](#page-2-0) represents schematic fow diagram of undertaken thermal studies. In the experimental setup, cylinder (5.5 kg capacity) flled with prepared PCM-based composites was installed in the circuit and two separate heat cycles were monitored which are (i) thermal charging and (ii) thermal discharging as shown in Fig. [1a](#page-2-0). In thermal charging cycle, HTF (water in present case) was flled in source tank with the help of centrifugal pump and heated through electric heaters till 85 °C was achieved. Thereafter, the heated HTF was allowed to fow through heat exchanger assembly in which the heat was transferred to PCM-based composite samples and stored again in storage tank (1). The cycle was remained open for charging till the complete melting of parafn wax occurred which was confrmed by achieving ~ 65 °C at 'outlet', shown in Fig. [1](#page-2-0)a. While, discharging cycle involves the fow of cold HTF fuid from diferent storage tanks (2) to extract heat energy from PCM-based composite and the fluid will be discarded as shown in Fig. [1](#page-2-0)a. The completion of thermal discharging cycle was determined by achieving 35 °C of heat exchanger assembly at 'outlet' as shown in Fig. [1a](#page-2-0). Rest of the assumption, materials and discussion on structural features of GO is given in supplementary information.

## **Results and discussion**

PCM with higher TC can signifcantly reduce time required for both thermal CH and DCH processes. The efectiveness of any TESS relies heavily on efciency of these processes. In this study, the exceptional TC properties of GO have been harnessed to enhance TC of parafn wax, aiming to improve overall thermal performance of system.

Considering the huge increment in TC as a result of GO addition in parafn wax, the prepared composite samples would be suitable for thermal charging and discharging application in TESSs. Therefore, a separate thermal charging and discharging study has been conducted. Figure [2](#page-3-0)b shows a detailed graphical representation of temperature profles and region considered for energy calculation. The process parameter such as GO concentration and fow rate of working fuid plays critical role in executing efcient heat transfer between storage material (prepared samples) and HTF (water). Figure [2](#page-3-0)a represents the efect of GO concentration (0.25–1.00 vol%) at diferent HTF fow rates (0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min) on thermal charging and discharging of prepared samples.

### **Thermal charging**

Thermal charging of parafn wax-based TESS was confrmed by achieving a constant temperature of 65 °C at outlet Fig. [1](#page-2-0)a. It was quite obvious to record the decreasing overall charging time as an efect of increasing GO concentration. Interestingly, it was observed that overall charging time was reduced upto 0.75 vol% GO (A3) while further increasement in GO concentration (A4) increased the overall charging time. The main reason behind the reduced charging time could be the faster energy transfer from HTF to storage material through additional thermal conductive network within parafn wax [[18\]](#page-5-17). For A3, about 50.0%, 42.85%, and 43.75% reduction in overall charging time as compared to pure parafn wax at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rate, respectively.

Beyond 0.75 vol% (A4; 1.00 vol%), the overall charging time was found to be increased by 33.33%, 25%, and 22.22% at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rates with respect to 0.75 vol% GO (A3). This behaviour can be attributed to the reduced intermolecular free mobility of GO particles at 1.0 vol% concentration of GO (sample A4) [\[19](#page-5-18)]. The decreased charging time at high fow rate (1.5 L/min) would indicate efcient energy transfer through HTF to working storage material resulting, 25.0% (for A0) and 33.3% (for A3) decrement in overall time (to reach 65 °C) as compared to 0.375 L/min, respectively. ### **Thermal discharging**

Once charging process was completed, discharging of TESS was started so that discharging behaviour of energy storage material, PCM in the present study, can be monitored through heat transfer from PCM to HTF. During discharging process, HTF absorbed the heat from PCM cylinder along with solidifcation (due to cooling) of parafn wax or waxbased composite as shown in Fig. [2](#page-3-0)b. In the present study, normal water (at room temperature) has been used as HTF fuid, circulated through PCM cylinder until temperature of PCM cylinder was reduced to ambient room temperature, i.e., 35 °C (completion of discharging). Efect of GO concentration (A0-A4) on overall discharging time was also studied at diferent fow rates as shown in Fig. [2a](#page-3-0). As an efect of incorporation of GO in parafn wax, it was clearly observed that discharging time was reduced with increasing GO concentration. The observed thermal behaviour is completely associated to higher TC of GO as compared to parafn wax resulting to enhanced heat transfer. As the GO concentration was increased, the rate of heat transfer has also been increased, resulting to a rapid discharging process. For A3, about 40.0%, 34.78%, and 31.57% reduction in overall discharging time was recorded as compared to pure parafn wax at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rate, respectively. Beyond 0.75 vol% (A4; 1.00 vol%), the overall discharging time was found to be increased by 6.67%, 6.94%, and 7.69% at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rates with respect to 0.75 vol% GO (A3). At 0.375 L/min, the convective heat transfer allows for faster heat dissipation from the composite, storage material, to the HTF. This accelerates the cooling process, leading to even more rapid reduction in temperature at faster fow rates (0.75 and 1.5 L/min). So, considering specifc application requirements, determining the optimal flow rate for discharging of cylinder requires system design and composite characteristics. An overall charging-discharging performance variation of A3 and A4 is shown in Fig. [2](#page-3-0)c and d, respectively.

### **Thermal efciency**

Figure [2](#page-3-0)e represents the overall thermal efciency (provided in supplementary information) of GO-reinforced parafn wax as energy storage medium. Here, it has been observed that reduced CH and DCH time, as an efect of GO addition, afected the overall efciency of the system. At a particular fow rate of HTF, addition of GO (A3; 0.75 vol%) enhanced overall thermal efciency of PCM-based TESS by~14% as compared to parafn wax-based TESS. Importantly, sample A4 exhibited reduced thermal efciency of TESS as compared to A3 which is similar to charging and discharging behaviour. While, the overall efciency has been reduced with increase in fow rate of HTF irrespective of composition of energy storage medium. In order to achieve maximum efciency of the TESS, the concentration of GO should be 0.75 vol% in parafn wax for energy storage medium and the HTF fow rate can be taken as low as possible, i.e., 0.375 L/min. ## **Conclusion**

Experimental study was successfully conducted on TESS utilizing GO-based parafn wax composite as energy storage medium. To analyse the efect of fow rate of HTF on CH and DCH, various experiments were performed with diferent fow rates of 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min. Following observations have been obtained from this study:

- i. As an efect of GO concentration, 0.75 vol% (A3) exhibited the optimized charging among all the sample with 60 min as overall charging time. Beyond 0.75 vol%, TESS took more time for complete charging. Similarly, A3 exhibited fastest discharging of TESS which is due to reduced intermolecular free mobility of GO particles.
- ii. HTF fow rate greatly afects heat transfer. Higher fow rate leads to shorter charging and discharging times, while lower fow rates result in longer times.

- iii. For A3, ~ 50.0%, 42.85%, and 43.75% reduction in overall charging time has been recorded as compared to pure parafn wax (A0) at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rate, respectively.
- iv. Till 0.75 vol% GO (A3), the overall discharging time was found to be decreased with increasing fow rate. 40.0%, 34.78%, and 31.57% reduction in overall discharging time was recorded as compared to pure paraffn wax (A0) at 0.375, 0.75 and 1.5 L/min fow rate, respectively. While, A4 exhibited longer charging and discharging time as compared to A3, i.e., 33.3% and 6.67%, respectively (at 0.375 L/min).
- v. The highest overall efficiency of 46.3% has been obtained when 0.75 vol% GO-reinforced parafn wax was used as energy storage medium at 0.375 L/min fow rate.
- vi. Experimental fndings indicate that the optimal concentration of GO is 0.75%, enhancing the TC of paraffn wax by 60%.
- vii. To optimize the storage system cost, it is advisable to incorporate 0.75% GO into parafn wax, resulting in reduced melting time and elevated PCM temperatures.
